Anger at God's Messenger

Now Hear This!Lesson 7Day 2 of 7

In brief

Two men carry a cluster of grapes on a pole into the camp at Kadesh. The land is as good as God said. But the scouts have also seen fortified walls and the descendants of Anak, and soon the people want a new leader to take them back to Egypt. When Joshua and Caleb plead with them, the crowd talks of stoning them. The crowd's anger at God's messengers grew out of fear of what He was asking, and God counted their contempt as contempt for Him.

Yesterday

Yesterday Paul told believers in Corinth that Israel's wilderness stories were written down as warnings for them. They were written ahead, for people who would come later. Today goes to one of those stories, at the edge of the promised land. What turns a frightened crowd against God's messengers?

Historical Context

Kadesh Sat on the Border

Kadesh lay in the desert south of Canaan, near the line that would mark the southern edge of the promised land (Numbers 34:3-4). From Mount Horeb it was an eleven-day walk (Deuteronomy 1:2). Israel set out from Sinai in its second year out of Egypt (Numbers 10:11-12). From Kadesh, twelve scouts explored the land for forty days. They reached Hebron and brought back a cluster of grapes so heavy that two men carried it on a pole (Numbers 13:21-25).

So the people stood at the doorstep, and what they decided there would shape the next forty years. After they refused, God told them they would bear their guilt for forty years, one year for each day the scouts had spent in the land (Numbers 14:33-34). An eleven-day walk became forty years.

Into the Word

Moses was the prophet God had sent to lead this people. Deuteronomy later remembers him as the one the LORD knew face to face, whose signs and wonders no later prophet in Israel matched (Deuteronomy 34:10-12). Now his scouts walk back into camp with fruit in their arms and fear in their report. What happens when God's messengers say go, and the people are afraid?

That night the camp wept, and the weeping turned into complaint against Moses and Aaron (Numbers 14:1-3). Then the complaint became a plan.

Street Level

The Shouting Was Meant for Her

Picture a mother who has finally found steady work, four states away, after a hard year of falling behind on rent. She sends her two oldest kids ahead with their uncle to see the little house she has rented. They come home with videos. There is a creek behind the yard and a bedroom for every child. They also tell the truth. The middle school is enormous, the town is strange, and they do not know a single person there.

The younger two hear only the second half, and their fear turns hot fast. You just want to go, the eleven-year-old yells at her sister. She says she will move in with Grandma and keep her old school. Her little brother throws his shoe at the older two. They keep pleading anyway. Yes, the school is big. But Mom has never moved us anywhere she did not take care of us. Then their mother steps in from the hallway. She has heard every word. Do not take this out on them, she says. They told you the truth. If you do not believe I will take care of you, say it to me.

Kadesh worked the same way. Israel had good reason to be afraid, and Caleb never said otherwise. But the anger the crowd threw at two scouts was really a verdict on the God whose promise they carried, the God who had kept every promise so far. Not everyone who says go speaks for God. Those two were right because the promise was His. The shouting was meant for Him.

Today’s World

The Warning About Spray Cans Was Ridiculed

In the early 1970s, a California chemist named Sherwood Rowland and a younger colleague, Mario Molina, found something alarming. Chemicals called CFCs were widely used in air conditioners and spray cans. The two worked out that these chemicals could drift high into the sky intact. There, strong sunlight would break them apart. The chlorine set loose would then destroy ozone over and over without being used up. And the ozone layer helps shield people from the sun's harmful UV rays.

The idea that chemicals from a spray can could harm the whole planet was ridiculed. Rowland himself was fiercely challenged for years, most of all by people in the chemical industry. Then scientists found a hole in the ozone over Antarctica. Nations agreed in the Montreal Protocol to phase out the chemicals. In 2023 a UN-backed panel reported that nearly 99 percent of the banned chemicals had been phased out. If current policies hold, it said, the ozone over most of the world should be back to 1980 levels by about 2040.

Ridicule did not make the warning true, and plenty of ridiculed ideas have been wrong. The evidence is what settled this case. But Rowland's story shows how someone who brings a costly warning can become the target. The same pull was at work at Kadesh.

Christ at the center

Centuries later, Hebrews retells this night. It asks who heard God and still rebelled, and it points to those whose bodies fell in the wilderness. They could not enter because of unbelief (Hebrews 3:16-19). Then it turns to its own readers with the warning of Psalm 95:7-8, not to harden their hearts when they hear God's voice today (Hebrews 3:15). Moses was faithful in God's house as a servant. Jesus is faithful over God's house as the Son (Hebrews 3:5-6). The rest that frightened generation refused is still offered, and those who believe enter it (Hebrews 4:1-3). At Kadesh the people wanted a leader who would take them back. Hebrews calls Jesus our forerunner, the One who has gone ahead of us (Hebrews 6:19-20). The invitation still has a date on it. Today.

Application

Take two minutes. Name one thing you sense God asking of you that you have been avoiding because it frightens you. Tell Him honestly what you are afraid of. Then read Numbers 14:8-9 slowly and ask Him for the trust to take one small step.

Discussion Question

The people's fear made sense, but it turned into anger at the men who told the truth. Why does fear so easily turn on the messenger? When have you seen that happen (at home, at work, or in your own heart)?

Closing Prayer

Lord, we confess that our fear has sometimes turned into anger at people who told us the truth, and at You. Thank You for messengers like Caleb and Joshua, who pleaded with a frightened crowd. Give us a different spirit, one that sees the walls and still trusts You. Where You are asking us to go forward, keep us from looking for a way back to Egypt. Today, when we hear Your voice, keep our hearts soft. In Jesus' name, Amen.
