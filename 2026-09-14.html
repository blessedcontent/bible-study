Boasting in the Lord

Dealing With False TeachersLesson 12Day 3 of 7

In brief

The newcomers in Corinth wrote their own reviews. They rated each other, compared each other, and the church was impressed, until Paul pointed out that a ruler which only measures you against you cannot measure anything. He pulls out a surveyor's word instead, the measuring rod God used to mark out his field, and shrinks his bragging rights to a single line from Jeremiah. Boast in the Lord. An old man's finish line proves the point. The only approval worth having comes from the One holding the tape.

Yesterday

Yesterday Paul leaned into the letter with "I, Paul" and answered the charge of cowardice with meekness, a war horse's strength under a light rein. His weapons were truth rather than volume, and his authority existed to build up. Today the newcomers' favorite weapon comes out. It is a tape measure, and they are always the ones holding it.

Historical Context

Rome Measured the Colony Before Anyone Moved In

When Rome refounded Corinth in 44 BC, the first men on the site were not builders. They were surveyors. Working with a sighting instrument called a groma, they laid a grid of straight lines across the land and carved it into square plots, then handed the plots to the freed slaves and veterans Caesar had sent to fill the empty city. Each boundary was marked with a stone, and the stones were treated as sacred. Rome had a god for boundaries, Terminus, and moving a neighbor's marker in the night was a crime people believed carried a curse. Every settler's family knew exactly which field was theirs, who had assigned it, and where their ground stopped and someone else's began.

That is the picture under Paul's odd language in 2 Corinthians 10:12-18 about limits, fields, and areas of influence. He talks like a man holding a survey map. God, not Paul, drew the lines, and God assigned him a plot that reached all the way to Corinth. The newcomers were working a field they had not been given and bragging about a crop they had not planted. Corinth had rules about bragging, too. Self-praise was expected in public life so long as you followed the conventions and did not bore or insult your audience, and a generation after Paul the writer Plutarch even published advice on praising yourself without offending anyone. Paul knew the rules. Today he breaks the game itself by asking who owns the measuring stick.

Into the Word

Picture the newcomers reading their own reviews. They arrived in Corinth with letters praising them, they rated one another highly, and when they wanted to prove they were the real apostles they pointed at each other. The church was impressed. Paul answers them in 2 Corinthians 10:12-18, and his opening line is almost funny. When people measure themselves by themselves, they show their ignorance, because a ruler that only measures you against you cannot tell you anything. So today's question is a simple one. What are you actually allowed to brag about, and who is holding the tape?

So what does a life look like when God's rod measures it from start to finish? The start was a calling, an apostle by the will of God (1 Corinthians 1:1). Here is the finish, written from a Roman prison near the end.

Street Level

Every Clock in the House Agreed

The kitchen clock was set from the microwave. The microwave was set from the clock on the stove, and the stove got its time years ago from the alarm clock in the bedroom, which somebody once set by the kitchen clock. Nobody remembers doing any of this. What everybody remembers is that the clocks always matched. You could walk from room to room and check, and the house agreed with itself to the minute, and that agreement felt like information. It felt like knowing what time it was. Then one morning you walk into the interview, or the dentist's office, or your kid's recital, and the room is already ten minutes into something.

The confusing part is that you did not feel late. You checked. Every clock you own said you were fine, and every clock you own was wrong by the same eleven minutes, and had been for years. That is the trouble with a standard that only refers to itself. Three clocks agreeing tell you nothing about the time. They only tell you about each other. The fix is not a better argument among the clocks. It is the one signal that comes from outside the house, the time your phone pulls off a tower, traced back to a clock nobody in the family gets a vote on. Set to that, and you stop checking the other rooms. You do not need to.

Paul's newcomers were a house full of clocks set to each other, and the church mistook their agreement for accuracy. The standard he reached for came from outside the room. God marked out the field, and God does the commending, and that verdict does not answer to any comparison. A person set to it can walk past every other clock in the house without a glance. Comparison ends the moment the real time arrives.

Today’s World

Everyone Got an A and Nobody Knew Where They Stood

In October 2025 Harvard's dean of undergraduate education delivered a report on the college's own grading, and the numbers were blunt. More than 60 percent of the grades it handed out were A's. Two decades earlier the figure was about 25 percent. The median grade had been an A since 2016. The report concluded that grading at the college "no longer performs its primary functions." A grade that says excellent about nearly everyone says nothing about anyone. A student could compare herself with her classmates all day and still not learn how good her work was. The whole grading scale had drifted upward together until it stopped telling strong work from ordinary work.

Most of the faculty accepted the diagnosis. That fall the share of A's fell from 60.2 percent to 53.4 percent. Results announced May 20, 2026, showed that the faculty had voted 458 to 201 to limit A's in each course to 20 percent of the students plus four more, starting in fall 2027. The point of the cap was to make the grade carry information again. Amanda Claybaugh, the dean who wrote the report, gave the reason in one sentence. Students are owed grades "that send clear signals, that give them a good sense of their strengths and weaknesses."

The report is about transcripts, not souls, and a faculty vote is not the judgment seat of God. What it shows is the pattern Paul named in Corinth. When a group sets its standard by looking at itself, the standard quietly stops telling the truth, and the people inside it are the last to find out. A measurement carries real information only when it comes from outside the room, and Paul says the one that counts comes from the Lord.

Christ at the center

Jesus did not build His identity on commending Himself. Ask Him for His credentials and He points to the Father, who commends Him out loud at His baptism and again on the mountain. He said plainly that if He glorified Himself, His glory would mean nothing (John 8:54). He stayed inside the field the Father assigned, a small country of fishing villages and hill towns, and His last report from inside it was that He had finished the work He was given (John 17:4). That is the pattern Paul is copying, and it is also the gift Paul is living on. At the cross the Son who deserved every boast was measured as a criminal, so that people with nothing to brag about could be commended by God as His own. That is why the only boast left is Him. Our righteousness is His, our approval is His, and the tape God holds over us reads the same for every one of us. Christ.

Application

Today, catch yourself in one comparison as it happens (a scroll through someone's highlight reel, a coworker's win, another family's kid, another church's numbers). The moment you notice it, stop and say out loud one thing God has actually assigned to you in this season and one thing about Him you know to be true. Then go work your own field for ten minutes, a call, a chore, a page of Scripture, something inside your lines. Comparison starves when you stop feeding it your attention.

Discussion Question

Where do you most often catch yourself measuring your walk with God against someone else's (their Bible knowledge, their ministry, their family, how they pray), and what does that comparison do to you when you win it? If the only approval that counts is the Lord's commendation, what would you stop doing this week, and what would you start?

Closing Prayer

Father, we confess that we know exactly where we rank. We know who prays better than we do, who serves more, who seems closer to You, and we know who we quietly outscore, and we have felt better about ourselves because of it. Forgive us for grading on a curve You never drew. Take the ruler out of our hands. Show us the field You have measured out for us, the people and the work that are actually ours, and make us content to labor there without looking over the fence. Teach us to boast in one thing only, that we know You, and let every boast turn into talk about Your loving devotion, Your justice, and Your righteousness. When we finish, let it be said of us that we kept the faith in the lane You assigned, and let the commendation we wanted most be Yours. In Jesus' name, Amen.
