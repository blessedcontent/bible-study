Harmony With the Bible

Testing the ProphetsLesson 9Day 3 of 7

In brief

A king of Moab moves a hired seer from hilltop to hilltop, hoping for a different word from God, and gets the same answer every time. Centuries later, frightened people in Judah urge one another to ask mediums to consult the dead. Isaiah sends them back to God's instruction instead. God does not lie or change His word, so a true messenger will agree with what He has already said, and a voice that contradicts it has no dawn in it.

Yesterday

Yesterday, Daniel lay face down by the Tigris until a hand lifted him to listen. Moses warned that even a sign that comes true can lead the wrong way. Today asks what every true voice will agree with.

Historical Context

A Frightened Nation Asked the Dead

In Isaiah's day, two neighboring kings marched against Jerusalem, and the hearts of King Ahaz and his people shook like trees in the wind (Isaiah 7:1-2). Ahaz sent to the king of Assyria for rescue (2 Kings 16:7). Everyone was talking about conspiracies, and God told Isaiah not to fear what the people feared (Isaiah 8:12).

Some went further. They urged consulting mediums and spiritists who whispered and muttered, as if the dead could advise the living (Isaiah 8:19). Israel's law had forbidden this long before (Deuteronomy 18:10-11), and King Saul, on his last desperate night, had gone looking for a medium anyway (1 Samuel 28:7). Fear can make people hungry for a new voice. Isaiah's answer was not a better medium. It was the word God had already given.

Into the Word

Can enough money, pressure, or fear get God to say something different? A king of Moab thought so. Balak paid a famous seer named Balaam to curse Israel, then led him from one hilltop to another and built seven altars at each stop, hoping a new view would bring a new word (Numbers 23:13-14). The second time, Balaam told him why it would not work.

Isaiah faced the opposite problem. Balak wanted God to give a new word. Isaiah's neighbors went looking for words from the dead.

Isaiah's test still works because the God behind that testimony has not changed. James calls Him the Father of the heavenly lights, with whom there is no change or shifting shadow (James 1:17), and Paul says everything written in the past was written for our instruction (Romans 15:4). Near the end of his life, Peter set the prophets and the dawn side by side.

Street Level

He Told Every New Hire the Same Thing

A nineteen-year-old at a hardware store rings up a riding mower at the price of a push mower, and the customer is gone before anyone notices. Eleven hundred dollars. The assistant manager finds him by the paint counter and says it flatly. The owner is going to let you go. Finish your shift. The kid's face goes hot. He walks to the break room and starts untying his apron.

Then he stops. On his first morning, the owner, a man in his seventies who still unlocks the front door himself, had walked him down every aisle and said one thing twice. Everybody here makes a mistake that costs me money. Come tell me first, and you still work here. The kid has heard him say it to every new hire since. He has never heard him say anything else. So he ties the apron back on, walks to the office, and tells him. The owner groans about the mower and sends him back out. Aisle nine needs restocking.

The voice by the paint counter was not wrong because the news was hard. The owner could give hard news when he had to. It was wrong because it contradicted what he had said from the first day and never taken back. When Isaiah's neighbors were afraid, they went looking for new voices, and Isaiah pointed them to the instruction God had already given (Isaiah 8:19-20). God does not say one thing on the first day and another when we are frightened (Numbers 23:19). The kid nearly walked out over a sentence the owner never said. The sentence that kept him was the oldest one in the store.

Christ at the center

The first promise of rescue in the Bible was spoken in a garden, to a serpent. A descendant of the woman would crush its head and be struck in the process (Genesis 3:15). Later prophets never replaced that promise. They kept adding to it, a Child born to rule (Isaiah 9:6) and a ruler from Bethlehem (Micah 5:2). Paul says that at just the right time God sent His Son, born of a woman (Galatians 4:4). Hebrews says Jesus shared our flesh and blood so that by His death He could break the power of the devil (Hebrews 2:14). Each new word built on the old one, and none of them took it back. The morning Isaiah promised has a name.

Application

Choose one message about God you have heard recently, from a sermon, a song, or a post. Hold it next to James 1:17 and ask whether it describes the same steady, generous God. If it does not fit, set it down.

Discussion Question

Why do frightened people so often reach for new voices instead of what God has already said? When you are anxious, what do you check first (the news, friends, your own feelings, or Scripture), and what might change if Scripture came first?

Closing Prayer

Father, You do not lie, and You do not drift. Thank You that Your word is steady even when we are not. Forgive us for the times fear sent us looking for other voices. When we are anxious, bring us back to what You have already said. Keep Your word in our hands like a lamp until the full day comes. Let every voice we trust agree with You, and let the morning star rise in our hearts. In Jesus' name, Amen.
