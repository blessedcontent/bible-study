Further Thought & Discussion

Sin in the ChurchLesson 4Day 7 of 7

Week in Review

This week Paul walked into the church's ugliest mess, a tolerated scandal, lawsuits between believers, and freedom twisted into license, and answered all of it with one staggering claim, that a believer's body is a temple the Holy Spirit actually lives in, bought at a price.

  • ☩Sabbath: Sin in the Church. In a city where the temples never closed, God claimed a temple with no marble at all, His people, purchased at the cross and indwelt by His Spirit.
  • ☩Sunday: Dissonance Between Faith and Practice. A man had his father's wife, a sin even pagans condemned, and the church was proud of its tolerance. Paul heard yeast working through the dough.
  • ☩Monday: Dealing With Scandals. The hard verbs, remove, hand over, do not even eat together, turned out to aim at rescue, and one letter later a repentant man is forgiven and welcomed home.
  • ☩Tuesday: Protecting the Church's Identity. Believers were suing each other before pagan judges, and Paul asked what the spectacle was teaching the city, then reminded them who they were. Washed.
  • ☩Wednesday: Antidote Against Sexual Immorality. Against the slogan that the body is just appetite hardware, Paul stacked three facts, washed, joined to Christ, and indwelt, and named God's will in one word, sanctification.
  • ☩Thursday: Marriage and Singleness. Flee what would defile the temple, the way Joseph left his coat and ran, and honor the gift God built in Eden for desire to live in.

Historical Context

The City Kept Seeping Through the Church Door

Look back and the whole week had one setting, a church with its city inside it. Corinth's temples never closed, and its appetites never slept. Its courts ran on honor, its dinner tables doubled as contracts, its philosophers called the body a tomb, and its comedians had turned the city's name into a punchline for sleeping around. Drop a young congregation into that world and nothing stays outside the walls on its own. The scandal, the shrugging tolerance, the lawsuits, the brothel logic, the marriage panic, every problem Paul tackled this week was the city seeping through the doorway.

That is what makes his answer so striking. Paul never proposes moving the church out of Corinth. No compound, no retreat, no thicker walls. Instead he keeps pointing at what moved in when the gospel arrived, the Spirit of God, taking up residence in bodies and in a people. The church's defense against the city was never distance. It was occupancy. A temple does not stay clean because the neighborhood improved. It stays clean because Someone lives there. The question the week leaves behind is not how bad the culture is. It is who is at home in us.

What This Week Clarifies

An uncomfortable week leaves three knots worth untying on the way out. The church that refused to judge a scandal was quick to hire pagan judges. The kindest thing Paul did for a fallen man sounded like the cruelest thing. And chapters spent condemning misused bodies end by defending the body's honor. All three loosen under the same truth, that the house belongs to Someone. Untie them slowly before Sabbath.

God Keeps the Street. The Church Keeps the House.

The week caught the church failing in two directions at once. A scandal inside the family drew no judgment at all, only pride in how accepting everyone had become. A business dispute between brothers drew plenty of judgment, hired downtown from a pagan bench. And the modern reflex only tightens the knot, since most of us were raised to believe that judging is the one thing a loving church must never do.

The Hard Verbs Were Building a Road Home

Monday's instructions still sound severe two thousand years later. Remove. Hand over. Do not even eat together. Set them next to everything Scripture says about grace and they feel like sentences that wandered in from a colder book, a church commanded to put a failing man out the very door it exists to hold open.

The Gift Always Had an Address

After days of scandal, brothel logic, and appetite slogans, a reader could conclude the week's real enemy is the body itself, and part of Corinth drew exactly that conclusion, deciding holiness meant renouncing sex entirely, even inside marriage. Thursday refused the trade. The same chapters that shout flee turn around and defend desire's home with full reverence.

Still Speaking

The Appetite Underneath Everything

Strip the week's crises down and you keep finding the same machinery, appetite trained until it rules. Paul saw the pattern in Israel's oldest cautionary tale. The people sat down to eat and drink before the golden calf (1 Corinthians 10:7), a festival that began with feasting (Exodus 32:1-6) and slid straight into immorality (1 Corinthians 10:8). He saw it again in Corinth, where his vice lists keep filing idolatry, drunkenness, greed, and sexual sin side by side (1 Corinthians 5:10-11, 1 Corinthians 6:9-10). These are not unrelated failures. They are one river with many mouths, the self on the throne, feeding. Even our most respectable indulgences belong to the same family, because a will trained to obey the appetite in small things has already rehearsed its surrender in the large ones. Holiness and self-indulgence cannot share a house, and a body kept perpetually overfed, overstimulated, and jittery dulls the very faculties the Spirit works through.

But the week's closing word is not try harder, and it is not eat less. It is occupancy. The self does not leave because we evict it. It leaves as Someone else moves in, the way darkness is not swept out of a room but displaced by dawn. When a person is emptied of self, every rival god carried out of the soul, the vacuum does not stay a vacuum. The Spirit of Christ fills it, and the faith that results works by love and washes the whole inner life clean. God is not whittling us down into joyless discipline. He is raising us up to His own high, pure standard, and His Spirit keeps patiently striving with us because, left to themselves, our natural cravings carry the seeds of death. The temple language was never about restriction. It was about glory. And the Owner ends the week asking the question He has been asking all along, gently. May I have the whole house?

Christ at the Center of the Week

Trace the week and every road bends toward the same Person. On Sabbath He was the price, the purchase that made a people His temple. On Sunday He was the Passover Lamb, already sacrificed, the whole reason the house gets swept. On Monday He was the One handed over to darkness, so that everyone handed over to consequences would have a home worth returning to. On Tuesday He was the wronged One who never sued, whose unjust verdict lets us be called washed, sanctified, justified. On Wednesday He was the Word made flesh, the true temple, raised bodily with the scars still on. And on Thursday He was the Bridegroom who did not flee the cross, faithful to an unfaithful bride at the cost of His life. A week that began with scandal ends with ownership. We are not our own. Thank God.

Weekend Practice

Before Sabbath ends, do two things. First, close the loop on the week's intake audit. Look at what you cut or swapped, and tell God honestly what changed, in your appetite, your patience, your prayers. Second, act like a temple-keeper. Choose one space you control, a room, a feed, a habit, a shelf, and clean it as worship rather than self-improvement. Then set a table. Invite someone who needs to know the family's door is open, the lonely friend, the person easing back toward faith. A clean temple with an open door is the whole week in one afternoon.

Closing Prayer for the Week

Father, this was an uncomfortable week, and we thank You for not sparing us. You walked us through scandal and discipline, courtrooms and appetites, and everywhere we looked we kept finding the same truth waiting, that we are not our own. Thank You for the price. Thank You that the Lamb has already been sacrificed, that the welcome-home is already planned, that Your Spirit has already moved in. Now finish what the week started. Sweep out whatever we have been calling harmless. Cool the fights we have been calling justice. Retrain the appetites we have been calling personality. Keep our marriages honored, our single ones cherished, our tables honest and open. And when the city seeps through our doors again next week, let it find the house occupied. In Jesus' name, Amen.
