A Portrait of Jesus

A Portrait of LoveLesson 7Day 5 of 7

In brief

Two days inside the love chapter and most of us are quietly discouraged. Patient, kind, no envy, no ledger. We have failed every line by Tuesday lunch. Here the argument turns and lifts a weight nobody should have been carrying. Paul was not describing us at our theoretical best. He was describing Someone he had met on a road outside Damascus. Every verb in the portrait has already been lived, in sandals, all the way to a cross. The love chapter is not first a mirror of what we must become. It is a portrait of who Jesus already is.

Yesterday

Yesterday you tore up a ledger entry, one old wrong retired from the books for good. Fifteen verbs are now on the table, seven things love does, eight things it refuses, and an honest reader is left with a problem. Nobody lives this. Measure any of us against the portrait and the frustration is almost instant, which is exactly where the passage meets us. When Paul wrote these lines, he likely had a particular face in mind. The portrait was drawn from a living model, and today we finally get to meet Him in it.

Historical Context

The Portrait Came Before the Gospels

A believer in Corinth in AD 55 had never seen Jesus' face, and neither had anyone she could ask. There was no picture of Him anywhere in the world. Her city was crowded with the faces of its lords, Caesar on every coin in her purse, statues of benefactors along every street, because in the Roman world a ruler made sure you knew exactly what he looked like. The church's Lord had left no statue, no coin, no painted panel. New converts asked the obvious question, what is He like, and the church had no image to hand them.

Here is what makes today's passage remarkable. When Paul wrote this letter, not one of the four Gospels was in circulation yet. Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John were still years from being written down. Which means the love chapter is one of the earliest portraits of Jesus ever put on paper, drawn before the Gospel writers picked up their pens, and Paul painted it without a single physical detail. No height, no face, no voice. Just verbs. Patient. Kind. Keeping no record. Bearing all things. The first portrait of Jesus the church ever owned was a portrait of how He loves, and it turned out to be the only likeness that matters.

Into the Word

Read the fifteen verbs (1 Corinthians 13:4-7) and then open the Gospels, and something uncanny happens. The abstract portrait starts moving. It picks up a towel. It stops for blind men. It sets its face toward Jerusalem. Scripture insists this is no coincidence, because the love Paul described has always had a face, and today's passages trace the resemblance feature by feature until the identification is unmistakable.

The resemblance keeps building. Love rejoices in the truth, and Jesus was no stranger to joy. He wanted His own joy planted in His friends, remaining in them, filling them up (John 15:9-11), and He prayed His joy into them on the night of His arrest (John 17:12-14). Love bears and endures all things, and no one has ever endured like Him, the cross itself, the hostility of sinners, for the joy set before Him (Hebrews 12:2, 3), obedient all the way down to death on a cross (Philippians 2:8). Feature by feature, the face is coming into focus.

Street Level

Every Uniform Matched and You Still Found Her

Every seat in the bleachers is doing the same impossible thing. Twenty-two kids in identical uniforms, same colors, same numbers blurred by distance, too far away for faces, and every parent up there has already found their own. Nobody is squinting at jerseys. You spot the run. The way she checks over her shoulder before the ball comes anywhere near her. The bounce your son has had since he was three. From two hundred feet, faces are useless, and you never needed one. It works everywhere. You would know your mother from behind, in a crowd, by nothing but her walk. You can hear your best friend's laugh through a wall. And long after a grandfather is gone, when the photographs have started to feel flat and strange, what stays vivid is how he folded the newspaper and patted his pockets at the door. We think faces are how we know people. Faces are only how we meet them. We recognize the people we love by how they move.

That is why the love chapter can identify Jesus without a single physical detail. Paul gives no height, no face, no sound of His voice, just how Love moves. Patient with the slow. Kind before being asked. Keeping no record. Staying when it costs everything. Read those movements slowly and a specific Person steps out of the sentences, unmistakable, the way your kid comes across a field. A portrait made of behavior turns out to be the most identifying likeness there is. And it means you can still recognize Him now, moving through an ordinary week, patient where no one else would be. You know the walk.

Today’s World

The Bank That Reads People in the Future Tense

Nobody expects stubborn optimism about human beings from a bank. Banks are in the file business, credit histories, risk scores, past behavior read as forecast. Which is what makes the hiring numbers at JPMorganChase so surprising. The company struck the criminal-history checkbox from its job applications, and in 2022 alone it hired more than 4,600 people with records in their past, roughly one in ten of its new hires in the United States in recent years. It helped found the Second Chance Business Coalition, more than 45 major employers making the same bet, and it now runs clinics that help people clear old records entirely. The bet is paying. When old records are expunged, workers' earnings climb about twenty percent within two years.

Now set that beside the road to Damascus. Ananias answered the Lord with a file, every entry accurate, the harm, the arrests, the authorizations, and Jesus replied in a different tense. This man is My chosen instrument. Love believes all things, Paul wrote, and here is what that verb looks like wearing a lanyard. Not gullibility. Not pretending the record is blank. It is the wager that a person's worst chapter is not their last one, made by somebody with the power to open a door. A file tells you where a person has been. It carries no data on what grace can still do. Heaven has been hiring on that basis all along, staffing its whole mission with people whose records should have disqualified them, starting with the man who wrote the love chapter. Every believer is a second-chance hire. The least we can do is read each other the way we were read.

Christ at the center

Today the whole page has been Christ connection, so let the connection turn personal. The portrait is not hanging in a museum. It is looking at you. The patience Paul exhibited to the world is patience Christ is currently extending to you, on the days you repeat the sin you swore off, in the prayers you keep forgetting to pray. The kindness believers have tasted is kindness He has already shown you, in mercies you have stopped noticing because they arrive so reliably. And the stubborn faith that saw an apostle inside a persecutor is the same faith He has in what His grace can still do with you. We spend so much energy asking whether we love God enough. Today the gaze runs the other way. Before the fifteen verbs are ever our assignment, they are His disposition toward us. Look at the portrait long enough and you will find it is not a standard glaring down at you. It is a Face, turned toward you, to the end.

Application

Tonight, read the towel scene in John 13:1-17 in one sitting. Watch Jesus the way you would watch someone you were trying to describe to a friend, noting each love-verb as it happens. Pick the single move that strikes you most, the interruption He allows, the feet He does not skip, the betrayer He still serves. Then copy that exact move on someone in your house or circle tomorrow before noon.

Discussion Question

"Which feature of Jesus' love is hardest for you to believe is aimed at you personally (His patience after your repeated failure, His kindness when you expect disappointment, His confidence in who you could still become)? And who in your life needs you to show them that exact feature this week, maybe because nobody ever has?"

Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, we came into this week thinking the love chapter was about us, and today we found You in every line. You are the patience we have been failing at. You are the kindness we ration. You loved Your own to the end, the very ones who would scatter before morning, and You have loved us the same way through every one of our worst days. Thank You for sitting for this portrait in real time, with real nails. Forgive us for reading it as a burden when it was always an introduction. Heal what discouragement these verses have stirred in us, and replace it with wonder. And do in us what only You can do. Believe us into becoming what You already see, the way You believed a persecutor into an apostle. We want to look like this portrait, because we want to look like You. In Jesus' name, Amen.
