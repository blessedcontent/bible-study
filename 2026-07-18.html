Sin in the Church

Sin in the ChurchLesson 4Day 1 of 7

In brief

A report crosses the sea to Paul. A man in the Corinthian church is sleeping with his father's wife, a scandal so ugly even pagan Corinth would not excuse it, and the church's response is to feel proud of how accepting it is. This week Paul refuses to look away. He walks straight into sexual scandal, church discipline, lawsuits between believers, and hard questions about marriage, and underneath all of it he plants one enormous claim. Your body is where the Holy Spirit lives. You are not your own. You were bought at a price.

Last week

Last week ended with the world watching. Jesus staked His credibility on our visible unity, and Paul spent four chapters pulling a church out of its fan clubs and back to the one name crucified for it (1 Corinthians 1-4). Now the same watching city gets an eyeful of something else. A scandal is being tolerated inside God's family that even Corinth's pagans would call shameful, and the church is not grieving. It is proud. Paul saw pride at the root of the factions, and he finds the same root here. So the cross he has been preaching turns from the church's divisions to the church's compromises (1 Corinthians 5-7), and the surgery goes deeper.

Historical Context

The Temples in Corinth Never Closed

A sailor stepping off a grain ship could walk the few miles up into Corinth and never once lose sight of a god. The seven heavy columns of Apollo's temple, already ancient when Rome rebuilt the city, stood over the forum like the bones of an older world. There were shrines to Asklepios the healer, altars to Poseidon who ruled the seas that made the city rich, and street-corner spirits whose names nobody bothered to carve. High overhead, on the gray rock of Acrocorinth, sat a sanctuary of Aphrodite, the goddess of desire. Worship here was not a weekly appointment. It was the water the city swam in, woven into the butcher's stall, the dinner party, the business deal, and the bedroom. Greek comedians had long since turned the city's name into a verb for sleeping around, and the new Roman city, flush with harbor money, did little to retire the joke.

Now notice what was missing from that skyline. The God of Israel owned no marble in Corinth. No columns, no statue, no smoking altar. And when He finally claimed a temple in the city, He did it in the strangest way imaginable. He moved into people. A tentmaker, a former synagogue ruler, a dockworker washed clean of an old life, each one now a dwelling where the Spirit of the living God actually lived. That is the claim this whole week stands on. The city's appetites had followed the converts through the church door, a scandal here, a lawsuit there, old habits waiting in the dark. Paul answers every one of them with the same staggering fact. The holiest ground in Corinth was no longer up on the hill. It got up every morning, put on sandals, and went to work.

  • ☩Monday: Dealing With Scandals. The hard instructions, remove, hand over, do not even eat together, turn out to be aimed at one goal, bringing the man home.

  • ☩Thursday: Marriage and Singleness. Fleeing immorality is half the answer. Honoring God's design for marriage and desire is the other half.

Christ at the center

Hold the phrase "bought at a price" up to the light and the cross shows through it. Paul never names the figure, because Corinth already knew how people were purchased, in silver, on a block, in public. God purchased differently. The price was His own Son, and the receipt is written in blood. Watch how every hard word this week stands on that purchase. The scandal matters because the man involved was bought, and so were the people shrugging at him. The discipline aims at rescue because the Owner wants His temple back, not burned down. Even the strangest sentence of the week, that Christ our Passover lamb has been sacrificed (1 Corinthians 5:7), turns cleaning out sin into an act of worship. Here is the question to carry through the week. If Jesus paid that much for you, what does He now want with what He bought? By Friday the question will no longer be how much is forbidden. It will be who you belong to. And the answer is Him.

Application

Before Sunday, run an honest intake audit. Our minds soak up whatever we hand them, and most of what we hand them arrives by habit, the feed we scroll at night, the shows running in the background, the voices we let talk to us for hours. Pick the one steady input you suspect has been quietly resetting your sense of normal. Cut it for the week, or swap it for something that feeds the temple instead of the appetite. Write down what you chose, so by Friday you can tell what changed.

Opening Question for the Week

"Where has the culture around you quietly reset your sense of normal, so that something that would have grieved you ten years ago barely registers today (a kind of entertainment, a way of talking about people, a habit you defend as harmless)? And if your body really is the place where God's Spirit lives, what would honoring Him with it look like this week?"

Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, this week is going to be uncomfortable, and we ask You to keep us in the room. Paul is about to talk about sex, scandal, lawsuits, and discipline, the subjects we usually lower our voices around, and every one of them touches something we would rather keep private. So before we begin, we hand You the key. Remind us all week that we are not our own, that You paid for us at the cross, and that Your Spirit has actually moved in. Where we have grown comfortable with what grieves You, make us tender again. Where we have confused tolerance with love, teach us the difference. And where something in us needs to be cleaned out, do it gently, but do it. We would rather be Your temple than our own landlords. In Jesus' name, Amen.
