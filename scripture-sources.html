Scripture sources

The Bible text on this site comes from the Berean Standard Bible, a modern English translation often abbreviated BSB, unless an exception is clearly identified beside a quotation. This page explains where our copy of that text comes from, how we keep it faithful, and what rights cover it. The heart of it is simple. Displayed Scripture passages reproduce wording from an exact, verified copy of the publisher's official text without alteration, and we credit the source voluntarily.

Where our copy comes from

Each finished study page carries its Scripture text directly. During production, the project draws displayed BSB passages from a pinned local copy of the publisher's official files, keeping the wording fixed and verifiable.

That copy was acquired on August 15, 2026 from the publisher's official downloads page, in the edition the publisher designates as Current, updated to 3rd Printing. Two official formats are retained together. The rendered plain text fixes the exact wording and punctuation readers see. The structured USX archive preserves details the plain text cannot carry, such as exactly where the words of Jesus begin and end.

How the copy is verified

The archive, the rendered text, and all 66 extracted book files are recorded by byte count and by SHA-256 checksum, a standard digital fingerprint that reveals any change to the retained source.

When the copy was acquired, the two official formats were also checked against each other across all 31,086 non-empty verses. The wording agreed everywhere, and every words-of-Jesus boundary aligned cleanly. That was a check of the whole dataset at acquisition. Individual studies then quote from this verified copy, and automated checks compare each study's primary displayed Scripture blocks with it before publication. That comparison covers wording, punctuation, verse boundaries, and words-of-Jesus markup.

How quotations are displayed

Displayed wording and punctuation follow the publisher's rendered plain text.

Some passages show the words of Jesus in red. Those boundaries come exclusively from the words-of-Jesus markers in the publisher's structured text. Red lettering is optional and can be turned off, and no study depends on the color to be understood.

The translators' footnotes are not currently shown on study pages, though their presence is preserved in our internal source records. Poetry is currently displayed as flowing prose. Its wording is preserved exactly, but the poetic line breaks are not yet reproduced on the page.

If a future study intentionally quotes a different translation, that exception will be identified beside the quotation itself as well as in the page's source information.

Bible app links

Each displayed Scripture block offers an “Open ↗” link. These links open the Berean Standard Bible on YouVersion, where it is version 3034. The quotation printed on the study page remains the locally retained text regardless of a reader's Bible-app preferences.

Rights and reuse

The Berean Bible terms state that the text was dedicated to the public domain effective April 30, 2023, and that all uses are freely permitted. Attribution is appreciated but not required, so the credit this project gives is voluntary.

Official terms · Official downloads

That public-domain status belongs to the Bible text itself. It is separate from this project's own CC0 1.0 Universal dedication, which covers eligible original study material and is described on the About Our Approach page.
