The Creator Speaks

The Creator SpeaksLesson 1Day 1 of 7

In brief

Before there was anyone to hear it, there was a Voice. Light came because God said so, and the first thing He did with the man and woman He made was talk to them. Then came a lie, a hiding place in the trees, and a guarded gate. This week follows what God did next. He called out. He made a promise. He sent messengers, and at last He sent His Son. We hid from the conversation. God has never walked away from it.

Last week

Last week ended at a front door. A church that had fought with Paul for years was sent out under a blessing of grace, love, and fellowship. This new quarter begins with a simpler question. How does a God we cannot see say anything to us at all?

Historical Context

Kings Once Read Sheep Livers to Hear From Heaven

Picture a Babylonian king stopped at a fork in the road, an army behind him and no idea which way to march. The prophet Ezekiel describes what he does next. He shakes arrows, consults his idols, and examines the liver of a sacrificed animal (Ezekiel 21:21). That last detail was ordinary practice. Across the ancient Near East, trained diviners studied sheep livers for marks they believed the gods had left there. Archaeologists have dug up clay models of livers, some covered in notes, in Mesopotamia and in Canaanite cities such as Hazor. Men spent whole careers on the craft. For many of Israel's neighbors, heaven did not volunteer much. It had to be asked, paid, and decoded.

Now open the Scriptures Israel carried through that world. Their God speaks before anyone asks Him anything. He speaks light into the dark. He speaks to the first man and woman before they have said a word to Him. When they ruin everything, He is the one who comes looking. Centuries later, the letter to the Hebrews was written to worn-out believers who were in danger of drifting away (Hebrews 2:1). Its first sentence is about a God who talks. Nobody had to read a liver. He had been speaking the whole time.

This Week’s Memory Text

Hebrews has no hello and no signature at the top. It starts in the middle of a thought, as if the writer could not wait to say it.

This Week’s Road Map

  • ☩Sunday: Communication in Eden. The first thing God does with the people made in His image is bless them and talk to them.
  • ☩Monday: Hiding From God. A serpent twists what God said, and two people who had never feared His footsteps duck behind the trees.
  • ☩Tuesday: God Seeks Humanity. God walks into the garden with a question, and it turns out He has been asking it ever since.
  • ☩Wednesday: Expelled From the Garden. The way back is guarded, yet Adam and Eve leave wearing clothes God made and carrying a promise.
  • ☩Thursday: God's Spokespeople. Outside the garden God keeps talking, through creation, prophets, Scripture, and finally a Son who shows us the Father.
  • ☩Friday: Further Thought. The week gathers around one voice and asks what it means that God spoke first and keeps speaking.

Christ at the center

Hebrews says God's greatest word is not a sentence. It is a Son. That changes what kind of week this is. If God had capped everything with a list of rules, we would study the rules. If He had ended on a warning, we would brace for it. Instead He spoke through a Person who ate with sinners, touched lepers, and called tired people to come to Him. So as you read about Adam crouched behind a tree, hold on to one question. What does the God he was hiding from actually sound like? Adam guessed, and he guessed from fear. We do not have to guess. The One through whom the universe was made has walked into it, and He took children in His arms (Mark 10:13-16). By Friday, see whether the footsteps in the garden sound different to you.

Application

Sometime today, find two quiet minutes. Read Hebrews 1:1-2 slowly. Then tell God honestly about the last time you sensed He was speaking to you. If the answer is never, or not for a long time, say that. It is a true place to begin the week.

Opening Question for the Week

When you picture God trying to get your attention, what does His voice sound like to you (a judge, a parent, a stranger, a friend)? Where did that picture come from, and how does it compare with a God whose clearest word was His Son?

Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, You spoke first. Before we asked, before we knew what to ask, You were already talking to us. We admit we do not always want to hear You. Some of us are tired, some of us are hiding, and some of us are not sure You still speak at all. Open these old stories to us this week. Let us hear Your footsteps in the garden without running. Show us the promise You tucked inside the worst day. And teach us to know the voice of Your Son when He calls. In Jesus' name, Amen.
