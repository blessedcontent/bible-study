A Clearer Picture of God

To Know GodLesson 2Day 2 of 7

In brief

The Bible doesn't just tell us about God; it pulls back a curtain, giving us the clearest view of His character available this side of eternity.

  • ☩ Scripture reveals a God who is holy, loving, just, merciful, wise, gracious, forgiving, purposeful, sovereign over death, and eternal
  • ☩ Satan's strategy from Eden onward hasn't been to deny God's existence but to distort humanity's understanding of who God actually is
  • ☩ The serpent's first move was not "God isn't real" but "God can't be trusted," which is a far more dangerous lie because it's harder to detect
  • ☩ Getting to know God's character through His Word is how the fog of misrepresentation begins to lift

Yesterday

Yesterday's Sabbath opener framed the question driving this entire week: who is the God we're trying to know? We saw that Jesus Himself defined eternal life as knowing God personally, not just knowing about Him (John 17:3). Today we pick up the next thread: if knowing God is that important, what has gone wrong with humanity's picture of Him, and where do we go to get it corrected?

Historical Context

The Original Character Assassination

The serpent in Genesis 3 does not open with a theological argument. He opens with a question. That detail matters more than most readers realize. In the rhetoric of the ancient world, the most devastating way to undermine authority was not a frontal assault but a carefully planted seed of doubt. You didn't accuse a king of evil; you wondered aloud whether his motives were really as noble as they appeared. The question format invited the listener to do the accusing themselves, which made the conclusion feel like their own idea rather than someone else's manipulation.

This is exactly the serpent's technique. "Did God really say...?" is not a request for information. It's a reframing device. By slightly exaggerating God's command ("You must not eat from any tree?"), the serpent paints God as restrictive, withholding, paranoid. Eve corrects the exaggeration, but the frame has already shifted. The conversation is no longer about God's generosity in giving them every other tree in the garden. It's about the one restriction. And from that foothold, the serpent builds his case: God is hiding something. God is protecting His own interests. God is not who you think He is.

What's striking is that this strategy has never changed. Every distortion of God's character since Eden has followed the same pattern: take one true thing (God did set a boundary), twist the motive behind it (He must be keeping something good from you), and let the lie grow from there.

Into the Word

Today's anchor passage is Genesis 3:1-5, the first recorded attempt to distort God's character. But the study also surveys the Bible's own answer to that distortion: a sweeping catalog of God's attributes, from His omnipotence and justice to His mercy, grace, and eternal faithfulness. We'll walk through the anchor passage first, then let several key attribute passages speak as correctives to the lies that started in Eden.

The Bible's answer to this distortion is not a single proof text but a whole-Bible portrait. From Genesis to Revelation, God reveals His character through action, invitation, and self-description. Here are three passages that directly counter the serpent's three lies.

Street Level

The Cover Song You Heard First

There is a disorienting experience that most music listeners have had at least once. You hear a song, you love it, you play it a hundred times, and it becomes yours. Then one day someone tells you it's a cover. You look up the original artist, press play, and something strange happens: the original sounds wrong. The timing is different, the production is rawer, the vocal doesn't hit the notes the way you expect. You know intellectually that this is the authentic version, the one that came first, but your ears have been trained by the copy. The cover rewired your expectations so thoroughly that the real thing feels like the imitation.

That is what the serpent accomplished in Eden, and it is still working. Most people's picture of God is a cover version. It was shaped by a harsh Sunday school teacher, a parent who wielded God like a threat, a movie that portrayed Him as a cosmic scorekeeper, or just the general cultural assumption that religion is about rules and punishment. By the time they open the Bible and encounter the original, the God who pursues, who grieves, who promises restoration and means it, He sounds wrong. He doesn't match the version they learned first. The serpent doesn't need you to reject God outright. He just needs you to hear the cover before you hear the original. Today's study is about pressing play on the primary source and letting it retrain your ears.

Today’s World

False Stories Still Travel Faster

In 2025, a study from Georgia State University's Robinson College of Business confirmed what researchers at MIT had documented years earlier: false information spreads significantly faster than the truth on social media, reaching people roughly six times more quickly. The Georgia State team found that emotional cues, not accuracy, are what drive people to share content. We don't pass along what's true; we pass along what feels true, what triggers surprise, outrage, or fear.

The parallel to Genesis 3 is almost too precise. The serpent's strategy was never about evidence. It was about emotional framing. "Did God really say?" appeals to suspicion. "You will not surely die" appeals to the desire for reassurance. "God knows you'll become like Him" appeals to ambition. Each move targets a feeling, not a fact. And the result is exactly what the modern research predicts: the distorted version of God's character spreads faster, sticks harder, and is more emotionally satisfying than the truth, at least in the short term.

This is why the Bible's invitation to know God through Scripture is not optional. Without it, we will inevitably build our picture of God from whatever emotional narratives happen to be circulating around us. The only corrective is going back to the source.

Christ at the center

Every lie the serpent told in Eden finds its answer in Jesus. The serpent said God is withholding good things from you. Jesus came and "did not consider equality with God as something to be exploited" but "emptied himself" for us (Philippians 2:6-7). The serpent said God can't be trusted. Jesus proved trustworthy to the point of death on a cross. The serpent said death isn't real. Jesus walked into death, stayed three days, and walked out the other side holding the keys.

If you want to know what God is really like, look at Jesus. He healed the people religion had written off. He touched the ones everyone else avoided. He wept at a friend's grave even though He knew He was about to raise him. He forgave the soldiers who drove the nails. This is the clearer picture of God that the Bible offers: not a distant power broker protecting His interests, but a Father who gave everything to bring His children home.

Application

Pick one attribute of God from today's study: His kindness, His plans for your well-being, His power over death, or any other. Write it on a sticky note and place it where you'll see it throughout the day. Each time you notice it, ask yourself honestly: "Do I really believe this about God, or have I been believing a distorted version?" Let the Bible's picture replace whatever secondhand version you've been carrying.

Discussion Question

"If you were honest about it, what is one thing you've believed about God's character that you now suspect might be more influenced by culture, past experiences, or other people's opinions than by what Scripture actually says?"

Closing Prayer

Father, I confess that some of my picture of You has been built from things that aren't true. I've listened to secondhand descriptions, absorbed cultural assumptions, and sometimes believed the enemy's version of who You are more than Yours. Today I ask You to begin correcting that picture. Open my eyes to see You as You've revealed Yourself in Scripture: kind, patient, purposeful, sovereign, eternal, and relentlessly good. Help me recognize the serpent's voice when I hear it, even when it comes dressed up as my own thoughts. I want the clearest, truest picture of You that this life can offer. In Jesus' name, Amen.
