The Incarnation of Christ

Testing the ProphetsLesson 9Day 4 of 7

In brief

Suppose a gifted speaker in one of John's churches says the Christ was too holy to be born and too high to bleed. It sounds reverent. People nod. John hands his readers a test for voices like that, one question about Jesus. A voice from God confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh, because everything He does for us depends on God's Son becoming truly human.

Yesterday

Yesterday, a hired seer admitted that God does not lie, and Isaiah sent a frightened people back to God's word. A true voice agrees with what God has already said. Today John names the one thing a voice must say about Jesus.

Historical Context

Some Said He Only Seemed to Suffer

In the early 100s, a church leader named Ignatius was led under guard from Antioch to Rome to die. On the way he wrote to several churches in the Roman province of Asia, where early Christian writers said John had spent his last years. More than once Ignatius warned these churches about teachers who said Jesus only seemed to suffer. He truly suffered, Ignatius insisted, and He truly rose.

Many scholars think John was answering an early form of the same idea. His letters describe people who had left the fellowship and denied that Jesus is the Christ (1 John 2:22), and deceivers who would not confess Jesus Christ as coming in the flesh (2 John 1:7). John would not let his churches soften the point. The Son of God did not borrow the look of a man. He became one.

Into the Word

If you could ask a voice that claims to speak for God only one question, what would it be? John chose his, and it is not about how the messenger sounds or what he can do.

John states his test in three verses. Other passages are not part of that test, but read beside it they show how much hangs on it. Everything Jesus did for us, He did in a real body.

Street Level

The Cold Got Him Too

The mayor has the plaque in one hand and the microphone in the other. Three weeks ago, the man standing beside him went into a frozen pond after a stranger's son, and the town has packed the fire hall to thank him. The mayor wants to honor him well. He tells the room this man is made of something different, that the water was near freezing and he went in like it was July. I don't think the cold even touched him, he says. People laugh and clap.

In the front row, the boy he pulled out, twelve years old, does not clap. He remembers the rest. The man crawled out flat on the ice until it broke under him too. He pushed the boy up onto the edge with both hands and held on in the water until the firefighters' rope reached them. In the ambulance the man's lips were blue, and he shook so hard the blanket kept sliding off his shoulders. After the ceremony the boy finds the mayor by the coffee urn. It got him too, he says. That's how I know he came in after me.

John told his churches that a voice which would not confess that Jesus Christ had come in the flesh was not from God (1 John 4:2-3). A voice like that can sound reverent, honoring a Savior too holy to be touched by our weakness. But a rescuer the cold never touched never came into the pond. Jesus is far more than a brave stranger. He is the Son of God. The honor John defended was not that the cold could not reach Him. It was that He came all the way in.

Christ at the center

The One who came in the flesh also came out of the grave in it. He had told Martha, I am the resurrection and the life (John 11:25), and on the third day His own tomb was empty. He promised His friends that He would come back and welcome them into His presence, so that they would be where He is (John 14:1-3). Standing before the same high priests who had handed Jesus over, Peter said salvation exists in no one else (Acts 4:12). A voice that gets the incarnation wrong cannot lead anyone to that name. God did not send us a message about rescue. He sent His Son in our flesh to carry it out.

Application

Read 1 John 4:2 slowly. Then thank God for one thing Jesus did in a real human body, perhaps His weariness by a well, His tears at a grave, or His wounds on the cross.

Discussion Question

Why does it matter that Jesus truly became human and did not just seem to? Which part of His human life means the most to you right now (His temptations, His tears, His death, or His risen body)?

Closing Prayer

Father, You did not keep Your distance from us. You sent Your Son, and He took on flesh, was tempted as we are, and carried our sin in His own body. Thank You. Keep us from any voice that would make Him less than He is, less than God or less than truly human. When we are weak, remind us that He understands, and give us courage to hold firmly to what we profess. Teach us to love the way You loved us, by giving. In Jesus' name, Amen.
