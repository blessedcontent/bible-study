General and Special Revelation

Revelation and InspirationLesson 5Day 2 of 7

In brief

On a clear night far from town lights, the sky is crowded with stars. A psalm of David says it is speaking. Paul says its message is plain enough to leave every person without excuse. But the same world grows weeds, and the stars cannot explain them. Creation tells everyone that God is real and powerful. Only His word, and at last His Son, tell us what went wrong and how He will put it right.

Yesterday

Yesterday John heard a voice on Patmos and was told to write down what he saw. That is God's word given to one man and carried to many. Today steps back to a wider question. What has God said to people who never hear a prophet at all?

Historical Context

They Bowed to the Sun With Their Backs to the Temple

In a vision, Ezekiel was taken into the inner court of the temple in Jerusalem. There, between the porch and the altar, he saw about twenty-five men with their backs to the LORD's house, bowing toward the east to the rising sun (Ezekiel 8:16). That is how strong the pull was. Egypt worshiped the sun as the god Ra. In Mesopotamia people praised the sun god Shamash as a judge whose light saw everything. Moses had warned Israel that looking up at the sun, moon, and stars could draw them away to bow down to them (Deuteronomy 4:19).

The psalm talks about the sun with real delight. It comes out like a bridegroom from his room and runs its course like a glad athlete (Psalm 19:4-6). But in the psalm, God pitched the sun's tent. The sun has a schedule, a path, and a job, and the job is to tell about Someone else. The same sky can lead a heart to worship its Maker, or to worship the sky.

Into the Word

David spent his early years outdoors with his father's sheep (1 Samuel 16:11), and the psalm we are about to read carries his name. It opens with a man looking up. Before anyone reads a page of Scripture or hears a prophet, there is already a message overhead, and it reaches every place on earth. The question is what that message says and what it leaves out.

Creation tells a harder story too. Genesis traces thorns, sweat, and death back to the first sin (Genesis 3:14-19). Look at the world long enough and you see good and evil growing side by side. Jesus told a story about a field like that.

Street Level

The Sculptor Knew What Was Missing

In a small-town park, a bronze boy kneels on one knee with his hand held out low, palm down, just above the grass. People love him. Anyone can see that the sculptor knew his work. The boy's shoelaces are untied, a cowlick sticks up in back, and his weight rests on that knee so naturally that little kids kneel beside him to copy it. Nobody walks by and decides the bronze poured itself.

But the hand bothers people. It reaches for nothing. Newcomers come up with reasons. Maybe the boy is reaching for something we cannot see. Maybe the money ran out before the work was done. One man decides the sculptor simply got it wrong. Then one newcomer stops guessing. She looks up the sculptor, now in his seventies, and calls him. There was a dog, he tells her. Last winter someone cut it loose in the night and sold it for scrap. He has known since the morning it happened. He still has the mold, and the dog goes back in the spring.

The statue never lied about him. Anyone could see his skill in it, the way anyone can see the Maker's power in the world. But bronze could not explain an empty hand, and some people blamed the sculptor for a gap he never made. In Jesus' story the servants did the wise thing with their question. They took it to the owner. The sculptor had not been careless. He knew exactly where the dog belonged.

Today’s World

Nobody Knows What It Was For

In the summer of 2023, volunteers from a local history and archaeology group were digging at a Roman site in Lincolnshire, England, when a small metal object came out of the ground whole. It was hollow and about three inches tall, with twelve flat sides. Each side had a hole in it, and a small round knob stood at every corner. Archaeologists call it a Roman dodecahedron. About 130 are known, and the first one found in Britain turned up in 1739.

Careful study has learned a lot. This one was cast from copper mixed with a large share of lead. It is one of the few ever found in a layer of soil that can be dated, and most of the others come from Roman Gaul, roughly modern France, with none from Italy. Their purpose is another matter. No surviving Roman text mentions them, and no mosaic or wall painting found so far shows one. Many uses have been proposed, from candle holders to fortune-telling tools, and none has been confirmed.

So the object can be measured, weighed, dated, and mapped. What it meant to the people who made it is still missing, because no word from them survives. Creation tells us a great deal about God in the same way. Paul says His eternal power has been clearly seen in what He made. What He intends for a world gone wrong is not something anyone can dig up.

Christ at the center

David's psalm says the heavens declare the glory of God. Paul says that same glory has come much closer. The God who first called light out of darkness has shone in our hearts, so that we can know His glory in the face of Jesus Christ (2 Corinthians 4:6). The sky shows that glory from a great distance. In Jesus it comes close enough to see in a face. And His face shows what no sky can. God shows His love for us in this, that Christ died for us while we were still sinners (Romans 5:8). Jesus said the Scriptures testify about Him (John 5:39), and that is why the written word matters so much. It brings us to Him. The stars show His power. The cross shows His love.

Application

Tonight, step outside or look out a window for two minutes. Thank God for what the sky tells you about Him. Then thank Him for one thing you know about Him only because of Jesus.

Discussion Question

The world shows God's power, and it also shows weeds, thorns, and graves. What question does the world raise for you that looking alone cannot answer? Where has God's word given you an answer the sky could not?

Closing Prayer

Maker of heaven and earth, thank You that the sky never stops telling of Your glory. Forgive us for the times we have admired what You made and ignored You, or looked at a broken world and decided You did not care. Thank You that You did not leave us guessing. You spoke through the prophets, and then You sent Your Son. Open our eyes to see Your glory in His face. And when we see the weeds, help us trust the One who knows where they came from and has set a harvest. In Jesus' name, Amen.
