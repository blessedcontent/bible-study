Dealing With False Teachers

Dealing With False TeachersLesson 12Day 1 of 7

In brief

A rock fortress towers over Corinth, and a small tentmaker with a reputation for weak speech writes that his weapons can bring down strongholds. The people mocking him have moved into his church, polished and confident, preaching a Jesus who fits Corinth better than the crucified One. Paul does not out-shout them. He opens four chapters that are gentle and fierce at the same time, and this week we walk through them to learn the difference between what looks strong and what is. A counterfeit falls to God's power, never to our volume.

Last week

Last week ended at a kitchen table, with the collection plate turned from an invoice into an heirloom and giving revealed as the family resemblance of a God who gives. Now the tone of Paul's letter changes. Someone has arrived in Corinth to challenge the man who planted the church, and the family recipe is about to be tampered with.

Historical Context

The Fortress Above the City Fell Before Dawn

Every morning in Corinth began under a rock. Acrocorinth rose nearly 1,900 feet above the gulf directly behind the city, a limestone mass with walls along its summit and a spring inside that never ran dry. Whoever held that height controlled the land bridge between northern and southern Greece, which is why one Macedonian king called it one of the "fetters of Greece." Armies looked up at it and made other plans. In 243 BC a general named Aratus took it anyway, not by assault but with a small party climbing a hidden path in the dark. When Rome flattened Corinth a century later, the rock lost its city. By Paul's day the summit still crowned the rebuilt colony, and a person walking to the market could see it from almost any street.

So when Paul told this church that God's weapons "demolish strongholds," nobody had to imagine one. They lived under one. He meant exactly that kind of walled height, a place built so that whatever hid inside it could not be reached. His strongholds were not stone, though. They were arguments, reputations, and a way of measuring leaders that some newcomers had carried into the church. Those visitors looked strong the way the rock looked strong. This week watches Paul walk toward that fortress with no siege engines at all, only the truth about Jesus and a willingness to look weak, and it watches what happens to the walls.

This Week’s Memory Text

One sentence from the middle of Paul's argument becomes the week's banner. He is answering people who say his letters roar and his presence squeaks, and instead of defending his personality he tells them what kind of war he is actually fighting, and with what.

This Week’s Road Map

  • ☩Sunday: Spiritual War. Paul answers the charge that he is all bark by naming the war he is actually in and the weapons God issued.
  • ☩Monday: Boasting in the Lord. Comparison only measures you against you, so Paul boasts inside the field God assigned and lets God do the commending.
  • ☩Tuesday: False Teachers Identified. The best counterfeit looks real, so Paul teaches the church to check the Jesus being preached, not the polish.
  • ☩Wednesday: Sufferings for the Sake of the Gospel. Paul plays the boasting game for one paragraph, wins it with a list of scars, then hands the trophy to grace.
  • ☩Thursday: Appeal to the Unrepentant. A third visit is coming with real authority, and the man bringing it prays he will never have to use it.
  • ☩Friday: Further Thought. The week's threads gather around one question, what strength looks like when it belongs to the crucified Jesus.

Christ at the center

Watch for a pattern this week. Every time Paul is pushed to prove himself, he points somewhere else. Pushed to boast, he boasts in the Lord. Pushed to show strength, he lists the times he was beaten. Pushed to prove that Christ speaks through him, he answers that Christ was crucified in weakness and lives by the power of God, and that he is glad to be weak the same way. The question the week keeps asking is not whether Paul was impressive. It is what Jesus is like, because the whole fight was over which Jesus Corinth would follow, the polished one the newcomers preached or the crucified One Paul had planted there. That is why counterfeits matter so much. A different Jesus is not a minor adjustment. It is a different God. By Friday we should be able to say what the real One looks like when He fights for His people, and it will not look like a fortress. It will look like a cross.

Application

Before Sunday, write down the name of one voice you have let shape what you believe about God this year, whether a preacher, a podcast, an author, or a friend. Do not judge it yet. Just name it, and beside the name write one sentence describing the Jesus that voice presents. Keep the paper somewhere you will see it. The week is going to hand you tests, and a test is only useful if you already know what you are testing.

Opening Question for the Week

When has something that looked strong in your spiritual life (a confident teacher, a big claim, a leader who never seemed to lose) turned out to be hollow, and what did it cost you to find out? If God's power really works through what looks weak, where might you be dismissing the very thing He is using right now?

Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, we are walking into four chapters where Your servant is under attack, and we admit we do not always know a counterfeit when we see one. We like what sounds confident. We trust what looks strong. Give us eyes this week to see the difference between polish and power, and give us the courage to want the real thing even when it comes wrapped in weakness. Guard the churches we love from teachers who preach a different Jesus, and guard our own hearts, because we are capable of building strongholds too. Where we have measured ourselves against other people, measure us against Christ. Where we have been harsh, teach us His gentleness, and where we have been cowardly, teach us His boldness. Open these letters to us the way You opened them to Corinth, so that when the week ends we are not more impressed with Paul but more devoted to the One he served. In Jesus' name, Amen.
