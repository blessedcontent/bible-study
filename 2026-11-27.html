Further Thought

Testing the ProphetsLesson 9Day 7 of 7

Week in Review

All week, people stood in front of voices that claimed to speak for God. Scripture never told them to believe everything or to trust nothing. It gave them tests, and the tests kept leading to the same Son.

  • ☩Sabbath: Testing the Prophets. John told churches deciding whom to let in to test the spirits, because testing is how we keep what is truly God's.
  • ☩Sunday: Role of the Supernatural. God's power put Daniel on the ground and lifted him to listen, but Moses warned that even a sign that comes true can lead the wrong way.
  • ☩Monday: Harmony With the Bible. God does not lie or drift, so a true voice agrees with what He has already said.
  • ☩Tuesday: The Incarnation of Christ. John's sharpest test asks whether a voice confesses that Jesus Christ came in the flesh, where our rescue happened.
  • ☩Wednesday: Positive Fruits. God's messengers ran, quarreled, and drew back, and Jesus still taught that a tree is known by what it produces.
  • ☩Thursday: Fulfilled Prophecies. Jeremiah's word against a false prophet of peace came true that year, and Nineveh's warning did not, because the city turned.

Historical Context

The Tests Were Taken at Doors and Riverbanks

Gather the week's settings. A house church deciding whom to let through its door. An old exile praying beside the Tigris while the rebuilding back home ran into trouble. A frightened Judah urged to ask the dead. Churches warned about teachers who said Jesus only seemed to suffer. The LORD's servant buying passage west. Envoys of five kings in Jerusalem, and a prophet promising that Babylon's power would soon break.

None of this happened in a classroom. Daniel was so overwhelmed that he needed strength just to listen. Jonah had to choose whether to obey. The others had to decide whose voice to trust, and homes, churches, and a nation lived with the answer. God's tests were made for places like that.

What This Week Clarifies

Coming True Was Never the Whole Test

On Sunday, Moses warned that a prophet's sign could come true and the prophet could still be false. On Thursday, Jeremiah made fulfillment the test for a prophet of peace, and Moses had given that rule too. So does a word that comes true prove anything or not?

He Does Not Change, and That Is Why He Relents

On Monday, a hired seer said God does not change His mind. On Thursday, the same Hebrew verb said God relents when a nation turns, and Nineveh was spared. If God can relent, can we trust what He says?

Some Tests Work at the Door

On Sabbath, John's churches had to decide at the front door whom to let in. But on Wednesday and Thursday, the tests of fruit and fulfillment took months or years to read. What were those households supposed to do on the doorstep?

Still Speaking

She Knew Which Voice to Follow Down

The windshield of the small plane has been gray from edge to edge for ten minutes. The pilot bringing it home cannot see the ground, the runway, or the hills she knows are somewhere off her left wing. What she has is a voice on the radio, and the radio is crowded. Other pilots are checking in. Someone keeps keying a microphone by accident. Before she trusts any instruction, she listens for her own call sign.

She checks everything the voice tells her. The turns and altitudes fit the approach she studied on the ground before she took off. Each one brings her down toward the runway and away from the hills, never the other way. The controller does not sound impressive. He sounds steady, and he keeps talking. Then, low over the trees, the gray thins, and the runway lights are right where every check said they would be. She flies the last mile on the word he gave her, and she is glad to.

The tests we met this week work like her checks. We ask where a voice leads, whether it agrees with what God has already said, what it confesses about Jesus come in the flesh, what grows from it, and whether its words hold over time. God did not give His people those questions to keep them circling in the gray, suspicious of every sound. John told his churches to test the spirits so they would know which voice to follow (1 John 4:1).

And the voice the tests lead to is not a stranger's. It belongs to the One who took on our flesh, and who told His friends what would happen before it happened so that they would believe (John 14:29). Most of us are still somewhere in the gray. We know whose voice this is, and we can follow it down.

Christ at the Center of the Week

Every day this week, the tests led to the same Person. On Sabbath, Jesus called Himself the resurrection and the life and proved it at a grave (John 11:25). On Sunday, the One whose presence put John on the ground laid a hand on him (Revelation 1:17). On Monday, the first promise of a crushed serpent pointed to Him (Genesis 3:15). On Tuesday, He took on our flesh to carry our sin. On Wednesday, the only life with no bad fruit in it was given for flawed messengers like us (Isaiah 53:4-6). On Thursday, He predicted that He would be killed and raised (Matthew 16:21), and He told His friends ahead of time that He was going to the Father, so that when it happened they would believe (John 14:28-29). The prophets were tested because their words mattered, and those words kept pointing to Him. The tests were never the goal. They clear the road so we can hear Him.

Weekend Practice

Before Sabbath, choose one voice that shaped your faith this week, whether a sermon, a song, a book, or a friend. Ask two of this week's questions of it. Does it agree with Scripture? What does it say about Jesus? If you want to go further, read 1 John 4:1-6 slowly over the weekend.

Closing Prayer for the Week

Father, You have not left us to guess which voices are Yours. Thank You for Your unchanging word, for Your Son who came in our flesh, and for the patience You showed messengers who stumbled. Give us steady hearts when signs dazzle us and when easy promises of peace tempt us. Teach us to test with love and to hold on to what is good. Where we have believed too quickly, forgive us. Where we have stopped listening, open our ears. Above every voice, let us hear Your Son, and follow Him. In Jesus' name, Amen.
