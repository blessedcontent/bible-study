Come!

Into EternityLesson 13Day 6 of 7

In brief

The whole Bible, sixty-six books and thousands of years, ends not with a command or a warning but with an invitation: "Come." Jesus says it to the tired and the burdened. Isaiah shouts it to the thirsty and the broke: come and drink, no money needed. And on the last page the Spirit and the bride say it like a longing, while Jesus answers, "Yes, I am coming soon." Two arrows pointing at each other: we cry "come" to Him, and He promises to come for us. The last word of the book is an open door, and the only thing left to do is walk through it today.

Yesterday

Yesterday we watched the Lamb become the Shepherd, leading His people to springs of living water and wiping away the last tear. Today He turns from the flock and looks straight at you, and He says one word: "Come." The water He leads us to is not behind a gate marked "qualified only." It is offered free, today, to anyone thirsty enough to walk up and drink. Wednesday showed us where the Shepherd is leading. Today is the invitation to start following, and the beautiful news that He is just as eager to come to us as we are to come to Him.

Historical Context

The Most Valuable Thing in a Dry Land Was Water

Picture a sunbaked town in the ancient Near East at midday. There is no tap, no bottle in a cooler, no faucet. Water means a daily walk to a well or a spring, a heavy jar balanced on the shoulder, and a careful eye on how much is left. In the cities, water-sellers worked the streets, calling out their wares, and people paid real money for what we pour out without a thought. In a climate where a drought could empty a village, water was not a convenience. It was the line between life and death, and everyone knew its price down to the last coin.

Now hear how scandalous Isaiah's invitation would have sounded in that world. He pictures God as a vendor in the marketplace, except this vendor is shouting at the thirsty and the penniless to come and take the most precious thing there is for nothing. Come, buy wine and milk without money. It breaks every rule of the market. And that is exactly the point. The thing we need most to live, real life with God, is the one thing we could never afford and could never earn, so He gives it away. When the Bible's final invitation arrives, it uses the same shocking word: the water of life, freely. In a world that priced water by the drop, God set the cost of eternal life at zero, because Someone else had already paid it in full.

Into the Word

It is no accident that the Bible ends with an invitation rather than an instruction. After all the prophecy and the warnings and the visions of the end, the last thing God wants ringing in our ears is a single open-armed word: come. Today's reading gathers that word from across the whole of Scripture, from a tired crowd in Galilee to a thirsty marketplace in Isaiah to the final sentence John ever wrote, and shows that the invitation has always been the same, and it is still open to you right now.

Street Level

The Gift You Tried to Pay For

Think about the most extravagant gift someone ever handed you, something they clearly saved for or went without to give. If you are like most people, your very first reaction was not clean joy. It was a kind of flustered scramble. Oh, you shouldn't have. This is too much. Here, at least let me give you something. We do it almost without thinking, and it feels like humility, but watch the giver's face the moment we do. Something falls. Because the one thing they wanted, the only thing, was to watch us simply receive it and be glad. Trying to pay them back does not honor the gift. It quietly refuses it. The hardest, most grown-up thing in the world is sometimes just to say thank you and let a gift be a gift.

That awkward scramble is the exact thing standing between most of us and God. Four times in three verses He calls, "Come, buy without money, without cost." And four times something in us answers, surely there is a catch, surely I have to bring something, let me clean myself up first and earn a little of this. That is not humility. It is the strange pride that would rather buy than receive, and it leaves the water sitting there untouched while we go on spending ourselves on things that never fill us. The whole Bible ends on one open-armed word, and He is the one saying it. Come. The tab is already paid. The only thing left for you to do is stop reaching for your wallet and drink.

Today’s World

The Money They Could Not Believe Was Free

Starting in 2017, an organization called GiveDirectly ran the largest experiment of its kind ever attempted, handing out roughly thirty million dollars in cash to about twenty-three thousand people across some of the poorest villages in Kenya. No conditions. No repayment. No strings. And one of the first hurdles the team ran into was not greed or waste. It was disbelief. Over and over, people simply could not accept that the money was actually free. Many assumed it had to be a loan they would owe back later. Some heard rumors and braced for a trap. It is a familiar reflex: when an offer looks too generous, the human mind goes hunting for the hidden cost, certain there must be a catch somewhere.

Read that and the last invitation in the Bible suddenly sounds very current. "Come, buy without money, without cost. Take the water of life freely." And something in us answers back exactly like those villagers. Surely this is really a loan. Surely there is fine print. Surely I will owe for this somehow. We go looking for the catch because a gift with no strings offends the part of us that wants to earn its own way in. But there is no fine print. The tab was paid at the cross, the offer is real, and the only thing left to do with water this free is to quit searching for the catch and drink.

Christ at the center

The water is free to us only because it was unbearably expensive to Him. On the cross, Jesus cried "I thirst," and was handed sour wine on a sponge. The One who shouts "come, everyone who thirsts" went thirsty Himself, so that the invitation could be free for the rest of us. That is why "let the one who desires the water of life drink freely" is not cheap. It is the most costly gift in the universe, marked down to nothing by the blood of the Giver. And the same Jesus who paid it is the One promising "I am coming soon." The invitation and the return are held in the same scarred hands. He came the first time as the Lamb to make the water free; He is coming the second time as the King to take His thirsty people home. Between those two comings stands one open door and one word echoing down the centuries to you, today: come.

Application

Take the invitation personally today, out loud. Find two private minutes and actually say it: "Lord Jesus, I come. I am tired and I cannot pay, and I am taking the water of life You offer for free." If you have said it a hundred times, say it again like it is true. Then add the prayer that ends the Bible, the one His people have whispered for two thousand years: "Come, Lord Jesus." One sentence of surrender, one sentence of longing. Let that be the shape of your whole day.

Discussion Question

"Where in your life are you still trying to 'pay' for what God wants to give you free (acceptance, rest, a clean record), spending yourself on things that do not satisfy? What would it actually look like this week to stop earning and simply come and drink, and then to turn and say 'come' to one other thirsty person?"

Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You that the whole story ends with an open door and not a slammed one. We are weary and we are thirsty, and we have spent too much of our lives paying for things that never satisfied. So we come. We take the water of life from Your hand, freely, the way You always meant for us to. Give us the faith to endure, to keep the longing alive, to surrender wholly to the One who died for us. And we pray back to You the oldest prayer of Your waiting people: come quickly. We will be watching for You. In Jesus' name, Amen.
