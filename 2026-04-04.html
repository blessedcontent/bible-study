To Know God

To Know GodLesson 2Day 1 of 7

In brief

This week we chase one of the Bible's most audacious claims: that the God who flung galaxies into existence actually wants to be known by you.

  • ☩ The Bible insists God is not distant or unknowable; He actively reveals His character so we can respond to who He really is
  • ☩ Sin has distorted humanity's picture of God, creating a fog most of us don't even realize we're looking through
  • ☩ Holiness, love, creativity, and incarnation aren't abstract theological categories; they are windows into the heart of a Person who wants relationship
  • ☩ Knowing God isn't collecting facts about Him; Jesus said it is eternal life itself (John 17:3)

Last week

Last week's "Reality Check" asked us to take an honest look at where we actually stand with God, confronting the Laodicean tendency to assume everything is fine when it isn't. That honest self-assessment was the starting point. This week builds on it: once we've looked in the mirror, the next question is, who exactly is the God we're trying to get closer to? You can't deepen a relationship with someone you've misunderstood. So before we go further, we need to let the Bible correct, sharpen, and expand our picture of who God really is.

Historical Context

Gods You Could Never Know

Every civilization surrounding ancient Israel worshiped gods who were, by design, unknowable. Mesopotamian deities like Marduk and Enlil were cosmic forces to be managed, not persons to be understood. Egyptian religion mapped the divine onto nature's cycles: Ra rode the sun across the sky, Osiris ruled the dead, and a priest's job was to keep the machinery running through ritual precision. Canaanite worship revolved around Baal, a storm god whose favor had to be purchased through offerings and whose moods shifted like the weather he supposedly controlled. In every case, the pattern was the same: the gods were powerful, the gods were dangerous, and the gods did not explain themselves.

Into that world, Israel's God said something no other deity had ever said: "Know Me." Not "fear Me" as a nameless force. Not "serve Me" and hope for the best. Know Me. The Hebrew word for this kind of knowing, yada, is the same word used for the most intimate human relationships. It implies depth, closeness, personal experience. This was a staggering claim in the ancient Near East, and it set the God of Scripture apart from every other religious tradition of the time.

This week, we explore what that invitation actually looks like. The passages span from Genesis to Matthew, from creation to incarnation, because God's self-revelation isn't a single moment; it's a story that unfolds across the entire Bible. Each day this week picks up a different facet of His character: holiness, love, creative power, and ultimately, His decision to move into the neighborhood and live among us.

Christ at the center

Here is the question that will build all week long: if God is holy, if God is love, if God is the Creator of everything, how could we ever truly know Someone that vast? The Bible's answer is breathtaking in its simplicity. We know God because God made Himself knowable. And He did it in the most unexpected way imaginable: He became one of us.

Every attribute we'll study this week, holiness, love, creative power, finds its fullest expression not in a theological textbook but in a Person. Jesus did not just teach us about God's character; He is God's character in human form. When Philip asked, "Lord, show us the Father," Jesus replied, "The one who has seen me has seen the Father" (John 14:9). The week builds toward Thursday's study, "Immanuel, God With Us," because that is where every thread converges: the holy, loving, creating God didn't stay at a distance. He moved into the neighborhood. And that changes everything about how we know Him.

Application

Before Sunday's study, take ten minutes with a blank page and answer this question honestly: "What do I actually believe about God's character right now?" Write the first things that come to mind, whether they feel theologically correct or not. Don't edit. Don't filter. Just be honest. Then fold the paper and keep it. At the end of the week, open it again and see what has shifted. You might be surprised how much of your picture of God was built from assumptions rather than Scripture.

Opening Question for the Week

"If someone who had never heard of God asked you to describe Him in three sentences, what would you say, and what would you leave out?"

Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, we come to You this week admitting that our picture of who You are is incomplete, and in some places, probably wrong. We have inherited assumptions, absorbed cultural distortions, and carried wounds that have colored how we see You. This week, as we open Your Word, we ask You to do what only You can do: show us Yourself as You truly are. Not the God of our fears, not the God of our projections, but the God who is actually there. Give us the courage to let go of the versions of You that feel comfortable but aren't accurate, and the faith to trust what You reveal in their place. We want to know You, the real You, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom You have sent. In Jesus' name, Amen.
