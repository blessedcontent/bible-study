Destroying the Message: Part 1

Now Hear This!Lesson 7Day 3 of 7

In brief

It is winter in Jerusalem, and a fire burns in the king's room. A man named Jehudi reads aloud from a scroll of God's warnings. Every few columns, King Jehoiakim cuts off what was just read and drops it into the fire, until the scroll is gone. Neither the king nor his attendants tear their clothes. Destroying God's message did not cancel what it warned about. It only threw away the king's time to respond.

Yesterday

Yesterday a frightened crowd at Kadesh threatened to stone two faithful scouts, and God called it contempt for Him. Centuries later, a king in Jerusalem refused God's word in a quieter way. The crowd had talked of stones. This king picked up a scribe's knife.

Historical Context

This King Had Already Killed a Prophet

Early in Jehoiakim's reign, Jeremiah stood in the temple courtyard with a warning. The temple could end up like Shiloh, where God's house had once stood. Priests and prophets demanded his death. Then officials and elders stepped in, and the elders recalled that King Hezekiah had listened to the prophet Micah instead of killing him (Jeremiah 26).

That same chapter remembers Uriah, who prophesied the same message. When Jehoiakim tried to kill him, Uriah fled to Egypt. The king sent men to bring him back, then struck him down with the sword (Jeremiah 26:20-23). When Baruch's scroll later reached the king's officials, they told Baruch and Jeremiah to hide (Jeremiah 36:19). One of the officials who begged the king not to burn it was Elnathan son of Achbor (Jeremiah 36:12). He had helped bring Uriah back from Egypt.

Into the Word

Jeremiah had been pleading with Jerusalem for years. Stand at the crossroads and look, God said through him. Ask for the ancient paths, find the good way, and walk in it. The people answered, We will not walk in it, and, We will not listen (Jeremiah 6:8-19). Now the warnings have been read aloud from a scroll, and the officials have carried the news to the king. What will a king do with words he does not want?

Street Level

He Stopped Opening the Envelopes

Imagine a young man who falls two payments behind on his truck after his hours get cut. Envelopes from the lender start arriving. He knows what they say, so he stops opening them. He stacks them on top of the refrigerator, where he does not have to look at them. When his girlfriend reaches for one, he takes it out of her hand. Just open it, she says. We can figure it out together. He tosses it back on the pile.

The envelopes keep coming. Each one carries the same news as the last, with another late fee added. Nobody leaves those envelopes sealed because he thinks the lender made a mistake. He leaves them sealed because he knows. One morning in March, he walks outside and the truck is gone. A tow truck took it before dawn. The stack on the refrigerator had not changed a single number. It had only used up the months when one phone call might have saved the truck.

Jehoiakim's knife did the same thing to God's scroll. Cutting it up could not stop Babylon's army. It could only spend the time the warning had been sent to give him. A lender is not God, and losing a truck is not a judgment from heaven. But the pattern holds. Ignoring a warning never reaches the thing it warns about. The stack kept nothing from him but time.

Today’s World

They Kept the Alarm Switched Off

Just after 3:30 on the morning of July 25, 2021, the cargo ship BBC Marmara ran aground on an island off the west coast of Scotland. Britain's Marine Accident Investigation Branch found that the officer on watch was asleep, alone on the bridge. He had been drinking before his watch and at its start. The ship's voyage recorder had picked up snoring on the bridge at 2:48. The ship was moving at about 11 knots when it struck. No one was hurt and there was no pollution, but the front of the hull was damaged.

The ship carried an alarm built for exactly that danger. If the officer on watch did not reset it every few minutes, it would show a light, then sound on the bridge, then sound for the captain or a backup officer. On that ship it was usual practice to leave it switched off. The investigators found that the watchkeepers and the captain saw resetting it as an inconvenience. The captain's key, kept on the bridge, made it easy to turn off.

Had the alarm been on, the investigators said, it should have woken him. Failing that, its next stage would have alerted the captain, and the grounding might have been avoided. Instead, the voice on the bridge built to say wake up had been switched off, and the island stayed right where it was. Jehoiakim tried that with a scroll.

Christ at the center

The warnings on that scroll came true. Years later, under King Zedekiah, Babylon's army surrounded Jerusalem, and Jeremiah was held under guard. That is when God told him to buy a field (Jeremiah 32), because houses, fields, and vineyards would again be bought in this land (Jeremiah 32:15). While Jeremiah was still confined, God promised to raise up a righteous Branch for David (Jeremiah 33:14-16). Jehoiakim had been told he would have no one to sit on David's throne. But God had not given up on David's throne. Centuries later an angel told Mary that God would give her Son the throne of His father David, and that His kingdom would never end (Luke 1:32-33). A king cut up the word that warned him. God kept the word that promised a King.

Application

Take two minutes. Think of a warning in Scripture you would rather skip. If none comes to mind, read Jeremiah 6:16. Instead of arguing with it, tell God plainly what you are afraid it might cost you. Then ask Him to help you take it to heart instead of setting it aside.

Discussion Question

Jehoiakim complained that the scroll said the king of Babylon would come. Why do people try to get rid of a warning instead of facing it? What helps you face one?

Closing Prayer

Father, we confess that we have sometimes treated Your word the way Jehoiakim did. We have skipped the hard parts, argued with them, or quietly set them aside. Give us hearts that are moved when Your word warns us. Thank You for keeping Your words when people tried to burn them, and for hiding Your messengers. Thank You that Your promise of a King came true in Jesus. While there is time, help us listen. In Jesus' name, Amen.
