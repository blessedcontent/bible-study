Share Him

Share HimLesson 12Day 1 of 7

In brief

Before the sun is up, before a single word is spoken, a Servant lets God wake His ear. That is where this week starts, because before we ever talk about sharing Jesus, God has something to say about listening. The week walks from the Great Commission to a kitchen-table grief: the postal carrier who has never heard, the friend who quietly walked away, the child you still pray home. No scripts, no pressure tactics, no forcing. Just a love so real it spills. Sharing Him begins with an awakened ear, not a polished pitch.

Last week

Last week ended at the top of the climb, looking into a Face turned toward us with joy, with the marks still in His hands and the verdict finally refused. A setback is never proof that God stopped loving us. But notice what happens to a person who has been carried through a storm like that. They cannot keep it quiet. The disciples who watched the wind get muzzled asked, "Who then is this?" and spent the rest of their lives answering that question out loud to anyone who would listen. This week turns us around on the trail. The God we have spent eleven weeks learning to know, trust, and hold onto in the dark is not meant to stay our secret.

Historical Context

The Rumor That Outran the Empire

Around the year 178, a Greek philosopher named Celsus sat down to write the most thorough attack on Christianity the ancient world had yet produced, and what offended him most was not the theology. It was the messengers. Wool-workers, he sneered. Cobblers. Laundry attendants. People with no education and no standing, talking about Jesus in kitchens, in shops, in the women's quarters, persuading children and tradesmen while the philosophers were not looking. He meant it as an insult. He had accidentally written down the secret.

The early church had no buildings, no broadcasting, no social position, and for long stretches no legal right to exist. Yet within three centuries the faith of a crucified carpenter had traveled every trade road in the Roman world, not carried by professional orators but passed from hand to hand like bread: a slave telling another slave, a merchant talking across a market stall, a mother praying over her children. Historians still marvel at it. The fastest-growing movement in the ancient world ran almost entirely on ordinary people who could not stop talking about what had happened to them. This week is about that kind of talking, where it comes from, and why it was never supposed to be the specialists' job.

Christ at the center

Whose tongue is the Memory Text describing? Read two verses further and the answer becomes unmistakable: "I gave My back to those who struck Me" (Isaiah 50:6). The Servant with the instructed tongue is the Servant on the road to the cross. The same mouth that learned, morning by morning, how to sustain the weary with a word is the mouth that stayed silent before His accusers and then spoke forgiveness from the nails. Every word in season we will ever offer a tired friend was purchased there. So here is the question planted at the start of this week, and worth carrying into every page: if His words to the weary now travel in our mouths, what is He waiting to say, through you, to someone this week?

Application

Before Sunday, write down the name of one weary person God has already placed in your path: a coworker running on fumes, a neighbor going through it, a family member who has drifted from Him. Just the name. Then each morning this week, before you check your phone, pray one sentence over that name: "Lord, awaken my ear, and if You have a word in season for them, let me carry it." Do not script the conversation. Keep showing up each morning to listen, and watch what God does with an awakened ear.

Opening Question for the Week

"When was the last time you talked about Jesus with someone who doesn't know Him (a coworker, a neighbor, the person who cuts your hair)? What would have to change inside you for that conversation to feel less like a duty and more like an overflow?"

Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, You have been speaking to us all quarter, and this week You ask us to open our mouths. We confess that the thought makes us nervous. We are afraid of saying it wrong, afraid of being awkward, afraid of what people will think of us. So before You give us words, wake our ears. Wake us morning by morning the way You woke Your Servant. Teach us to listen to You first and to the weary people around us second, and to trust that You can do more with one honest sentence from an ordinary person than we ever imagined. Show us this week that sharing You was never a performance. It is an overflow. Fill us until it spills. In Jesus' name, Amen.
