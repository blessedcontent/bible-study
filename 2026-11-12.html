For Our Admonition

Now Hear This!Lesson 7Day 6 of 7

In brief

Near Corinth, athletes trained hard for a wreath of pine that would soon dry out. Paul tells the Corinthians that he runs to win. Then he points to a whole generation that left Egypt together and fell in the desert, and says their failures were written down as warnings for us. The Bible's warnings show us where others fell so that we can stand, leaning not on our own record but on God's faithfulness.

Yesterday

Yesterday Jesus warned leaders who honored dead prophets while refusing to hear the living word. It is tempting to read that chapter as being only about them. It turned out to be a mirror. Today Paul explains why Scripture holds up mirrors like that.

Historical Context

Corinth's Athletes Raced for a Pine Wreath

Every two years, athletes from across the Greek world gathered near Corinth for the Isthmian Games. The games were held at a sanctuary of the sea god Poseidon. In Paul's day the winners were crowned with a wreath of pine, and the prize soon dried out.

Paul uses those games just before his wilderness warnings. Everyone in a race runs, he says, but only one wins the prize. He trains hard so that after preaching to others he himself will not be disqualified (1 Corinthians 9:24-27). Then he turns straight to Israel in the wilderness. They all started out together, and most of them did not finish (1 Corinthians 10:1-5). Starting well was never the same as finishing.

Into the Word

Paul has just reminded the Corinthians that Israel's whole generation passed under the cloud and through the sea, and ate and drank what God provided (1 Corinthians 10:1-4). They had every advantage. So why did most of them fall in the desert, and what does that have to do with a church in Corinth?

Paul does not leave the Corinthians with only a warning. In the next breath he adds a promise. God is faithful, and when we are tempted He provides a way out, so that we can stand up under it (1 Corinthians 10:13). If our standing rests on His faithfulness, what is left to boast about? Paul answered that question for another church.

Street Level

The Men Who Fell Were Good Roofers

Suppose a roofing crew is working a low, easy roof on a sunny morning. The newest hire is twenty-two and quick on his feet, the best natural roofer the foreman has hired in years. He leaves his harness in the truck. He has never slipped, and this roof is barely tilted. At lunch the oldest man on the crew sits down next to him on an upside-down bucket and starts naming men he has known who fell.

None of them were clumsy. One had roofed for twenty years. One fell on the last row of the day, reaching for a bundle he could almost touch. One unhooked for a minute to move his line. Two fell from roofs as flat as this one. The old man is not trying to scare the kid off the roof. He wants him to notice what the stories have in common. Every man he named was sure of his footing. Then he hands the kid his harness and points to the anchor screwed into the ridge.

Paul told Israel's wilderness stories in the same spirit. He was not saying those people were weaker than the Corinthians. He wanted the Corinthians to see that pattern coming in their own lives. A harness does not make a roofer timid. It lets him work freely, because his life no longer hangs on his balance. Paul says God is faithful and provides a way out. The kid's feet were good. The anchor was better.

Today’s World

The First Flight's Scars Changed the Second

When NASA's Orion capsule came home from its first trip around the Moon in late 2022, no one was aboard. Its heat shield had lost charred material in more than a hundred spots. Flight data showed that a crew would have been safe, but engineers still set out to learn why. They removed about 200 samples from the shield and ran 121 tests. In December 2024 NASA named the cause. During the capsule's skipping return, gases inside the shield's material could not escape as expected. Pressure built up, the material cracked, and pieces of the charred layer broke away.

NASA kept the heat shield already built for the next flight but changed the way home. It shortened the longest distance Orion could fly between entering the atmosphere and splashing down. That would limit how long the shield spent in the temperature range linked to the damage. On April 10, 2026, four astronauts splashed down off San Diego. NASA's later analysis counted nine spots of char loss, compared with 103 on the first flight, and found the performance in line with engineers' predictions.

No heat shield makes anyone right with God, and learning from a test flight is not the same as trusting Him. But the habit is worth noticing. The damage from the first flight did not stay a curiosity in a report. NASA let that record change the plan before a crew flew. Paul wanted the Corinthians to treat Israel's record the same way.

Christ at the center

Israel's wilderness story has another chapter. After His baptism, Jesus went into the wilderness for forty days (Matthew 4:1-11). He was tempted to turn stones into bread, to test God by throwing Himself from the temple, and to worship someone other than God. Those were the same tests Israel failed in the wilderness. They craved food, they tested God, and they worshiped an idol. Each time Jesus answered with words from Deuteronomy, words first given to Israel in the wilderness. Where they fell, He stood. Then He went to the cross for people who had fallen, and that cross is the only boast Paul allowed himself (Galatians 6:14). Our hope is not that we will never fall. It is that He stood for us, and that God is faithful to hold us up.

Application

Take two minutes. Read 1 Corinthians 10:12-13. Name one area where you are sure you could never fall. Ask God to show you the way out He has promised, and thank Him that your footing rests on His faithfulness, not on your record.

Discussion Question

Paul says Israel's failures were written down as warnings for us. What is the difference between a warning that shames and one that protects? Which Bible story has helped you see a pattern in your own life?

Closing Prayer

Faithful God, thank You for writing down the failures of Your people, so that we could see the pattern before we walk into it. Keep us from thinking we stand by our own strength. When we are tempted, show us the way out You have promised, and give us the humility to take it. We boast in nothing but the cross of Your Son, who stood where Israel fell. Hold us up, because You are faithful. In Jesus' name, Amen.
