Testing the Prophets

Testing the ProphetsLesson 9Day 1 of 7

In brief

Picture a knock at the door of a house where a small church meets. A traveler outside says he speaks for the Lord and asks to stay and teach. Someone inside has to decide whether to let him in. John wrote to churches that faced moments like this. Do not believe every spirit, he told them. Test them. Not every voice that uses God's name speaks for Him, so God asks His people to test what they hear, and every test leads back to His word and His Son.

Last week

Last week ended at a read-through, where a woman in the back row turned a cast back one page to hear a hard line rightly. The One the Scriptures are about still walks beside the reader. This week asks what to do with voices that claim to speak for Him.

Historical Context

A Stranger at the Door Said He Spoke for God

The first Christians had no church buildings. They met in homes, like the church in the house of Priscilla and Aquila (Romans 16:5). Traveling teachers moved from one of these households to the next, and the churches were expected to take them in. John praised a friend for welcoming brothers he had never met and helping them on their way, because they had gone out for the sake of the Name (3 John 1:5-8).

That open door carried a risk. In another short letter, John told his readers not to take in teachers who denied that Jesus Christ had come in the flesh (2 John 1:7-10). His own churches had watched some people walk away (1 John 2:19), and he warned that others were trying to lead them astray (1 John 2:26). So when John wrote test the spirits, he was not setting a classroom exercise. The test happened at the front door, before a stranger sat down to teach the people you loved.

Christ at the center

Jesus did not ask to be excused from testing. When His critics doubted Him, He pointed to His works and told them that if He was not doing His Father's works, they should not believe Him (John 10:37-38). Beside the grave of His friend Lazarus, He made a claim no one could fake for long. I am the resurrection and the life (John 11:25). He asked Martha whether she believed it, and soon after He called a man who had been dead four days out of the tomb (John 11:43-44). Carry one question into the coming days. What does this voice do with Jesus? He made His claim out loud, and then He let a grave answer it.

Application

This week, when something claims to come from God, whether a sermon clip, a shared post, or a strong impression of your own, pause before you pass it on. Ask one question. Does it agree with Scripture and lead toward Jesus? Try it once today with something you heard recently.

Opening Question for the Week

John wrote Beloved before he wrote test. Why might love and careful testing belong together? Which voices are you most likely to believe without testing (ones that sound sure of themselves, ones you already agree with, or ones that make you feel good)?

Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, You have never stopped speaking, and You have never asked us to believe a voice just because it uses Your name. This week, open these passages to us. Give us the humility to test what we hear and the courage to hold on to what is good. Where we have been quick to believe, slow us down. Where we have grown suspicious of everything, teach us to listen again. Train our ears to know Your voice, and keep our eyes on Your Son. In Jesus' name, Amen.
