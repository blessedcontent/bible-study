Triumph in Christ

Love-driven MinistryLesson 9Day 6 of 7

In brief

An open door in Troas, listeners leaning in, and Paul cannot stay. His brother Titus is missing, the news from Corinth has not come, and there is no peace in his spirit. Then, mid-story, remembered joy breaks through and he starts to sing. Thanks be to God, who always leads us in triumph, glad captives in Christ's own parade, spreading the fragrance of knowing Him through every anxious mile. God is leading the parade even on the days you cannot find your peace, and the scent of Christ travels with you.

Yesterday

Yesterday ended in the best kind of church meeting, censure giving way to comfort, a drowning man pulled back into love. But that was the ending of the story. Today the letter rewinds to its anxious middle, the stretch after Paul had sent the tearful letter and heard nothing at all. We get to watch him wait. It matters that Scripture kept this part, because most of our lives happen here, after the hard step of obedience and before any word of how it landed. Paul waited badly by the world's standards, restless and unable to settle. And it was exactly there, he says, that God was leading a parade.

Historical Context

Rome Had a Smell for Winning

Rome had a smell for winning. When a general was granted a triumph, the city turned his route into one long ribbon of celebration, and the celebration reached your nose before it reached your eyes. Incense smoked on altars along the way, garlands hung sweet over the streets, and the crowd caught the scent of glory drifting ahead of the procession itself, before the trumpets, before the wagons of plunder, before the general's chariot rolled past. To a Roman on the curb, that perfume meant victory, holiday, spectacle, the gods smiling on the city. It was the smell of being on the winning side.

But the parade had a back half. Behind the treasure walked the chained, captured enemies displayed as living proof of conquest, and for the men in chains the same sweet smoke told a different story. Triumphs commonly ended with prize captives led away to execution while the city cheered. One scent, two futures. Glory in the nostrils of the victors, doom in the nostrils of the condemned. Paul sets his entire ministry inside that picture and makes one scandalous edit. He numbers himself among the captives, seized on the Damascus road and marched ever since wherever his Captor pleases, and then he does what no Roman prisoner ever did. He gives thanks for the parade. Keep the incense in the air as you read today, because the aroma is about to become the whole point.

Into the Word

The story resumes at Troas, and by every ministry metric things were going well there. A door stood open in the Lord, listeners leaning in, the kind of opening missionaries pray years for. And Paul could not stay. Titus was not there, the news from Corinth had not come, and there was no peace in his spirit. So he walked away from an open door to go find one church's answer. What kind of triumph is that? Today's passage answers, the truest kind, because the parade was never measured by Paul's composure. It is measured by who is leading it.

We already know how the road ends, Titus arriving with news that turned sorrow into joy (2 Corinthians 7:5-9). And here in the middle of the travel story, that remembered joy detonates early. Paul interrupts his own itinerary and starts to sing.

Street Level

You Sound Like Where You Live

Nobody ever sat you down and taught you your accent. There was no class, no drills, no certificate on the wall. You just lived somewhere. You bought groceries, argued with neighbors, told stories on somebody's porch, and the place quietly moved into your mouth. Years later, three sentences into a phone call, a stranger says it. You're not from around here, are you. Actors hire dialect coaches and rehearse for months trying to fake what you got for free, and one vowel still gives them away. An accent cannot really be performed. It can only be picked up, and only from the voices you live closest to.

Paul says knowing Jesus works the same way. Stay near Him long enough, in His word, in prayer, among His people, and something starts riding on your ordinary sentences that you never rehearsed. People catch it before they can name it. And notice when that fragrance was spreading in today's verses. Not from a man on a victory lap. From a man with no peace in his spirit, walking away from an open door, scanning every road for Titus. Here is the mercy inside the picture. An accent does not switch off on your anxious days. On the worst afternoon of your year, you still sound like home. Your influence for Christ was never your composure. It is His nearness, absorbed and carried, and it keeps giving Him away in seasons when all you can manage is to stay close. So stay close. You will never have to remember to sound like Him.

Today’s World

The Ten-Day Stay That Lasted Nine Months

In June 2024, NASA astronauts Butch Wilmore and Suni Williams traveled to the International Space Station for what was supposed to be a nine- or ten-day stay. Issues with their Boeing capsule made the trip home impossible until another ship became available, and ten days stretched into about nine months, every plan they had made dissolving into one long, unresolved wait. Wilmore did not let being stuck in space keep him from worshiping with his church. From orbit he watched videos of the services at his home congregation, Providence Baptist Church in Pasadena, Texas, called his church friends, and joined devotional events across the distance. To tie in and worship with my church family was vital, he said afterward. It's part of what makes me go.

Here is the strange arithmetic. A ten-day trip that ran on schedule would have come and gone with the calendar. The interrupted version turned into nine months of waiting with worship threaded all the way through it. The ride home finally came on March 18, and five days later, on Sunday, March 23, Wilmore was back in person at Providence Baptist, telling the congregation he understood that God is at work in all things. That is Troas arithmetic. Paul lost the door he wanted and discovered that God was leading the parade down the road he did not choose, spreading the fragrance of knowing Him through every anxious mile. The scent does not wait for your news to arrive. It rides the waiting too.

Christ at the center

The New Testament uses Paul's parade verb in only one other place, and it will take your breath. Colossians says God disarmed the powers of darkness and made a public spectacle of them, triumphing over them by the cross (Colossians 2:15). Line the two parades up. Rome staged a triumph when its killing succeeded. God staged His when His Son died, with every dark power marched behind a Victor who still carries nail marks. That is the procession Paul was glad to be captured into, a parade led by a General who won by laying His life down. It explains the fragrance too. Christ gave Himself up for us as a fragrant offering and sacrifice to God (Ephesians 5:2). The aroma we carry was never our own. It is His, the scent of self-giving love, and it clings to everyone who walks close behind Him. Rome's parades celebrated the day the killing worked. This one celebrates the day dying won, and it has been gathering glad captives ever since.

Application

Name the open loop you are living in right now, the test result that has not come back, the child who has not called, the application sitting in someone else's inbox. That is your Troas. Today, from inside it, before any news arrives, pray one sentence out loud. Thanks be to God, who leads me in His triumph even here. You are not thanking Him for an outcome you cannot see yet. You are thanking Him for the parade, and for the scent of His Son that your waiting can still carry.

Discussion Question

"Where are you living between the letter and the news right now (a test result, a prodigal child, an application, a conversation you cannot take back)? What would change if you thanked God from the middle of it instead of waiting for the ending, and who might catch the scent of Christ off the way you wait?"

Closing Prayer

Father, we know the road between Troas and Macedonia, the seasons with an open door in front of us and no peace inside us, the waiting that follows us from place to place. Thank You for a Bible honest enough to show us an apostle with a restless heart, and a God who did not shame him for it. Lead us in Your triumph anyway. When our composure fails, keep our feet in the procession, and let the fragrance of Christ cling to us in waiting rooms and hallways and kitchens where nothing is resolved yet. We pray tonight for the ones still waiting on their news, and we ask You to send each of them a Titus soon. Keep us from peddling You. Keep our motives in the daylight, and keep us close enough to Jesus that people can tell where we have been. Thanks be to You, God, who always leads us. In Jesus' name, Amen.
