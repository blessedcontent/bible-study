The God of Love

Grace, Love, and FellowshipLesson 13Day 4 of 7

In brief

Walk through Corinth and the gods you pass have titles. The god of the sea. The goddess of desire. The emperor's goddess of peace, carved in marble back in Rome. Then a tentmaker ends a hard letter with a title found nowhere else in Scripture, the God of love and peace. Today collects the names Paul gave God and finds one thing under all of them. God's titles are not departments He runs. They are the character He gives away.

Yesterday

Yesterday the love of God turned out to have a verb. God sent His Son while we were still enemies, and nothing on Paul's list can pry that love loose. Today Paul turns the noun into a name. He calls God the God of love, and it is the only place Scripture uses that exact title.

Historical Context

The Gods Had Titles for Everything They Ran

Every other spring the road east out of Corinth filled with athletes and gamblers. They were headed for Isthmia, where the games were held under the eye of Poseidon, god of the sea and of the earthquake. Up on the rock above town stood the shrine of Aphrodite, goddess of desire. Zeus had a title for one job after another. Protector of guests. Keeper of oaths. Sender of rain. A god's title told you his territory, and his territory told you what he could do for you or to you. Devotion to gods like these was often a matter of keeping them favorable. Rome added one more to the collection. Pax, the goddess of peace, got an altar from Augustus in the capital, built to celebrate the quiet that follows a Roman victory.

So when a letter read aloud in a Corinthian house called the Lord the God of love and peace, the phrase would have sounded strange. Love as a god's title pointed to Aphrodite and the desire she governed. Peace as a title pointed to Rome and the calm that follows a conquest. Paul takes both words and hangs them on the one God as a description of who He is rather than what He controls. Then he attaches a promise. This God will be with you. Of everything in that sentence, the promise may have been the strangest part.

Into the Word

Paul has a habit worth noticing. When he wants to bless people, he names God by what God is about to give them. The God of endurance. The God of hope. The God of all comfort. Today we collect those titles, and the collection starts with the rarest one. The phrase the God of love appears only once in the whole Bible, in the closing lines of this letter, spoken to a church Paul had just spent four chapters warning. Why there? And what does it mean that every one of God's titles turns out to be something He hands over?

That title was not a one-off. Paul had been naming God this way for years, and when you set the names side by side a portrait comes into focus.

Of all the titles, the God of peace is the one Paul used most, and the word he chose for peace is bigger than the absence of a fight.

Street Level

The Calm Was Already on the Line

Imagine a kitchen at seven in the evening. A man's wife has collapsed between the table and the stove, and he is on the phone with an emergency dispatcher, shouting. He shouts the address, shouts that she is not breathing right, shouts at her to hurry. On the other end is a woman he will never meet. Help is already on its way. The address went out the moment she had it, and the ambulance is rolling while he is still shouting into the phone. So she stays on the line and lowers her voice. She tells him she is right here and she is not going anywhere. She tells him to put the phone on the floor beside his wife and put his hands where she says.

Then something happens. He stops shouting. Not because she asked for quiet. Because the calm on the line is the only steady thing in that kitchen and he needs it. He counts with her. He presses when she says press. For four minutes the calm in that woman is the calm in his hands, and he did not earn a second of it. If he writes to thank her later, he will not know her name. He will call her what she gave him. The calm one.

Paul names God the same way, by what He is and what He hands over. From the God of hope, hope. From the God of peace, a whole person. So when he writes live in peace, and the God of love and peace will be with you, he is not putting a price on the visit. The help was on the line before the shouting stopped. Stopping is how you hear it.

Today’s World

The Salmon Were Back in Three Weeks

Four dams once blocked salmon from parts of the Klamath River in Oregon and California. Fish coming up from the ocean stopped below the lowest one. The river's tribes led a decades-long campaign to take them out, through hearings, lawsuits, and negotiation. In 2024 the dams came down. It was the largest river restoration project in American history, and it reopened nearly 400 miles of habitat.

On September 26, 2024, the last barrier in California came out. On October 15, less than three weeks later, state biologists found fall-run Chinook spawning in Jenny Creek, four miles above the old dam site. The first salmon in those tributaries since 1961. Nobody carried them there. A year on, a conservation group counted more than 10,000 fish over two feet long passing the old dam site, most likely Chinook. The group says it will take years of monitoring to know how the river recovers.

Paul's word for peace means more than a truce. It means things put back where they belong. A settlement that only stopped the lawsuits would have left the concrete in the river. The peace that mends takes the barrier out, and what was missing comes home.

Christ at the center

How would anyone know that the God behind the universe is a God of love and peace rather than a god to be managed? Corinth's temples suggested otherwise, and so does plenty of human experience. The title was not announced. It was shown. God made peace through the blood of Jesus' cross (Colossians 1:20), which means the God of peace paid for the peace Himself instead of billing the people who broke it. And the love in the title is the love Monday traced, the Son sent while we were still enemies. Jesus is where the God of love and the God of peace turn out to be the same God with one saving purpose. When He said He was leaving His peace with the disciples, not the way the world gives (John 14:27), He meant the shalom that mends a whole person, and He was hours from paying for it. The God of love and peace will be with you. He already came.

Application

Pray today using one of Paul's titles for God, and choose the one that matches what you are short on. God of hope, God of all comfort, God of peace, God of love. Say it out loud, then tell Him in one sentence why you picked it. Then take one step toward peace that needs nobody's permission, such as writing the apology you owe or dropping the argument you were rehearsing, whether or not anyone else moves.

Discussion Question

When something goes wrong, which god sits under your reaction, one who must be appeased or the God of love and peace (think of what you assumed the last time a prayer went unanswered)? Where are you being asked to live in peace right now, and what would change if you believed He is the source and not the referee?

Closing Prayer

God of love and peace, we have managed You more often than we have loved You, bargaining like people who thought You had to be kept happy. Forgive us, and show us Your face in Jesus until the bargaining stops. Fill us with the peace that mends the whole person, spirit and soul and body, and put back what this year has knocked loose. Where we are at war with someone, give us the first move, and where a church we love is arguing, help it lay the fight down long enough to notice You standing in the doorway. Be with all of us, the way Paul asked. In Jesus' name, Amen.
