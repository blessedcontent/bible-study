Positive Fruits

Testing the ProphetsLesson 9Day 5 of 7

In brief

A prophet the Bible calls the LORD's servant buys passage on a ship sailing away from where God sent him. Centuries later, an apostle stops eating with Gentile believers because he fears what a certain group will say. Scripture records both failures. Jesus still gives a test for prophets, by their fruit you will recognize them. God's messengers were never flawless, so the fruit test looks at what a life and message produce, not at a spotless record, and it never excuses a life that contradicts the message.

Yesterday

Yesterday, John's test asked whether a voice confesses that Jesus Christ came in the flesh. The rescue was real because His body was real. Today Jesus gives a test that looks at the messenger's life.

Historical Context

The LORD's Servant Sailed the Other Way

Outside his own book, the Old Testament mentions Jonah only once. Second Kings records that King Jeroboam II restored Israel's border, just as the LORD had said through His servant Jonah son of Amittai, the prophet from Gath-hepher (2 Kings 14:25). Scripture does not say whether that word came before or after Nineveh. It remembers Jonah as a prophet whose word from God came true.

When God sent him to Nineveh, a great Assyrian city far to the east, Jonah went down to the port of Joppa instead and boarded a ship for Tarshish. Scripture describes Tarshish as a distant land that traded in silver, iron, tin, and lead (Ezekiel 27:12). No one knows exactly where it was, but many scholars place it in the far west of the Mediterranean, perhaps in Spain. Jonah did not simply stay home. He headed for the far edge of the map he knew.

Into the Word

If God's messengers had to be flawless, the Bible would have very few of them. Its pages show a prophet running, two partners splitting up, and an apostle backing away from a table. Start with the prophet who paid his own fare in the wrong direction.

Even trusted partners clashed. Paul and Barnabas disagreed so sharply about taking John Mark along that they went separate ways (Acts 15:36-39). Luke does not say who was right. Years later, Paul asked for Mark by name because he was useful to him (2 Timothy 4:11). Paul also knew when a friend had to be confronted.

Their flaws were not the whole story. Moses led Israel out of Egypt, and no later prophet in Israel knew the LORD face to face as he did (Deuteronomy 34:10-12). When Haggai spoke, the leaders and people obeyed the voice of the LORD their God (Haggai 1:12). Isaiah told a king that his treasures would be carried off to Babylon (Isaiah 39:5-6). Daniel prayed we have sinned, counting himself among the guilty (Daniel 9:15), and Jeremiah wished his eyes were a fountain of tears for his people (Jeremiah 9:1). Their lives pointed people back to God, and they loved the people they warned. Near the end of the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5-7, chapters 5, 6, and 7), Jesus told His listeners to look for that kind of fruit.

Street Level

The Girl He Yelled At Runs the Club Now

For thirty-one summers, one man ran the county fair's livestock club for kids. He taught them to feed a calf before school, to walk it on a halter until it trusted them, and to stay patient when it would not move. One August a twelve-year-old's steer broke loose in the show ring and dragged her through the wood shavings, and he shouted at her in front of the full bleachers. The man who taught patience had lost his. She cried all the way to the barn. The next morning, before the first animals came into the ring, he took the microphone, said her name, and apologized in front of the same bleachers.

Twenty-five years later the club gives him a dinner when he retires. The room is full of people he taught. A veterinarian. A dairy farmer. A nurse who still wakes at five without an alarm. The woman who runs the club now stands to speak, and she starts with that August. I was the girl he yelled at, she says. I was also there the next morning. Then she points around the room, table by table.

Jesus told His listeners to recognize a tree by its fruit (Matthew 7:16-20). Scripture keeps the record of Jonah's flight and Peter's fear (Jonah 1:1-3; Galatians 2:11-14), and it never calls those days good fruit. One bad August was real, and it was answered in public. But thirty-one summers of fruit filled that room. If his summers had kept producing frightened children, he would have been a different tree, and the club would have been right to send him home, no matter how many ribbons his kids had won. The girl he failed was holding the microphone. She was part of his fruit.

Christ at the center

Every messenger in today's readings needed grace. Isaiah saw Someone coming who would carry what none of them could. Surely He took up our infirmities, He was pierced for our transgressions, and the LORD has laid upon Him the iniquity of us all (Isaiah 53:4-6). Isaiah pictures Him growing up like a tender shoot (Isaiah 53:2) and then cut off from the land of the living (Isaiah 53:8). Jesus is the one Messenger whose life bore no bad fruit, and He did not use that to discard flawed servants. After Peter's worst failure, the risen Lord asked him three times whether he loved Him and gave him sheep to feed (John 21:15-17). The one tree with no rot in it was cut down for the rest of us.

Application

Think of a Christian leader or messenger who disappointed you. Take two minutes to ask what the larger pattern of that life and teaching has produced, and pray for that person by name. If the pattern has caused real harm, stepping back and seeking wise counsel is right.

Discussion Question

How can the fruit test be fair to messengers who stumble without excusing those who keep doing harm? Looking at your own life across a whole year, what fruit would others see (patience, honesty, kindness, or something else)?

Closing Prayer

Father, thank You for telling the truth about Your messengers, their running, their quarrels, and their fear. Thank You that You did not give up on them. Make us honest about our own bad fruit, and keep us from excusing it. Give us clear eyes for the patterns in the voices we follow, and mercy for those who stumble. Grow in us the kind of fruit that points people back to You. In Jesus' name, Amen.
