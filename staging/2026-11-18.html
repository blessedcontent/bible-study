The Meaning of the Text

Interpreting Prophetic WritingsLesson 8Day 5 of 7

In brief

An expert in the law stands up to test Jesus with a question about eternal life. Jesus answers with two questions of His own. What is written in the Law? How do you read it? The man answers well and then asks who his neighbor is. Jesus' answers stretch that word to include enemies. Everyone who reads the Bible interprets it, and a faithful reading lets the text and its parallels set the meaning, even when it reaches further than we hoped.

Yesterday

Yesterday the devil quoted a psalm but left its context behind. Jesus answered by setting Scripture beside Scripture. Today an expert in the law hears a question from Jesus. How do you read it?

Historical Context

He Quoted Words Many Recited Each Morning

The first command the expert quoted comes from a passage that opens, Hear, O Israel (Deuteronomy 6:4-9). Moses told the people to keep these words on their hearts, teach them to their children, and talk about them when they lay down and when they got up. The Mishnah, a body of Jewish teaching written down around AD 200, opens with rules for saying this passage each evening and each morning. The expert was not reaching for an obscure verse. He was quoting words Moses meant for every bedtime and every breakfast.

The second command comes from Leviticus, love your neighbor as yourself (Leviticus 19:18). When another teacher asked Jesus which command came first, Jesus named the same two (Mark 12:28-31). The expert knew the right words. The harder question was how far they reached.

Into the Word

Some people say the Bible needs no interpreting, only obeying. Jesus did not talk that way. When an expert in the law asked Him a question, Jesus answered with two questions, and the second was about interpretation (Luke 10:26).

The expert was not the first to draw a line around the word neighbor. On the hillside in Galilee, Jesus quoted a saying His hearers knew, love your neighbor and hate your enemy. The second half is not in Moses. It is what someone had made the first half mean.

Street Level

She Built the Bed by the Gate

The sign on the community garden's gate says, Every plot goes to a neighbor. In his first spring on the garden committee, a young man asks the woman who started the garden what neighbor means. He is really asking about one name. The man across the street called the city twice to get the garden shut down, and now his name is on the waiting list. Surely a neighbor has to act like one.

She does not argue. The next Saturday the young man finds her by the gate with a stack of lumber and a drill, building the only raised bed in the garden, waist-high. The man across the street cannot kneel anymore. Nobody has to explain the sign to the young man again. He has watched the woman who wrote it decide how far it reaches.

The expert in the law asked Jesus the young man's question. He wanted to know where the word neighbor stopped. Jesus never narrowed it. He told the man a story that stretched it, and on the hillside He pointed to the way His Father treats people who stand against Him and told His hearers to be like their Father. A command is read rightly beside the One who gave it. The young man had asked where neighbor ended. The answer stood by the gate, waist-high, built for the one man he hoped it would leave out.

Christ at the center

Be perfect, as your heavenly Father is perfect. If that is a call to love enemies, it is also a description of what the Father has already done. Paul says that while we were God's enemies, we were reconciled to Him through the death of His Son (Romans 5:10). Sun and rain on the evil and the good were a small picture. The cross is the full one. Jesus did not only teach love for enemies. He lived it, and through Him the Father's mercy reached people who were still on the wrong side. We do not love enemies to earn His favor. We love because He loved us first (1 John 4:19), while we were the enemies.

Application

Take two minutes. Think of someone who is hard for you to love. Read Luke 6:36, then pray for that person by name and ask God to do them good. Praying for someone does not require contact or pretending nothing happened.

Discussion Question

The expert knew the command and still looked for its smallest meaning. Why do we sometimes read the Bible for the least it asks? Where does Jesus' meaning of neighbor stretch you most (a coworker, a relative, or someone who hurt you)?

Closing Prayer

Father, You send sun and rain on the evil and the good, and You loved us while we were still Your enemies. Forgive us for reading Your word looking for the least it asks. Give us honest minds that let Scripture explain Scripture, and humble hearts that accept what it says. Make us merciful as You are merciful, toward the people we find hardest to love. In Jesus' name, Amen.
