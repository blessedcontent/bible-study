Further Thought

Interpreting Prophetic WritingsLesson 8Day 7 of 7

Week in Review

This week asked how to read what God has already said. We read promises in their own world, verses in their own setting, and passages side by side. Each time, the reading led back to the One the Scriptures are about.

  • ☩Sabbath: Interpreting Prophetic Writings. Two discouraged followers had the right Scriptures but only part of the reading, and Jesus explained what all of them said about Him.
  • ☩Sunday: The Bible, Its Own Interpreter. Jesus came to fulfill the Law and the Prophets, and one part of Scripture kept shedding light on another.
  • ☩Monday: The Historical-Cultural Context. Isaiah painted the new creation in his hearers' colors, and Paul's holy kiss meant family.
  • ☩Tuesday: The Literary Context. The devil quoted a psalm without its setting, and Jesus answered by setting Scripture beside Scripture.
  • ☩Wednesday: The Meaning of the Text. Jesus asked an expert how he read the Law, and Luke's merciful showed what Matthew's perfect means.
  • ☩Thursday: Biblical Exposition. Jesus read Isaiah as fulfilled in Himself, and the risen Lord opened His followers' minds.

Historical Context

Every Reader Stood Somewhere

Gather the week's settings. A road out of Jerusalem, in years when some had prayed for a son of David to drive out the nations. A king's court in a region where scholars kept handbooks for reading dreams. Homes in Judah where many knew the grief of burying a young child. A temple whose heights could make a visitor dizzy. Words Moses meant for every bedtime and breakfast. A synagogue built for reading.

Each setting was a particular time and place, and God's word reached people there in words they could grasp. We read from a time and place too, and His word still reaches us there.

What This Week Clarifies

The Setting Never Shrank the Command

On Monday the holy kiss turned out to be about family more than about lips and cheeks. That can raise a worry. If a passage's setting shapes what it means, could we explain away any command we dislike as belonging to another time? What we read this week answers that worry.

The Devil Had the Words Right

On Tuesday the devil quoted a psalm almost word for word. On Wednesday an expert gave Jesus the right answer and then looked for a smaller one. Both had the words. If Scripture can be quoted accurately and still misused, how can we trust our own use of it?

The Lord Opens Ordinary Minds

All week the call was to read with care, paying attention to setting, context, and parallel passages. That can make the Bible feel like a book for experts. On Thursday a traveler even asked how he could understand without a guide. Is careful reading only for scholars?

Still Speaking

She Turned Them Back a Page

The cast of a small-town play sits around folding tables for its first read-through. The play is based on the life of a woman who still lives in town. In the second act, after six weeks of visiting her husband every day at a rehab hospital, her character tells him, I'm not coming tomorrow. The actress playing her, who has never been in a play before, stops. However she reads it, the line sounds cold. Someone says people were tougher back then. The director wonders aloud whether they should cut the line.

From the back row, the woman herself speaks up. She is in her eighties and has been listening with a cup of coffee in her hands. Don't cut it, she says. She does not add anything to the script. She asks them to turn back one page and read what her husband says first. You've been here every day for six weeks. Promise me one day at home. They had all read right past it. The actress reads his line and then hers, and this time her voice catches. Nobody mentions cutting it again.

This week gave us good questions to bring to a hard passage, about its world, the words around it, and the passages beside it. They matter, and turning back a page was one of them. But something else changed that room. The one the script was about was sitting in it. Nobody asked what they could do with the line anymore. They wanted to know what she meant. And the newest actress at the table read it the way it was meant.

The two travelers on the road to Emmaus had read past the hard part too, the part about suffering. The One the Scriptures were about walked beside them and took them back through Moses and all the Prophets (Luke 24:27). He promised His followers He would be with them to the end of the age (Matthew 28:20). So when a line in His word sounds wrong to us, we can leave it in and ask Him to turn us back a page.

Christ at the Center of the Week

On Sabbath, a Stranger on the road explained what all the Scriptures said about Him (Luke 24:27). On Sunday, He said He came to fulfill the Law and the Prophets, and He met the bar He set. On Monday, His resurrection became the first piece of Isaiah's promise that death will be swallowed up, and the reason strangers greet each other as family. On Tuesday, He held to the whole word in the wilderness and would not force His Father's hand, even on the cross. On Wednesday, the Father's love for enemies reached us through Him while we were still the enemies (Romans 5:10). On Thursday, He read Isaiah and said, Today (Luke 4:21). Every way of reading this week ended in the same place. We read carefully, but we do not read alone. The Book is about Someone, and He still walks beside the reader.

Weekend Practice

Before the Sabbath begins, choose one passage you have found hard or have tended to skip. Read it slowly, then read the verses around it. Ask what it meant to its first hearers and what it shows you about Jesus. If you can, talk it over with someone this weekend.

Closing Prayer for the Week

Father, thank You for giving us Your word and for not leaving us alone with it. Forgive us for the times we read only what we expected, bent a verse to get what we wanted, or looked for the smallest meaning we could find. Give us patience to read Your word in its setting, humility to learn from one another, and honest hearts that let it ask what it asks. Open our minds the way Your Son opened His disciples' minds. As we rest this Sabbath, let us meet Him in what we read. In Jesus' name, Amen.
