Role of the Supernatural

Testing the ProphetsLesson 9Day 2 of 7

In brief

An old man stands beside the Tigris after three weeks of mourning. A vision drains his strength and drops him face down, and the men with him run. Then a hand lifts him, and a voice calls him precious. God truly met His prophets in visions. Yet Moses warned that even a sign that comes true can lead people away from God. An overwhelming experience cannot prove that a message came from God, so we test every voice by where it leads.

Yesterday

Yesterday, John's churches stood at the door deciding whom to let in. John told them to test the spirits, and testing turned out to be a way of keeping what is good. Today begins with the hardest kind of voice to test, one that arrives with a sign.

Historical Context

He Stayed Behind When the Exiles Went Home

Daniel dates this vision to the third year of Cyrus king of Persia (Daniel 10:1). Two years earlier, Cyrus had decreed that the exiles could go home and rebuild the temple, and about fifty thousand people made the journey (Ezra 1:1-4; Ezra 2:64-65). Daniel was still in the east. Close to seventy years after he was carried off as a young man, he stood on the bank of the Tigris (Daniel 10:4).

Back in Judah, the builders were already running into trouble. Ezra says local opponents frightened them and hired advisers to frustrate their plans all through the reign of Cyrus (Ezra 4:4-5). Daniel does not tell us why he mourned. He went three weeks without rich food, meat, or wine (Daniel 10:2-3), and he says only that he set his heart to understand and to humble himself before God (Daniel 10:12). The vision came to a praying old man, and it concerned his people's future (Daniel 10:14). It was not a show for a crowd.

Into the Word

When someone says God showed them something, people often watch the person as closely as they listen to the message. Did they tremble? Did they fall? Did something happen that no one can explain? God does speak to His prophets in visions and dreams (Numbers 12:6). The question is what the trembling proves.

Visions run all through Scripture. Daniel dates another one to Belshazzar's third year (Daniel 8:1), and Isaiah's whole book opens by calling itself a vision (Isaiah 1:1). The experiences were not all alike. In Damascus the Lord spoke to a disciple named Ananias in a vision, and he simply answered, Here I am, Lord (Acts 9:10). Long before any of them, Moses had warned that an experience alone cannot tell you who is speaking.

Street Level

Everything the Promoter Promised Came True

The promoter told the kid he could get him a fight on television within six weeks. Six weeks later the kid was on television, and he won in the second round. That night, in a hotel suite twenty floors up, the promoter slid a contract across the table. Bigger fights. A training camp in the mountains. More money than the kid had ever seen in one place. None of it was a bluff. There was one condition. The old man was not coming.

The old man ran a gym above a tire shop. He had let the kid train there for free at fourteen, and for six years he had wrapped his hands, driven him home, and stood in his corner for every fight. The promoter was not unkind about it. The old man got you this far, he said. I can take you the rest of the way. The kid looked at the pen. Bigger fights were not the problem. The condition was. The promoter had kept every promise he made, and none of that could tell the kid whether to leave the one who had taken him in.

Moses told Israel what to do if a prophet's sign came true and he then invited them to follow gods they had never known (Deuteronomy 13:1-5). Nothing about the sign would be fake. What that invitation asked them to give up was the God who had brought them out of slavery. The kid flew home the next morning in a middle seat, two rows behind the old man. The promoter had been right about the fight. He was wrong about the corner.

Christ at the center

Centuries after Daniel, John saw the risen Jesus, His eyes like blazing fire, and fell at His feet as though dead. Jesus laid His right hand on him and told him not to be afraid (Revelation 1:17). Many readers notice how closely John's description echoes Daniel's (Daniel 10:5-6; Revelation 1:13-16). The same presence that put both men on the ground reached down and touched them. Jesus also answers Moses' question. False prophets may come looking like sheep (Matthew 7:15), but a true word leads to the God who redeems, and Jesus did not merely point toward that redemption. He paid for it. In Him, the God who brought His people out of slavery came close enough to lay a hand on us.

Application

Think of one powerful spiritual experience, your own or one you have heard about. Take two minutes and ask Moses' question of it. Did it lead toward the God who redeems, or away from Him? Then thank Him that His word, not the size of an experience, is the measure.

Discussion Question

Why are dramatic experiences so persuasive, even when a sign alone proves nothing? When someone tells you about a powerful encounter with God, what helps you listen kindly and still test it (Scripture, time, or trusted friends)?

Closing Prayer

Lord, You met Daniel by the river and lifted him to his feet. Thank You that You treat Your servants as treasured, even when they are undone. Keep us from being dazzled by what looks powerful, and keep us from despising what You truly give. When a voice comes with signs, turn our eyes to where it leads. Hold our love for You whole, so that no wonder can pull us away from the God who redeemed us. Lift us up to listen. In Jesus' name, Amen.
