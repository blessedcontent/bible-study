Functions of Prophets

Old Testament ProphetsLesson 3Day 4 of 7

In brief

The elders of Israel arrive at an old prophet's door with a reasonable request. Give us a king like everyone else has. Samuel prays, warns them what a king will take, and in the end pours the oil himself. That pattern runs through the whole Old Testament. Prophets installed priests and kings, and then came back to hold them to God's word. Some died for it. They also taught, wrote, and prayed. In Israel nobody outranked the word of God, and the prophet was the living reminder.

Yesterday

Yesterday a king kept four hundred prophets who said yes and hated the one who would not. Micaiah told the truth and went to prison for it. That raises a bigger question. What were prophets for in the first place?

Historical Context

The King Had to Copy the Law by Hand

In the ancient world the king usually sat at the top of the chart. Egypt counted its pharaoh among the gods. Hammurabi of Babylon set his laws on a stone pillar that shows him standing before the god of justice. Kings claimed heaven's backing. Israel put its king's limits in writing.

Israel's law was written before Israel had a king, and it saw one coming. When you ask for a king like the nations around you, Moses said, here are his limits (Deuteronomy 17:14-20). He must not pile up horses, wives, or silver. And when he takes the throne he must write out his own copy of God's law and read it all his life, so that his heart is not lifted above his brothers. Picture a king at a desk, copying line by line the book that outranks him. But who would hold him to it? Not a general. God gave that job to prophets, whose authority was the word He gave them.

Into the Word

Samuel has led Israel for most of his life. Now the elders have come to his home in Ramah. He is old, they say. His sons take bribes. It is time for a modern government. Behind the request sits a question. Who has the last word over God's people?

Soon Samuel himself will pour oil on the first king's head (1 Samuel 10:1). Centuries earlier, Israel's first priest had received his office the same way.

Kings heard it too, and some hit back. Jezebel slaughtered the LORD's prophets while an official named Obadiah hid a hundred in caves, and Ahab called Elijah the troubler of Israel (1 Kings 18:4-19).

Prophets did more than confront. When a lost scroll of the law turned up in King Josiah's day, his men carried it to a prophet, and her answer was blunt. Disaster is coming, because they have forsaken Me (2 Kings 22:15-17). The king listened, and reform reached the whole nation (2 Chronicles 34). Prophets also graded the other teachers. Jeremiah said the prophets lied, the priests ruled on their own authority, and the people loved it (Jeremiah 5:31). Ezekiel said the priests had blurred the line between holy and common (Ezekiel 22:26).

They looked past Israel's borders. Isaiah saw nations walking toward Zion's light (Isaiah 60:1-3), and he would not keep quiet until they saw her made right (Isaiah 62:1-2). Some wrote history. Chronicles names the prophet Iddo as a source (2 Chronicles 13:22). Under all of it lay something quieter.

Street Level

The Cook Brought Him the Founders' Page

Imagine a summer camp with one odd tradition. On opening night the cook, who has run the kitchen for thirty years, hands the new director a whistle and a single framed page. The family that founded the camp wrote it, and they still own the place. One line says any child who wants to come may come, whether the family can pay or not. The cook holds no rank, and she has turned down the director's job twice. She does the handing over because the family asked her to.

This director is good, and the camp fills. By his third summer the lake cabins are going to the families who give the most, and the scholarship kids keep getting moved to next year. Nobody complains. The numbers have never looked better. One August night the cook knocks on his office door with the framed page. He can fire her, and they both know it. She reads him the line. He asks who made her the boss. Nobody, she says. I only handed you the whistle. It was never mine to give, and it is not yours to use any way you like. We both answer to this page.

That is where the man of God stood with Eli, and Zechariah with Joash. The oil came through a prophet's hands, so a prophet could come back with the word, and the word was all he had. Notice what the cook did not do. She did not grade his every decision or make herself his critic. She carried one page she did not write to the man it was written for. What he did next was up to him. Kings went both ways.

Today’s World

He Would Not Put the Numbers to a Vote

In August 2010, during Greece's debt crisis, Andreas Georgiou took charge of the country's statistics office. The post is meant to stand apart from politics. Europe's code of practice makes the head of a statistics office solely responsible for its methods and its releases. That way no government grades its own report card. That November Georgiou sent Europe's statistics agency corrected figures for Greece's deficit and debt. He did not first put them to a vote of his office's board. Under that code, official figures are not settled by a vote.

Europe's agency accepted his figures and kept affirming them. At home a criminal investigation opened in September 2011. The claim that he had inflated the deficit ended in 2019 with no conviction. The charge that stuck was breach of duty, for skipping the board vote. He was acquitted in 2016, then convicted on appeal in 2017. In 2023 the European Court of Human Rights ruled that Greece had denied him a fair trial. In August 2026 Greece's Supreme Court annulled the conviction. It had taken fifteen years.

No statistics office speaks for God. The case shows what such a post can cost. The numbers were never Georgiou's to bargain with, and they were not the board's either. Israel's prophets held a post like that, with a far weightier word.

Christ at the center

The title Messiah means Anointed One, and so does the title Christ. In Israel's story the anointed callings usually rested on different people. Kings ruled, priests offered sacrifice, and prophets spoke God's word to both. Jesus holds all three. He is the Prophet who speaks God's word, the Priest who offered Himself, and the King who came to give His life and not to take (Mark 10:45). He was anointed with the Holy Spirit and not with oil (Acts 10:38). He also knew what the prophets' work cost. He spoke of the prophets' blood, from Abel to a Zechariah killed beside the altar (Luke 11:50-51), most likely this man. Then He joined that line. Zechariah died asking God to call his killers to account. Jesus died asking the Father to forgive His (Luke 23:34).

Application

Take two minutes and ask one question. Who is allowed to tell me no? Name one person who has your permission, and God's, to say a hard thing to you. If no name comes, ask God to send someone, and ask Him to make you able to listen.

Discussion Question

Israel asked for a king like all the other nations and was warned what he would take. Where do you feel the pull to be like everyone around you (money, schedule, screens)? Who in your life could say what Samuel said?

Closing Prayer

Lord, You are our King, and we admit that we often want a different one. We want what the people around us have, and we do not count the cost. Thank You for sending voices that told the truth to thrones and altars, and for the ones who paid for it with their lives. Keep Your church from putting any leader above Your word. Give those who lead us humble hearts, and give us humble hearts when You correct us. Teach us to pray for people before we speak to them. In Jesus' name, Amen.
