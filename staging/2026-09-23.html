The Fellowship of the Holy Spirit

Grace, Love, and FellowshipLesson 13Day 5 of 7

In brief

In a Corinthian lecture hall a philosopher explains that spirit is a fiery breath running through everything. Across town a letter says the Spirit searches the mind of God, teaches, reminds, testifies, decides who gets what, and cries Father from inside a person's chest. One of those is a force. The other is Someone. Today follows the verbs Paul and Jesus give the Holy Spirit and asks what fellowship with Him means. The Spirit is not a power you use. He is a Person you keep company with, and that company is where every other fellowship begins.

Yesterday

Yesterday Paul gave God a title found nowhere else in Scripture, the God of love and peace, and every title on his list turned out to be something God hands over. The last name in the blessing is still waiting. Today we ask who the Holy Spirit is, and what it means to have fellowship with Him.

Historical Context

The Philosophers Said Spirit Was a Kind of Fire

If you had asked an educated Corinthian what spirit was, he would not have looked puzzled. He would likely have given you the Stoic answer, since Stoicism was one of the most influential philosophies of Paul's day. Spirit was a fine, fiery breath, a blend of air and fire that ran through every stone, tree, and body in the universe. It held things together the way tension holds a bowstring, and it was as physical as the string. The Stoics could call that breath divine, even reason itself, and they urged people to live in step with it. But it was a principle woven into the world, not a Person you could meet. You could reason about it. Nobody expected it to know your name.

Paul used that same everyday word, the one that also meant breath and wind, and then gave it verbs no Stoic would have used. This Spirit searches. He teaches. He reminds. He testifies. He decides. He can be grieved (Ephesians 4:30), and He calls God Father from inside a person. It is easy to keep the philosophers' version, a divine energy you tap into, which is why it matters that the letters give the Spirit verbs only a someone can do. Nobody has fellowship with a bowstring. You can only keep company with a Someone, and Paul closes his letter by asking that this Someone be with all of them.

Into the Word

Paul stacks up four ifs in a letter to the Philippians, and he does not doubt one of them. Any encouragement in Christ, any comfort from His love, any fellowship with the Spirit (Philippians 2:1), any affection and compassion. Look at the company that third item keeps. Every other item in the list is something that happens between persons. So the question today is simple to ask. Is the Holy Spirit a something or a Someone, and what changes if you can keep company with Him?

Paul learned to talk about the Spirit this way from Jesus, who described Him to the disciples on the night before He died.

Street Level

The Monitor Never Said Her Name

Picture a hospital room in the middle of the night. A woman is hours into labor and losing the thread. A monitor beside the bed records the baby's heartbeat and each contraction and draws them across a screen as two lines. The information is real. The nurses and the doctor read those lines, and what they read shapes what they decide. The screen itself decides nothing and knows nothing. It has never once said her name.

There is also a doula in the chair by the bed, hired to stay. When the next contraction comes she does not look at the screen. She looks at the mother's face and says, low, you are doing this, one at a time. She suggests trying a new position and helps her turn. She hands the husband, frozen by the window, a cup and tells him to count out loud, and suddenly the room has one purpose instead of two frightened people and a machine. Nobody hired her for strength. They hired her for company.

Paul lists fellowship with the Spirit beside encouragement in Christ and comfort from His love. The Spirit searches, teaches, reminds, and testifies that we are God's children. His power is real. But you were not handed a screen to read. You were given Someone who knows the mind of God and knows your name, and the one purpose Paul wanted for the church is what His company does to a room.

Today’s World

Fifteen Years With the Same Doctor

Norway gives its residents a named family doctor, which let researchers ask whether it matters to keep the same doctor for years. In a 2022 study, Hogne Sandvik and colleagues followed 4.5 million people through 2018, sorted by how long each had kept the same doctor. Compared with people in their first year with a doctor, those past fifteen years with the same one had 28 percent lower odds of an emergency hospital admission and 25 percent lower odds of dying that year. The longer the relationship, the lower the numbers.

The same national system served everyone. What differed was how long each person had stayed with the same doctor. The study is observational, so it shows an association, not proof of cause, and it does not show that the doctor's personal knowledge made the difference. Still, the pattern suggests that ongoing care from the same person may matter in ways that access to a system alone does not capture. That is the difference Paul had in view when he set fellowship with the Spirit beside encouragement and comfort.

Source British Journal of General Practice

Continuity in general practice as predictor of mortality, acute hospitalisation, and use of out-of-hours care: a registry-based observational study in Norway. British Journal of General Practice, 2022. Read the source.

Christ at the center

Every verb the Spirit has points at one Person. He testifies about Jesus. He reminds the disciples of what Jesus said. He cries Father with the word Jesus taught. So the fellowship of the Spirit is not a separate department from the grace of Jesus. It is Jesus keeping His promise not to leave them as orphans (John 14:18). When Paul reminds Corinth what happened to them, he puts the two names together, washed, sanctified, justified, in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God (1 Corinthians 6:11). The church itself is a letter from Christ written with the Spirit of the living God on human hearts (2 Corinthians 3:3). The Author and the ink are not rivals. The One who was rich and became poor for you now lives in you by His Spirit, closer than He was to the disciples who could touch Him.

Application

Speak to the Holy Spirit today as a Someone, once, on purpose. Not a request for power. A sentence of company, the kind you would say to a friend who walked into the room, then two minutes of quiet with your phone face down. Before the day ends, notice one place where you and another believer are not like-minded, and pray for that person by name instead of rehearsing your side.

Discussion Question

Have you been treating the Holy Spirit as a Someone or a something (think about the last time you mentioned Him at all, and what you wanted)? If fellowship among believers is what fellowship with Him produces, which strained relationship in your church or family might change if you started with Him instead of with them?

Closing Prayer

Holy Spirit, we confess we have talked about You more than we have talked to You, and we have reached for You like a power source when You came to us as a Person. Forgive us. Testify to our spirits again today that we are God's children, and teach us the things about Him we could never have found alone. Search us, since You already know us. Then do what Paul prayed for. Let our fellowship with You spill over into one love and one purpose with the people around us, especially the ones we have been avoiding. Stay with us, the way Jesus promised You would. In Jesus' name, Amen.
