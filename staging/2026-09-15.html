False Teachers Identified

Dealing With False TeachersLesson 12Day 4 of 7

In brief

A father has promised his daughter to one husband, and now smooth strangers are in the house talking her out of him. That is how Paul describes Corinth. The newcomers preach a different Jesus, take a fee for it, and wear the costume of apostles so well that the church cannot see the difference. Jesus had already given the test on a hillside. Wolves dress as sheep, so stop grading the wool and watch the fruit. A costume can change in a minute. Fruit takes a season, and the season always comes.

Yesterday

Yesterday the newcomers held up their tape measure and Paul took it away. A ruler that only measures you against you cannot measure anything, and the only report card that counts is the Lord's commendation inside the field He assigned. Today Paul stops talking about their measuring habits and says plainly who they are.

Historical Context

The Serpent Had Worn a Costume Before

When Paul writes that Satan disguises himself as an angel of light, he says it as if everyone already knows. Many of his readers did. A Jewish retelling of the Eden story that circulated in Paul's day describes the devil coming back for Eve a second time, this time dressed in the shining appearance of an angel, so that she would trust him before he opened his mouth. Whether or not the Corinthians had heard that exact tale, the point behind it was common knowledge in the synagogue. The serpent's power was never his fangs. It was his wardrobe. He got into the garden by looking like he belonged there, and he got Eve talking by sounding reasonable.

Corinth understood costumes from the other direction, too. The city's theater held thousands, and the crowd knew that the same actor could play a king in one scene and a slave in the next by changing his mask and robe between entrances. Nobody in the seats mistook the mask for the man. That is what makes Paul's charge in chapter 11 so sharp. The newcomers had changed into apostles' clothing, and the church, which could spot a costume change from the back row of the theater, could not spot this one from the front pew. Today's passages hand over the only test that works when the wardrobe is perfect.

Into the Word

Long before Corinth, Jesus sat on a hillside and warned a crowd about wolves that would come dressed as sheep. Paul repeated it to a young pastor (1 Timothy 6:3-5), and Peter promised that false teachers would slip in quietly, with greed underneath (2 Peter 2:1-3). So the problem Paul faces in 2 Corinthians 11:1-15 was not new, and neither is ours. If the costume is convincing and the voice sounds right, how does an ordinary believer tell?

Paul takes that test into Corinth, but he does not sound like an inspector. He sounds like a father who has just found strangers in the house talking his daughter out of her engagement.

So why did a smart church fall for it? These false apostles (2 Corinthians 11:13) charged for their teaching, and Paul had refused to, which they read as proof he was second rate (2 Corinthians 11:7-12). He calls it proof of love (2 Corinthians 11:11). And the different gospel (2 Corinthians 11:4) still used the name of Jesus. Paul had watched that happen before, in Galatia.

Street Level

The Shelf Was a Photograph of a Tree

The bookshelf in your first apartment looked like oak. It said oak on the box. It had grain, knots, the warm color, the whole wardrobe of a real tree, and it took an hour and a lost screw to put together. Here is what it actually was. Pressed sawdust wrapped in a thin printed sheet, a photograph of wood glued onto something that is not wood. The print is good. That is the point of the print. Nobody manufactures a fake that fails on sight. The whole craft goes into the surface, because the surface is the only part that has to convince you, and it only has to convince you on the day you carry it home.

Two things give it away, and neither one is the look. The first is comparison. Set it beside a real oak board and start hunting for the knot. On the fake you will find the same knot again a shelf or two down, because a print has a repeat and a tree never does. The second is time. Load the shelves with real books and wait two winters. The middle bows. Spill one glass of water and the corner swells and never goes back down. What it was made of was there from the first day. It just took weight and a couple of years to show. The tree was never in it.

That is why Paul refused to grade the newcomers on their finish. A counterfeit apostle is built to pass the eye. So he handed the church the only two tests a good surface cannot beat. Hold the teaching against the gospel you first received and look for the repeat. Then watch what it does to people under weight and over time. The Jesus inside either matches the original or He does not, and the fruit will say so.

Today’s World

The Lot Number Was Real and the Pen Was Not

Three times in two years, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration has warned patients about counterfeit Ozempic pens found in the American drug supply. The latest alert came on December 5, 2025, after Novo Nordisk, the drug's maker, reported that dozens of fake units had been distributed outside the company's authorized channels and had entered the legitimate U.S. drug supply. The pens were built to pass as the real thing. The only visible tell the FDA could describe was two small words, EXP/LOT, printed beside the date instead of above it. Testing of what was inside the pens was still underway when the warning went out.

The April 2025 batch is the one worth remembering. Several hundred counterfeit pens carried lot number PAR0362, and that number was genuine. It belonged to a real production run. What gave the fakes away was the serial number printed with it, a string beginning 51746517 that the company identified as illegitimate. The packaging could not settle the question. The pairing had to be checked against the manufacturer, which is why both the company and the FDA told anyone unsure to call Novo Nordisk before using the pen. Novo Nordisk said plainly that it could not confirm the contents or quality of the counterfeit product, and the FDA seized the units.

These alerts are about medicine, not doctrine, and the FDA does not grade sermons. But the pattern is the one Paul warned Corinth about. A counterfeit that borrows a genuine name is the hardest kind to catch, and it is caught the way the pens were, by checking it against the record of what was truly issued and refusing to trust what cannot be verified. Paul handed the church its record. It was the gospel that had reached them first.

Christ at the center

Set the two of them side by side. Satan changes his outward form into light so that he can lead people away from God. Jesus, who is the Light, took on real flesh so that He could lead people home, and it was no costume. He was born, He got tired, He bled, and He died, and none of it came off between scenes. That is the difference between a masquerade and an incarnation. One puts on glory to hide what it is. The other laid glory down to show what God is. So when Paul begs Corinth to stay devoted to the Jesus he preached, he is not defending a brand. He is defending the only Bridegroom who ever paid for His bride with His own life, who loved the church and gave Himself up for her to present her to Himself without spot or wrinkle (Ephesians 5:25-27). Check His fruit. It is a cross and an empty tomb. No counterfeit has ever produced either.

Application

Take out the paper from Sabbath, the sentence you wrote about the Jesus your favorite voice presents. Hold it up to three questions today and write your answers underneath. Does this Jesus need a cross, or does He just need my improvement? Does He ever ask something of me that costs me? After a year of listening, am I more devoted to Christ or more devoted to the teacher? If any answer makes you wince, do not throw the voice out yet. Just start watching the fruit on purpose.

Discussion Question

Think of the teacher, book, or channel you would hand to a new believer. What fruit has it produced in your life this past year (more patience, more honesty, more Scripture, or mostly more opinions)? If its Jesus began to drift into a slightly different Jesus, would you notice, and what one habit could you build so that you would?

Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, You promised us to Yourself, and we confess that we have been an easy bride to talk to. We have listened to voices because they were confident, followed them because they were popular, and never asked what they were doing to our devotion to You. Keep us simple. Keep us pure. Give us the patience of the fruit test, the willingness to watch what a teaching grows in a life before we swallow it, and give us the courage to walk away from a different Jesus no matter how bright the costume. Protect the people in our churches who are young in the faith and cannot yet tell wool from a wolf, and make us the kind of believers who help them see. Above all, keep our eyes on the real You, the One who bled for us and rose for us, until no imitation can hold our attention. In Jesus' name, Amen.
