Jesus as Prophet

New Testament ProphetsLesson 4Day 2 of 7

In brief

Jesus is on the road near Caesarea Philippi when He asks His disciples what people are saying about Him. The answers are flattering. John the Baptist. Elijah. Jeremiah. One of the prophets. Then He asks what they say, and Peter goes past every name on the list. John's Gospel goes further still. Prophets carried God's word. This One is the Word, who was with God and was God. Jesus is the Prophet Moses promised, and He is more than any prophet. The others brought a message. He is the message.

Yesterday

Yesterday we saw altar stones put in storage until a prophet should come. Then God sent His Son and poured out gifts on a whole church. Many gifts, one Giver. Today starts with the One every prophet was waiting for.

Historical Context

The Delegation Had a Checklist

When John the Baptist began drawing crowds, priests and Levites came out from Jerusalem with three questions. Are you the Christ? Are you Elijah? Are you the Prophet? John said no to all three (John 1:19-21). Notice that the list has three separate lines. The Christ was the promised king from David's family. Elijah was expected back because Malachi had said so. And the Prophet was the one Moses had promised. The questioners did not treat these as one person.

A rule book found among the Dead Sea Scrolls shows the same habit. It was copied about a century before Jesus by a Jewish community near the Dead Sea, and it looks ahead to the coming of the prophet and the messiahs of Aaron and Israel. As most scholars read it, that makes three figures, a prophet, a priestly leader, and a royal one. So when the crowds in today's passage reach for prophet names to explain Jesus, they are not being careless. They are looking for His line on the list. The surprise is that He will not fit on one line.

Into the Word

A crowd of thousands has just eaten its fill of bread on a hillside by the Sea of Galilee. When they see the sign, they say, truly this is the Prophet who is to come into the world (John 6:14). By the next day they are talking about Moses and the manna (John 6:31). They had a promise in mind.

But is Prophet the whole truth about Him? Jesus raised that question Himself, away from the crowds.

Even after the cross, two disciples on the road to Emmaus still described Him as a prophet, powerful in speech and action. The stranger walking beside them began with Moses and all the Prophets and explained what was written about Himself (Luke 24:13-35). The prophets were not His equals. They were His witnesses. John's Gospel opens by saying how much more He is.

John adds that in Him was life, and that life was the light of men (John 1:4). The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it (John 1:4-5). Peter uses the same picture of light when he explains what the prophets were for.

Street Level

The New Violinist Had Written the Music

A community orchestra is three weeks from its spring concert. The main piece on the program is new, written by a composer nobody in the room has met. On Tuesday night a stranger takes the empty chair at the back of the second violins. He tunes, nods to his stand partner, and plays. He is good. At the break the guessing starts around the coffee pot. Maybe he teaches music at the college. Maybe the board hired a professional to steady the section. One of the older players says he reminds her of the man who led the violins here thirty years ago. Every guess is a compliment. Nobody in the room knows if any of them is right. But they all agree on one thing, and that much is true. He is a violinist. He is holding a violin.

After the break the conductor stops at a hard passage, looks to the back row, and asks a question. What did you want here? The room goes quiet. The man in the last chair wrote every note on every stand. All evening the others have been passing along music that reached them on paper. He has been playing what came out of him.

That is the distance between the crowd's answer and Peter's. The prophets were given God's word and passed it on faithfully, and the printed parts on those stands are still good and still needed. To call Jesus a prophet is no insult, any more than it insulted that man to call him a violinist. It only stops too soon. The others played what they were handed. He is where the music came from.

Today’s World

The Catalogue Called It a Copy

In 1946 the library at Harvard Law School bought an old sheet of parchment from a London book dealer. The price was $27.50. The auction catalogue called it a copy of Magna Carta made in 1327, somewhat rubbed and damp-stained. That label was not foolish. Handwritten copies of the great charter turn up in medieval chronicles and law books, and this one was stained and faded. It was filed as a copy, and for almost eighty years that is what it was called.

Then David Carpenter, a historian at King's College London, was looking through the library's scanned manuscripts online. He thought this one might be more than a copy. He and Nicholas Vincent of the University of East Anglia checked its size and its handwriting. Ultraviolet light and special imaging brought out the faded text. Then they compared the wording with the six known originals that King Edward I sent out in 1300. It matched. In May 2025 Harvard announced that its copy is the seventh.

Nothing about the parchment changed that day. Only the label did. The catalogue said copy, and that word did not name what the sheet was, an original sent out by a king. A sheet of parchment can tell us nothing about who Jesus is. It only shows that a label can sound sensible and still miss what is in front of it. The crowd's names for Jesus did that.

Christ at the center

A prophet like Moses. Hold the two side by side. Moses went up the mountain and came down with God's words on stone. Jesus came from the Father's side, and He is God's Word made flesh (John 1:14). When Israel sinned with the golden calf, Moses offered to be blotted out of God's book in their place (Exodus 32:32). God did not accept the offer. Moses could plead for sinners, but he could not carry their sin. Now remember who John says Jesus is. The One through whom all things were made (John 1:1-3) let Himself be nailed to wood that He had made. What Moses could only offer, the Maker did. That is how far the love of God goes. The Prophet did not only bring God's message to us. He brought us back to God.

Application

Take two minutes with the question Jesus asked. But what about you? Who do you say I am? Answer Him in your own words, out loud or on paper. Try to get past the titles you have always used and say who He is to you today. There is no wrong place to start.

Discussion Question

The crowds called Jesus a prophet, which was true and still too small. Which true but too small picture of Jesus is easiest for you to settle for (teacher, helper, example)? What would change if you listened to Him as the Word who made you?

Closing Prayer

Father, thank You for every prophet who carried Your word through the dark. Thank You most for sending the Word Himself. Forgive us when the names we give Your Son are too small. Lord Jesus, You are the Christ, the Son of the living God. You made us, and You died for us. Teach us to listen to You in everything You tell us. Shine in the places where we are still in the dark. Keep Your lamp lit in our hands until the morning star rises in our hearts. In Jesus' name, Amen.
