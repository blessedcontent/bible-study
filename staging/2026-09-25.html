Further Thought

Grace, Love, and FellowshipLesson 13Day 7 of 7

Week in Review

A letter that argued for thirteen chapters ended with a blessing, and this week took it apart. Grace was a trade, love was a sending, fellowship was a Person, and all three came from one God in three Persons.

  • ☩Sabbath: Grace, Love, and Fellowship. Where Greek letters often said be strong, Paul wrote three names, and the last word to a divided church was a gift.
  • ☩Sunday: The Grace of Jesus. Grace opens and closes Paul's surviving letters because it is a trade, the rich One made poor for your sake.
  • ☩Monday: The Love of God. God is love, and the proof is a verb. He sent His Son while we were enemies, and nothing can pry that love loose.
  • ☩Tuesday: The God of Love. A title found nowhere else in Scripture, and every title on Paul's list named something God is and hands over.
  • ☩Wednesday: The Fellowship of the Holy Spirit. The Spirit searches, teaches, testifies, and decides, a Someone whose company spills over into fellowship with each other.
  • ☩Thursday: Our Triune God. Grace, love, and fellowship come from Father, Son, and Spirit together, in one rescue told in three verses.

Historical Context

A City With a Title for Every God Heard One Name

Put the week's scenes side by side and a portrait of Corinth forms. Letters that ended with be strong. A theater weeping over a queen who died for her husband. A road to Isthmia full of people managing Poseidon, a shrine to Aphrodite on the rock, an altar to Caesar's peace in Rome. Lecture halls where spirit meant a fiery breath woven through the world. It was a city fluent in gods, and the gods it knew came with departments, prices, and tempers. Few people expected a god to want their company.

Into that city came a Jewish tentmaker who confessed with his people that the Lord is one. The last sentence of his surviving letters to them named three, and the three were not departments. They were one God, three Persons, handing over grace, love, and company to people who had given Him every reason not to. Corinth had a title for everything it feared. Paul gave it a name it could be with.

What This Week Clarifies

The Doorway Was Never Locked

Sunday put grace before everything, the first word to a church still sued, scandalized, and charmed by rivals. Then Tuesday read a sentence with strings on it. Live in peace, and the God of love and peace will be with you. A reader could be forgiven for hearing a condition. Is His presence a gift that comes first, or a reward that comes after we behave?

The Ought Came After the Gift

Monday insisted that love was finished business. A Son was sent while we were enemies, and nothing can break the bond. Then the same verses said we ought to love one another. Wednesday expected fellowship with the Spirit to produce like-minded people. If the love is done and cannot be lost, where does all this ought come from? Is it the old scorekeeping sneaking back in?

You Are Not Praying to a Crowd

Thursday pressed a question the whole week had been circling. Paul confessed that the Lord is one and then blessed people in three names. A reader can hold both and still feel a practical worry. If there are three, whom do I go to? Is grace with the Son, love with the Father, company with the Spirit, and am I supposed to keep them straight?

Still Speaking

She Said It at the End of Every Call

Think of a man in his forties who still calls his mother on Sunday nights. Some calls were easy. Some he sat in the driveway ten minutes before dialing, because he had to tell her about the job he lost, or the marriage that was ending, or the thing he could not explain. She has never been soft in the middle of a call. She asks the question he was hoping she would not ask. More than once she has told him he was wrong. But every call has ended the same way. Love you. Be careful. Call me when you get there.

He did not notice how much those words were carrying until the calls stopped and he caught himself saying them to his own kids at the end of every call. The sign-off was never a report card. It was the last thing she had for him whatever the call had held, and it is the word he walked out of the house under, every time.

Paul closes his surviving correspondence with Corinth the way she closes a call. Thirteen chapters of hard questions, and then, where the reader braces for the verdict, the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, the love of God, and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit, with all of you (2 Corinthians 13:14). Not because the hard middle did not matter. Because the last word was never going to be the verdict. It comes from one God in three Persons, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and the reader is not asked to work out which of them is speaking before he can receive it. He is asked to walk out under it.

That is what this week ends with. Not a grade. A blessing said over people who had given every reason not to say it, and said last, so it would be the thing they carried out the door. The people who walk out under it are the ones who find themselves, years on, saying it in the same cadence to somebody else.

Christ at the Center of the Week

Paul named Jesus first in the blessing, and every day this week explained why. Sabbath asked what it says about God that He introduces Himself through a crucified Son. Sunday answered with a trade, the rich One made poor for your sake, and found grace was a report about Him before it was a comment about you. Monday found that the love of God had a verb, and the verb had a face, a one-of-a-kind Son sent into a world that had earned condemnation and got rescue. Tuesday found that the God of peace made peace with His own blood rather than billing the people who broke it. Wednesday found that every verb the Spirit has points back at Jesus. Thursday stood at the cross and saw all three there, the Father giving, the Son offering Himself, the Spirit named in Hebrews' account of that offering, nobody standing back. The blessing is not a summary of three doctrines. It is what happened to people who met Jesus and could not describe God any smaller afterward. The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ is the door. Walk through it and the love of God and the fellowship of the Spirit are already in the room.

Weekend Practice

Before Sabbath ends, say the blessing over yourself once, out loud and slowly. Put your own name in place of all of you. Then sit for two quiet minutes with whichever of the three names you needed most this week. That is the whole practice. If you want more, choose one person still in the middle of a fight. Write the blessing over them by name, without adding a single instruction to it.

Closing Prayer for the Week

Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, one God, we came into this week braced for a verdict and You gave us a blessing. Thank You for grace that came first, for love that had a verb, and for company that will not leave. Forgive us for handing You out in pieces and for treating Your nearness as something we had to keep up. Where we are still at war with someone, help us put the fight down long enough to see You in the doorway. Let the love poured into us run through to the people we have been avoiding, and teach us to pray without wondering which of You is listening. Send us out under the last words of Paul's letters to Corinth, and let them be with all of us. In Jesus' name, Amen.
