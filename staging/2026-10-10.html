Old Testament Prophets

Old Testament ProphetsLesson 3Day 1 of 7

In brief

At dawn a small army walks out of Jerusalem toward a force it cannot beat, with the choir in front. Their king stops them and asks for one thing. Believe God, and believe the prophets He sends. This week meets them. A worn-out man under a desert bush. One voice against four hundred. A woman with a tambourine, a judge under a palm tree, a wife in a new part of town. God's prophets were ordinary, breakable people. What made them worth believing was the One who sent them.

Last week

Last week ended in a church basement, where a man fed in March carried out plates Someone else cooked. God's question went to the whole room. This week looks closer at those who carried His words before Christ. What were they like? How could anyone spot a fake?

Historical Context

The Choir Marched in Front

Word reaches Jerusalem that a huge army from Moab, Ammon, and Mount Seir is already at En-gedi, on the near shore of the Dead Sea (2 Chronicles 20:1-2). King Jehoshaphat is afraid, and he says so. He calls the whole nation to fast. He stands in the temple court with the men, their wives, and their little ones, and he ends his prayer with a sentence no general wants to say. We do not know what to do, but our eyes are on You (2 Chronicles 20:12). Then the Spirit of the LORD comes on one man. Jahaziel is a Levite from the family of temple singers, and we never hear of him again. He tells them where the enemy will be tomorrow, and he tells them the battle belongs to God (2 Chronicles 20:14-17).

The next morning the king does what no soldier would advise. He puts the singers ahead of the troops (2 Chronicles 20:21). This king has a history with prophets. Earlier in his reign he sat beside King Ahab, heard a true prophet warn them, rode into the battle anyway, and nearly died (1 Kings 22:29-33). We will visit that scene twice this week. On this morning, with nothing to go on but a singer's message, he stakes every life in the column on it.

This Week’s Memory Text

The column is heading for the Wilderness of Tekoa. Nobody knows yet how the day will end. The king stands up to speak, and he does not give a battle plan.

This Week’s Road Map

  • ☩Sunday: The Humanity of Prophets. The man who called down fire sits under a bush and asks to die, and God answers with bread, sleep, and a quiet voice.
  • ☩Monday: Identifying True Prophets. One prophet tells a king the truth he hates, and a servant on a donkey hunt knows where to find a man whose words come true.
  • ☩Tuesday: Functions of Prophets. Prophets pour the oil on priests and kings, and then they hold them to account, teach the people, write, and pray.
  • ☩Wednesday: Women Prophets. Miriam sings, Deborah judges, and a king's officials carry a lost scroll to Huldah's door.
  • ☩Thursday: False Prophets. In a drought the popular prophets promise peace, and God says He never sent them.
  • ☩Friday: Further Thought. The week's frail and faithful voices are gathered into one picture, and we ask how to listen well.

Christ at the center

Every true prophet this week has a crack somewhere. Elijah runs. Miriam turns on her brother. Samuel's own sons go wrong. God speaks through them anyway, and the message is not spoiled by the messenger. Still, the cracks raise a longing. Moses had promised that God would one day raise up a Prophet like him from among the people (Deuteronomy 18:15). When Jesus began to teach and heal, that was the category people reached for. A great prophet has appeared among us, they said (Luke 7:16). A woman at a well said she could see He was a prophet (John 4:19). So carry a question through the week. What would a prophet look like with no crack in Him at all? One who never runs, never flatters a king, and never says more or less than the Father gave Him to say (John 12:49)? And what would a world that prefers the four hundred do to Him?

Application

Take two quiet minutes today. Read 2 Chronicles 20:20 slowly, once. Then ask yourself one question. The last time I was afraid, whose voice did I reach for first? Tell God the honest answer. There is nothing to fix today. Just let Him hear it.

Opening Question for the Week

Jehoshaphat asked frightened people to believe a prophet's word before they had seen any result. What makes a voice trustworthy to you when you are afraid (think of whose advice you reach for first)? How would you tell a God-sent messenger from a confident one?

Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, our eyes are on You. Some of us come to this week the way Judah came to that morning, outnumbered and unsure what to do. Thank You that You do not leave Your people guessing. You spoke through tired men and overlooked women, and You still speak. Open these stories to us. Let us see the people You sent as they really were, and let their weakness give us hope. Teach us to know Your voice from the ones that only flatter. Show us the Prophet they were all waiting for. In Jesus' name, Amen.
