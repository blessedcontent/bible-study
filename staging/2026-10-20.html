Functions of New Testament Prophets

New Testament ProphetsLesson 4Day 4 of 7

In brief

Two men walk into Antioch carrying a letter. The church there has been through a sharp dispute over whether Gentile believers must be circumcised, and the letter from Jerusalem settles it. The carriers are Judas and Silas, and Luke mentions that they are prophets. What do prophets do at a moment like that? They stay. They talk. They encourage and strengthen. New Testament prophets spoke to build people up, encourage them, and comfort them, so that Christ's whole body could grow up into Him.

Yesterday

Yesterday Paul showed us what lies under God's household. The apostles and prophets are the foundation, and Christ Jesus is the cornerstone. That tells us where prophets belong. Today asks what they did when the church gathered.

Historical Context

The Letter Came With Two Men Attached

The Roman Empire had a postal service, but it carried government mail. Everyone else sent letters with whoever was going that way, a friend, a slave, a merchant. So a carrier was often more than a courier. He might read the letter aloud, answer questions about it, and vouch that it was genuine. The council in Jerusalem used that custom. When the apostles and elders wrote their decision about Gentile believers, they did not simply hand it to Paul and Barnabas, who had been part of the dispute. They chose two leading men of their own, Judas called Barsabbas and Silas, and the letter itself says why. They will tell you the same things by word of mouth (Acts 15:22-27).

Picture the reading in Antioch. The letter is short, and the people rejoice at its encouraging message (Acts 15:30-31). Then the two carriers begin to speak, and only now does Luke mention that they are prophets. The written decision came with living voices to explain it and press it home. That is one of our clearest glimpses of New Testament prophets at work.

Into the Word

What is prophecy for? Many people would say it means predicting the future. The church in Corinth seems to have been dazzled by the more spectacular gifts. Paul gives them one plain sentence about what prophecy does.

Paul sets prophecy inside a larger picture. It is one gift among many (1 Corinthians 12:10), in a church he compares to a body where every part is needed (1 Corinthians 12). God has appointed first apostles, second prophets, third teachers, and then a long list of others (1 Corinthians 12:27-28). In Ephesians he says where these gifts come from and what they are for.

Luke shows us two prophets doing exactly this.

The last page of the Bible adds one more thing prophets were given.

Street Level

He Never Picked Up the Pencil

A woman of forty-one goes back to community college, and algebra is about to end the whole plan. She has failed the first two quizzes. So she sits down in the tutoring lab, slides her paper across the table, and waits for the tutor to fix it. He reads it and slides it back. He does not tell her she is doing great. He tells her the truth. The trouble is not algebra. She never learned fractions, and everything since has been stacked on that gap. Then he tells her the other half. Fractions can be learned, and he has an hour.

For ten weeks that is how it goes. He asks the questions, and she does the writing. When she gets one right without help, he points to it. You did that one. I only watched. The night she fails a third quiz and starts packing up her books for good, he skips the pep talk. He sits with her a while and lets it be hard. Then he asks her to show him the one problem she got right. In ten weeks he never picks up the pencil.

She passes in December. By spring she is at the next table, showing a nineteen-year-old how fractions work. A tutor's words are only his own, and a prophet's came from God. But Paul measures prophecy the way that lab measures a tutor, by the person who walks out the door. Built up from the place where the gap was. Braver than she came in. Comforted on her worst night. The gift was never given to do her work for her. It was given to get her ready for her own.

Today’s World

All He Could Send Was His Voice

On May 10, 2022, a small plane was on its way from the Bahamas to Florida when the pilot passed out at the controls. A passenger named Darren Harrison got on the radio. He told the controller he had no idea how to fly the airplane. A controller at Fort Pierce told him to hold the wings level and start down slowly.

At Palm Beach, a controller named Robert Morgan was called back from his break. Morgan was also a flight instructor, but he had never flown that model. From the ground, all Morgan could do was talk. He told Harrison what was true and what came next. Follow the shoreline north. Palm Beach has a big runway, and emergency crews will be waiting. Ease the power back. Press the tops of the pedals to brake. Harrison put the plane down safely. The pilot survived surgery and was later cleared to fly again.

Morgan never touched the controls. Every word he sent was for the man in the seat, and the test of those words was a passenger who could now do what he could not do that morning. This is one landing, and it proves no rule. By Morgan's account Harrison stayed calm, and that steadiness was his own. Still, the day shows what plain, true words from a voice worth trusting can do. They left a frightened man able to do the work in front of him.

Christ at the center

Everything Paul says prophets do, Jesus did first. He builds. I will build My church, He said (Matthew 16:18). He encourages. To terrified men in a boat at night He called out, take courage, it is I (Matthew 14:27). He comforts. The neighbors came to console Martha and Mary. Then Jesus came, and He wept with them and called their brother out of the tomb (John 11:33-44). The gifts are His way of going on doing these things. He ascended, Paul says, and gave gifts to His people. So when a true word builds you up, more is happening than a kind friend talking. The risen Christ is caring for His body through one of its members. And the measure He is building toward is Himself. He does not plan to stop short.

Application

Take two minutes. Think of one person you know who is worn down right now. Ask God for one true sentence that would build up, encourage, or comfort that person, and write it down. The asking is the practice. If you want to do more, send it to them today.

Discussion Question

Paul says prophecy builds up, encourages, and comforts. Recall words someone spoke to you that did one of those (a note, a talk after church, a phone call). What made them land, and what does that show about how God wants His people to speak?

Closing Prayer

Lord, thank You for every voice You have used to put us back on our feet. We remember words that reached us on bad days, and we thank You for them. Build us up where we are weak. Put courage back into those of us who are ready to quit. Console the ones among us who are grieving. Then make us useful to each other. Give us true words for worn-down people, and keep us growing until Your church looks like Your Son. In Jesus' name, Amen.
