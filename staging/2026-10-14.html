Women Prophets

Old Testament ProphetsLesson 3Day 5 of 7

In brief

Five of the most powerful men in Judah hurry across Jerusalem with a scroll that has just made their king tear his robes. They need a word from God, and they knock on the door of a woman named Huldah. Scripture reports it without surprise, because she stands in a long line. Miriam led a freed nation in its first song. Deborah judged Israel under a palm tree and sent a general to war. God gives His word to whom He chooses, and every messenger faces the same test.

Yesterday

Yesterday prophets poured oil on priests and kings and then held them to God's word. One scene took us to Josiah's court, where a lost scroll was carried to a prophet. That prophet was a woman. Today we meet her, and two who came before her.

Historical Context

The King's Men Knew Her Address

The year is about 622 BC, the eighteenth year of King Josiah. Jeremiah has been preaching for five years by now (Jeremiah 1:2), and Zephaniah belongs to the same reign. Yet when the king orders his officials to ask the LORD about a newly found scroll, the high priest and the royal secretary go with the others to a woman named Huldah. The text even says where she lived, in Jerusalem's Second District.

Archaeologists working in Jerusalem's Jewish Quarter in the 1970s uncovered a city wall about seven meters thick, more than twenty feet. It was built in the late 700s BC to take in new neighborhoods that had spread across the western hill, and many scholars connect that newer part of town with the Second District. So picture the scene. The top men of the kingdom walk out of the palace, cross to the new side of the city, and stop at the home of a wardrobe keeper's wife. Nobody in the story treats it as strange.

Into the Word

The sea has closed over Egypt's chariots. On the far shore stands a nation that was enslaved a few days ago and hardly knows what to do with itself. Moses sings. Then a woman picks up a tambourine. Who gets to speak for God? The Bible's answer begins here, on a beach, with a title.

Generations pass. Israel has been crushed for twenty years by a Canaanite king with nine hundred iron chariots (Judges 4:1-4). The narrator introduces the nation's leader in a single plain sentence (Judges 4:4), and then shows her at work.

About five hundred years later, workers repairing the temple find a scroll of the law (2 Kings 22:8). The secretary reads it aloud to King Josiah. The king tears his clothes (2 Kings 22:11), because he can hear how far the nation has drifted from what is written.

The title alone proved nothing, for women or for men. Nehemiah prays about a prophetess named Noadiah, who joined other prophets in trying to frighten him away from God's work (Nehemiah 6:14). The test from Monday holds for everyone. Did God say it?

Street Level

Their Father Had Told Their Sister

Picture a family whose father fishes out of Alaska every summer. For weeks at a time his boat is beyond the reach of any cell tower. This June a buyer makes a cash offer on the family's back acreage, good for ten days. The two oldest sons handle the family's business, and one of them is an accountant. They search their father's desk for the paperwork and argue over what he would want. They cannot reach him, and the days are running out.

Their mother finally says it. Ask your sister. She drove him to the airport. The brothers had not thought of her. She works nights at a warehouse, and nobody has ever asked her about family money. They drive across town to her apartment, and she does not give them her opinion. Dad said not to sell, she tells them. He said an offer might come while he was gone, and to let it pass. And he said the deed is not in the desk. It's in the blue coffee can on the shelf in the shop. They go and look. It is in the blue can.

He could have told any of them. He told her. That is how Josiah's officials ended up at Huldah's door. They needed what God had said, so they went to the person He had said it to. Notice how her word was proved. Being overlooked did not make the sister right. The blue can did, and so did their father's voice on the phone three weeks later, saying the same thing. Had the accountant claimed to speak for Dad, the can would have tested him too.

Today’s World

The Last Twelve Meters Were Dug by Hand

On November 12, 2023, part of a highway tunnel under construction in northern India's mountains caved in and sealed 41 workers inside. The army, engineering agencies, and overseas experts came. Their best hope was a drill pushing a steel escape pipe through the rubble. It got almost 47 meters. Steel bars in the debris tangled it, and on November 25 it broke.

So officials turned to about a dozen laborers from Delhi and nearby. These men lay sewer and water lines by hand where machines cannot reach. That December one told a reporter he made about seven dollars on a day with work. The press called them rat-hole miners, after a banned way of digging coal by hand. They crawled into the pipe, less than three feet wide, and dug with spades. They cleared the last twelve meters in about a day. On November 28 they broke through, and all 41 came out alive.

A state academy's review credited their expertise alongside the machines. Digging skill makes no one a prophet, and the story settles nothing about church office. But when the drill failed, what mattered was who knew how to dig. Josiah's officials crossed a city on the same logic.

Christ at the center

The line of women prophets runs straight to Jesus. When Mary and Joseph carried Him into the temple as a baby, a prophetess was there. Anna was very old, a widow who all but lived in the temple courts (Luke 2:36). She came up at that moment, gave thanks to God, and spoke about the child to everyone who was waiting for Jerusalem's redemption (Luke 2:38). Miriam sang after the rescue at the sea. Anna announced the greater rescue when it was still small enough to carry. And on resurrection morning, the risen Jesus sent Mary Magdalene to His brothers with the news (John 20:17-18). I have seen the Lord. At Pentecost, Peter said the old promise had arrived. Your sons and your daughters will prophesy (Acts 2:17-18). Jesus is the Word every true prophet carried. He is glad to be carried by anyone He sends.

Application

Think of one woman whose words or example God has used to steady your faith. A mother, a teacher, a friend, a writer. Take two minutes and thank God for her by name. If you would like to go further, tell her sometime this week what her faithfulness did for you.

Discussion Question

Josiah's officials went to Huldah because she could tell them what God had said. Whose voice might you be overlooking because it comes from an unexpected person or place? What test should any voice pass, whoever is speaking?

Closing Prayer

Father, thank You for Miriam's song, for Deborah's courage, and for Huldah's plain truth. Thank You for the women who have carried Your word to us, many of whom no one will ever write about. Forgive us for the times we have weighed the messenger and skipped the message. Guard all of us from Miriam's pride and Noadiah's misuse of a gift. Make Your sons and daughters faithful with whatever You give us to say. And give us the good sense of Josiah's men, to go wherever Your word is found. In Jesus' name, Amen.
