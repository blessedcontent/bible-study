John the Baptist, Preparing a People

The Call of a ProphetLesson 2Day 6 of 7

In brief

A man who once shook a nation from a riverbank now sits in a stone cell, and he sends Jesus a question he never expected to ask. Are You the One, or should we look for someone else? Jesus does not scold him. He sends back evidence and a blessing, and then He praises John to the crowd. Today follows John from the wilderness to the prison. A prophet's work is to point to Jesus, and even a prophet's doubts are safe when they are carried to Him.

Yesterday

Yesterday Daniel settled a small matter in his heart and became a witness to kings. He saw a Son of Man receive a kingdom that never ends. Nearly six centuries passed. Then a voice in the wilderness announced that the King had come.

Historical Context

Josephus Names the Prison

Matthew says only that John was in prison. A Jewish historian named Josephus, writing later in that same century, names the place. Herod Antipas, the ruler of Galilee, sent John in chains to Machaerus, a fortress on a bare hilltop east of the Dead Sea, and had him put to death there. Josephus gives a political reason. Crowds hung on John's words, and Herod feared where that influence might lead. The Gospels add a personal one. John had told Herod that his marriage to his brother's wife was unlawful (Matthew 14:3-4).

Picture the change. John had lived under open sky with a river for a pulpit. Now he sat behind stone, and news reached him only through visiting friends. He heard about the works of the One he had announced, and none of them opened his door.

Into the Word

What is a prophet for? We have met four of them this week, and each one carried a message bigger than himself. John is the purest case, because the Gospels introduce him as almost nothing but a voice. He writes no book and works no miracle (John 10:41). He has one task. Mark opens the story of Jesus with him, and with words that were already centuries old.

Then the voice was silenced. Herod arrested him, and the man of the open wilderness waited in a cell while Jesus preached in Galilee. Here is what came out of that cell.

As John's friends walk away, Jesus turns to the crowd and defends him. No reed shaken by the wind. More than a prophet. Among those born of women, no one greater (Matthew 11:7-11). He said that on the very day John doubted. John never left that prison alive, but the work of a witness did not die with him. After the resurrection Jesus handed it on.

Street Level

She Sent Back the First Song

Imagine a piano teacher who spent forty years in a front room with one upright piano. Her best student was a girl she taught for free, and she told everyone the same thing. That one is going to fill concert halls. Now the teacher is past eighty and living in a care center after a bad fall. It is not where she meant to end up, and she will not be leaving. Word reaches her in pieces. Her student plays no concert halls. She teaches children at a community center across town, on donated keyboards. One evening the teacher hands her niece a note to carry. Did I get you wrong? Were you the one I said you were?

The student cannot give her teacher back her house or her strength. She does not scold her for asking, either. She tells the niece, stay for the recital, then go back and tell her what you heard. That night thirty children play, and the last one plays the first little song the teacher gave every beginner. Before the applause ends, the student tells the parents that the finest musician she ever knew is in a room on Birch Street. The niece records all of it on her phone.

That is close to what Jesus sent into John's cell. Not a key. Evidence, in words John had known all his life, and honor spoken out loud while the door stayed shut. One difference matters. The student could not change where her teacher was. Jesus could have opened John's door, and the Gospels never tell us why He did not. They tell us what He sent. They tell us what He said about John that same day.

Today’s World

Their Job Was to Leave Before the Finish

On April 26, 2026, Sabastian Sawe of Kenya won the London Marathon in one hour, 59 minutes, and 30 seconds. He became the first man to officially break two hours for the distance, and he took 65 seconds off the old world record.

Other runners had a different job that morning. Before the race, organizers named three pacemakers for the lead group, Oscar Chelimo of Uganda, Andrea Kiptoo of Kenya, and Nibret Melak of Ethiopia. Their assignment was to guide the favorites through the first half at record pace. The target for halfway was 60 minutes and 30 seconds. The lead group reached it in 60 minutes and 29. Then, just past 25 kilometers, about fifteen and a half miles, the last pacemaker stepped off the course, and Sawe launched his bid for the record.

Pacemaking is a job, and nothing here measures anyone's heart. It is simply a clear picture of an unusual kind of success. These men did their work well by holding the pace, hitting the mark, and then leaving the road to someone else. John's ministry had the same shape. The front of the road was never his to keep.

Christ at the center

Every prophet this week pointed forward. John is the one who got to point with his finger. Look, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world (John 1:29). In one sentence he gathered up Abraham's promised lamb and Isaiah's silent one and aimed them both at a carpenter from Nazareth. Then John suffered for telling the truth, and that pointed ahead as well. He was arrested, held by a ruler who knew he was a righteous man (Mark 6:20), and killed anyway. The One he announced would walk that same road. But John never claimed to take away anyone's sin, and his grave stayed closed. Jesus did claim it, and His tomb is empty. That is why a prophet must decrease. The best thing John ever did was step out of the way of a Savior who would not stay dead.

Application

If you are carrying a question about God today, do what John did. Take two minutes and send it to Jesus in plain words. Are You really the One? Why is my door still shut? Then read His answer in Matthew 11:1-6 once, slowly. If you have no such question today, pray for someone who does.

Discussion Question

John preached Jesus boldly and still doubted in the dark. What does it tell you that Jesus sent evidence and a blessing, and praised John that same day (recall a door God did not open for you)? What helps you not stumble over Him?

Closing Prayer

Father, Jesus is the One, and we do not need to look for another. Still, some of us are in cells today, places we did not choose and cannot leave, wondering why He heals others and leaves our door shut. Thank You that He does not scold honest questions. Send us what He sent John. Show us what You are doing, and keep us from stumbling over the kind of Savior He is. Level what is rough in us. Make us voices that point to Him and are glad to grow smaller. In Jesus' name, Amen.
