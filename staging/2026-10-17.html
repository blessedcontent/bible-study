New Testament Prophets

New Testament ProphetsLesson 4Day 1 of 7

In brief

A pile of stones sits in storage on the temple hill. They came from a ruined altar, and nobody knows what to do with them. So they wait for a prophet to come and say. Then the New Testament opens, and the quiet breaks. A widow of eighty-four tells the temple courts about a baby. Soon there are apostles, house-church prophets, and four sisters who prophesy. After His Son came, God kept speaking through people. The gifts differ. The Giver is the same, and every true gift points to Jesus.

Last week

Last week ended on a mountain. Moses and Elijah stood beside Jesus, and a voice from the cloud said, listen to Him. That raises a fair question. Once the Son has come, does God still send prophets? This week opens the New Testament to see.

Historical Context

They Stored the Stones and Waited for a Prophet

In 164 BC, Jewish fighters won back the temple in Jerusalem from a foreign king who had defiled it. The altar was the hard question. Pagan sacrifices had been burned on it. Could its stones be used again, or thrown out like rubble? A Jewish history of those years, the book of 1 Maccabees, says they pulled the altar down and stored its stones on the temple hill until a prophet should come and tell them what to do. The same book calls one later crisis the worst since prophets stopped appearing. It even says the people confirmed a new ruler only until a trustworthy prophet should arise.

That is how this writer saw his own day. The prophets' voices belonged to the past, and the present was for waiting. Then Luke opens his Gospel, and the waiting breaks. An old priest prophesies over his newborn son. A widow named Anna speaks about a Child in the temple courts. John preaches in the desert. And after Jesus rises and returns to His Father, Peter stands up at Pentecost and says Joel's promise has arrived. Sons and daughters will prophesy (Acts 2:16-18). They had stored the stones and waited for one prophet. God sent His Son, and then He poured out His Spirit on a whole church.

This Week’s Memory Text

Paul is writing to a church in Corinth with plenty of spiritual gifts and plenty of arguments about them. Before he sorts anything out, he sets down three short lines.

This Week’s Road Map

  • ☩Sunday: Jesus as Prophet. The crowds call Jesus a prophet, and they are right, but Peter and John show why that title is too small for Him.
  • ☩Monday: Apostles and Prophets. Paul defends his calling, and God's household rests on the witness of apostles and prophets, with Christ as the cornerstone.
  • ☩Tuesday: Functions of New Testament Prophets. Prophets build up, encourage, and comfort, and two of them carry a letter to Antioch and stay to strengthen the church.
  • ☩Wednesday: Female Prophets and Young Pastoral Associates. An elderly widow, four sisters, and two younger coworkers show that the Spirit does not sort by age or sex.
  • ☩Thursday: False Prophets in the New Testament. Jesus warns that fakes will be impressive, and He tells us to watch the fruit and wear God's armor.
  • ☩Friday: Further Thought. We gather the week's voices and ask what prophets are for after the Son has come.

Christ at the center

Hebrews says something that could sound like the end of this week before it starts. In the past God spoke through prophets, and in these last days He has spoken by His Son. If Jesus is the radiance of God's glory, what is left for anyone else to say? Yet the same New Testament is full of prophets, and Paul says the risen Christ Himself gave them to His church (Ephesians 4:11). So carry a question through the week. What is a prophet for, once the One all the prophets waited for has come? Jesus gave a hint on the night before He died. He said the Spirit would not speak on His own. The Spirit would glorify Jesus, taking what belongs to Jesus and making it known (John 16:13-14). The old prophets pointed ahead to Someone they had not met. This week, watch where the new ones point.

Application

Take two minutes. Read 1 Corinthians 12:4-6 slowly, once. Then think of one believer whose gift has helped you lately. It might be a teacher, a friend who prays, or someone who encourages. Thank God for that person by name. There is nothing else to do today.

Opening Question for the Week

Paul says the gifts are different and the Giver is the same. Where have you seen believers treat a difference in gifts as a ranking (in a church, a family, a team)? What changes if every true gift was handed out by the same Lord?

Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, thank You that You did not go quiet after Your Son came. You gave Your Spirit, and You have kept giving gifts to Your people. Open the New Testament to us this week. Let us meet the apostles and prophets as they were, the old widow, the young pastor, the sisters whose words we never got to read. Teach us to be glad for gifts that are not ours. Keep us from voices You did not send. And in every true voice, let us hear Your Son. In Jesus' name, Amen.
