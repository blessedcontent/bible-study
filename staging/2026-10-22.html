False Prophets in the New Testament

New Testament ProphetsLesson 4Day 6 of 7

In brief

Jesus is sitting on the Mount of Olives with the temple across the valley, and His disciples ask about the future. Much of His answer is warning. People will come using His title and working wonders, and they will be convincing. So He tells His friends ahead of time and points them to fruit, which is slower than flash. A sign can be impressive and still be false. Jesus told us in advance so that we could stand, and everything we need to stand comes from Him.

Yesterday

Yesterday an old widow, four sisters, and a young pastor all served God's word. The authority was in the message, not the messenger. That cuts the other way too, because an impressive messenger proves nothing. Today Jesus warns about voices He never sent.

Historical Context

They Led Crowds Into the Desert for a Sign

Within a generation of Jesus' warning, Judea watched it happen. The Jewish historian Josephus, who lived through those years, describes men he calls impostors and deceivers. They persuaded crowds to follow them into the wilderness, promising to show them wonders and signs done by God's design. The Roman governor Felix sent troops after them. One man from Egypt called himself a prophet, led a crowd to the Mount of Olives, and promised that at his command the walls of Jerusalem would fall. Soldiers killed or captured hundreds of his followers, and he slipped away. A Roman officer once mistook Paul for him (Acts 21:38).

Put that beside what Jesus said on the same hill. If they tell you He is out in the wilderness, do not go (Matthew 24:26). His warning was not only about some far-off age. It described the world His first followers were about to live in. The men Josephus describes had confidence, a crowd, and a promise of wonders. The crowds went, and Josephus says many of them paid for it.

Into the Word

It is a few days before the cross. Jesus is sitting on the Mount of Olives, and His disciples come to Him privately with a question. When will these things happen, and what will be the sign of Your coming (Matthew 24:3)? His first words in reply are a warning. See to it that no one deceives you (Matthew 24:4). Then He comes back to the subject again and again.

If wonders cannot settle it, what can? Jesus had answered that earlier in His ministry, on another hillside.

John, writing later, puts it plainly. Do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, because many false prophets have gone out into the world (1 John 4:1). His first test is what a voice says about Jesus Christ come in the flesh (1 John 4:2-3). Testing is not cynicism. It is what love for the truth does. Paul tells us what to wear while we do it.

Street Level

He Had Told Her in August

In August, before he leaves for college, a young man sits at his grandmother's kitchen table and tells her something odd. Someday somebody may call you and say he is me. He will be in trouble, and he will need money fast. Hang up, Grandma, and call my number. It is on your refrigerator. She laughs and says she would know her own grandson.

In November the phone rings at suppertime. Grandma, it's me. The voice is thick from crying, and it sounds like him. There has been a car accident. Then a second man comes on the line, calm and official. He explains where the money must go within the hour, and he says she must not tell anyone. It is an impressive performance. She has her coat half on before she sees the number on the refrigerator door. She does not argue with the men on the phone or try to trip them up. She hangs up and dials the number she already had. Her grandson answers on the third ring, in the library, fine.

Notice what kept her safe. She was not sharper than the callers. They had the right name, a voice full of tears, and a good story, and she was nearly out the door. What she had was a word given ahead of time by someone who loved her, and a number to check against. His plan never said that every frightening call is a lie. Some emergencies are real. It told her where to take any call before she moved. Jesus arms His friends the same way. He tells us first, hands us what God has already said, and asks us to stand.

Today’s World

The Video Came Before the Tests

In July 2023 a team of South Korean researchers posted a huge claim online. They said a material they called LK-99 was a superconductor at room temperature. A superconductor carries electricity with no loss. Known ones need extreme cold or crushing pressure, so this would be a big deal. Then came a video. A small piece of the material seemed to float over a magnet, the way superconductors do. The claim raced across social media, and hobby chemists tried to make the stuff themselves.

A floating speck does not prove that claim. The material had to be made and measured. By August other labs were reporting results. The floating was most likely ordinary magnetism, the kind that leaves one edge still touching the magnet. A change in electrical resistance near 100 degrees Celsius was traced to an impurity, copper sulfide. Purified samples did not carry current. In December 2023 a review committee from Korea's superconductivity society reported on months of tests by eight teams. It found no ground for calling LK-99 a superconductor.

Those tests weighed the material, not the people. A mistaken claim is not a lie, and measurements cannot show what anyone intended. One of the researchers still stood by the claim that December. Real discoveries go through the same checks and come out standing. The clip took seconds to watch. Careful answers took weeks, and the full review took months. Jesus' warning assumes that same gap between what dazzles today and what holds up later.

Christ at the center

The armor Paul describes had been worn before. Isaiah pictures the LORD Himself putting on righteousness like a breastplate and a helmet of salvation, because He saw that there was no one else to step in (Isaiah 59:16-17). The armor of God is first of all God's own. Jesus wore it into the wilderness. The tempter came quoting Scripture and proposed a spectacular sign, a leap from the top of the temple. Each time Jesus answered, it is written (Matthew 4:1-11). He was never deceived, and He stood where Adam and Israel had fallen. So when we are told to put on truth, righteousness, peace, and salvation, we are being handed what is already His. And remember how He spoke about all this. See, I have told you in advance (Matthew 24:25). That is how a Shepherd talks to sheep He means to keep.

Application

Take two minutes. Read Ephesians 6:14-17 and name the pieces out loud. Truth, righteousness, the gospel of peace, faith, salvation, the word of God. Ask which one you have been leaving off lately. Tell God, and ask Him to put it on you today.

Discussion Question

Jesus said false prophets would be impressive and that fruit is the test. What impresses you quickly in a teacher or leader (confidence, results, a big following)? What fruit would you want to see before you trusted the voice?

Closing Prayer

Father, thank You that Jesus told us in advance. He did not leave us to find out the hard way. We admit that we can be easily impressed, and that what is loud can pull at us. Make us slow to be dazzled and patient enough to look for fruit. Fasten Your truth around us. Cover us with Your righteousness. Put Your word in our hands and keep us praying. Guard us from hating anyone who has been led astray, and bring them home. When the evil day has passed, let us be found standing. In Jesus' name, Amen.
