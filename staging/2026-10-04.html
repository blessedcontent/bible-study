Abraham, Defender of the Covenant

The Call of a ProphetLesson 2Day 2 of 7

In brief

An old man stands outside his tent at night, trying to count stars. He has no son, no deed to any land, and no proof. God says his family will be like that sky. Abram believes Him, and heaven writes it down as righteousness. Today follows that trust from the day he packed to the mountain where a ram took a son's place. Abraham was not sent because he was qualified. He was counted right because he trusted the One who called.

Yesterday

Yesterday Isaiah heard a question in the throne room and answered, here I am. A coal had already touched his lips. Today goes back over a thousand years further, to the first person the Bible calls a prophet (Genesis 20:7). What made Abraham fit to be sent?

Historical Context

Ancient Promises Were Walked Through Blood

The strangest scene in Abram's story looks like a butcher's yard. God tells him to bring a heifer, a goat, a ram, and two birds. Abram cuts the larger animals in half and lays the halves facing each other, with a path down the middle (Genesis 15:6-17). To Abram it was paperwork. In his world a solemn agreement could be sealed this way, and the Hebrew phrase for making a covenant is literally to cut one. Centuries later God speaks through Jeremiah about leaders who had walked between the pieces of a calf, and says the oath breakers will end up like the animal (Jeremiah 34:18-19). Many scholars read that walk as a curse a person called down on himself. May this happen to me if I break my word.

So we would expect both partners to walk the path. Watch who walks in Genesis. Abram is on the ground in a deep sleep. A smoking firepot and a flaming torch pass between the halves, and Abram never takes a step. God staked Himself on the promise. Abram's part was to believe Him.

Into the Word

Picture what Abram is asked to do. He is seventy-five and settled in Haran, and a voice tells him to leave all of it for a land that has not even been named (Genesis 12:1-9). No map. No timeline. Only a promise that every family on earth will be blessed through him. What kind of person says yes to that, and what does God see in him? Hebrews sums up the whole move in one sentence.

Years pass, and no child comes. One night God takes him outside and tells him to count the stars (Genesis 15:5).

Two thousand years later, Paul reaches for that verse to explain the gospel to non-Jewish believers in Galatia.

Then came the morning that tested everything. God asked for Isaac, the son the whole promise depended on (Genesis 22:1-17). Abraham got up early and went. On the climb Isaac asked where the lamb was, and his father said God Himself would provide it. At the last second the angel of the LORD stopped his hand, and a ram caught in a thicket died in the boy's place. Abraham named the mountain The LORD Will Provide. Where did that leave the covenant for the generations who broke it?

Street Level

The Ticket Was Bought Before He Packed

Picture a young man on a friend's couch in a town with no work. His phone rings, and it is his aunt, three states away. She has a spare room, she says, and the plant near her is hiring. Then she adds the part that matters. I bought your bus ticket this morning. It is at the counter under your name. The bus leaves Friday at dawn.

He has never seen her town. He has eleven dollars, and she reads him no confirmation number. All he has is her voice. So on Thursday night he gives away what will not fit in a duffel bag, and on Friday he walks two miles to the station in the dark. At the counter he slides his ID across and half expects the clerk to frown at her screen. She prints a ticket. The receipt stapled to it shows one name on the payment line, and it is not his. Paid in full, Tuesday morning.

Tuesday. She paid before she even called, before he had said yes or packed a sock. His walk did not buy that seat, and his eleven dollars could not have. If he had stayed on the couch, the ticket would still have been paid for, and he would still have been on the couch. Genesis tells Abram's story the same way. The promise came first, and it was God's alone to keep. He alone passed between the pieces. Abram's part was to take Him at His word, and the long road was what taking Him at His word looked like. Trust does not pay the fare. It gets on the bus.

Today’s World

A Kidney Given on a Promise

A 64-year-old man wanted to give a kidney to his 4-year-old grandson. The boy had chronic kidney disease but would not need a transplant for many years, and the grandfather was healthy now. Could he donate today, to a stranger in need, and leave his grandson a kind of credit for later? In 2014 Dr. Jeffrey Veale of UCLA Health started a voucher program around that question.

In 2021 Veale and his colleagues reported on the first 250 voucher donors in JAMA Surgery. Each of them went through surgery and gave up a kidney, and the person they loved received only a voucher, which had no cash value and could not be sold or transferred. Their donations set off chains of matches that led to 573 transplants. During the study six vouchers were redeemed, and each holder received a transplant 36 to 155 days after redemption. Six is a small number, and most of the promises are still untested.

A transplant program is not God, and human promises can fail. What the record shows is narrower. Two hundred fifty people paid a real price on the strength of someone's word, before they could see how it would turn out. Abram left Haran the same way, with a promise in his ears and nothing yet in his hands.

Christ at the center

Jesus once told a hostile crowd that Abraham rejoiced to see His day (John 8:56). He did not say when, but Moriah reads like a preview. A father walks up a mountain with his only son, the son he loves. The son carries the wood on his own back. The father says God Himself will provide the lamb. For Isaac, a substitute appeared and the knife stopped. Centuries later, among those same hills, another Father walked that road with His only Son, and this time no voice from heaven called it off. Paul says God did not spare His own Son but gave Him up for us all (Romans 8:32). That is where credited righteousness comes from. It was never free. It could be written into Abraham's column, and ours, because it was paid out of Christ's. The LORD did provide.

Application

Take two minutes today with one promise of God you find hard to believe right now. If none comes to mind, use I will never leave you (Hebrews 13:5). Say it out loud to Him, say amen, and tell Him honestly how much weight you can put on it today. Abram started with one sentence too.

Discussion Question

Abram's obedience did not earn his righteous standing, and God alone walked between the pieces. Where do you still try to earn what He gives as a gift (at church, at home, in private)? What would leaning on His promise look like there?

Closing Prayer

Father, You called a flawed man out of a comfortable life, and You counted his trust as righteousness. We have nothing better to bring You. We confess that we keep trying to walk between the pieces ourselves, as though Your promise depended on our performance. Thank You for taking the weight of the covenant on Yourself. Thank You for the Lamb You provided. Write Your law inside us, as You said You would. Teach us to say amen to what You have spoken, and then to get up and go where You send, even when we cannot see the place yet. In Jesus' name, Amen.
