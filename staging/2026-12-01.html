Biblical Authority

Prophetic Authority and RelevanceLesson 10Day 4 of 7

In brief

Shaphan reads King Josiah a book found during temple repairs. The king hears what his people owe God and sends to ask what it means for him, too. Later he gathers everyone, great and small. He reads the book aloud and makes his promise where they can hear it. The king who read God's words to his people was answering to those words himself.

Yesterday

On the lake the disciples saw the wind and water obey Jesus. In His prayer they could hear what He meant to give with such authority. Today a king hears God's word in private. What will he do with it in public?

Historical Context

The Report Josiah Expected

Josiah sent his scribe Shaphan to the temple about money for repairs. The doorkeepers had collected gifts from the people. In Judah a payment could be weighed out in silver. A small stone found in Jerusalem is marked for half a shekel. Set a weight like that in one pan of a balance and you could measure out that much silver in the other. Josiah wanted the money released to pay the carpenters and masons and to buy timber and cut stone for the temple (2 Kings 22:3-7).

Shaphan came back with the report Josiah expected. The money had been handed over to the people doing the work. Then he had something else to tell the king. Hilkiah the high priest had found the Book of the Law in the temple and given it to him. Shaphan read it aloud in the king's presence (2 Kings 22:8-10). He had gone to the temple carrying the king's orders. Now it was the king's turn to listen.

Into the Word

There are several people Josiah can send to inquire of the LORD. Listen for the little word that includes the king himself among those who need an answer.

Now follow the book back to the temple where it was found. Josiah gathers the priests, the prophets, and the people, great and small. He reads the whole covenant to them. Then he stands by the pillar and promises before the LORD to keep it with all his heart. The people enter the covenant too (2 Kings 23:1-3). Josiah could have sent them home with a set of royal orders. What they actually hear is God's word and their king giving his own word to follow it. They can answer God for themselves.

Josiah's son Jehoiakim later used his position to cut short a reading (Jeremiah 36:22-27). During the siege of Jerusalem, officials even sought Jeremiah's death because they feared what those hearing him might do (Jeremiah 38:1-4). It takes some humility to let a difficult word have a hearing. Nicodemus, a teacher himself, stayed to ask Jesus how a man could be born again (John 3:1-10). In Philippi a jailer also had a question he needed someone else to answer.

The apostles once had to choose whose orders to follow when men with power told them to stop speaking (Acts 5:29). Josiah had power of his own. Letting others hear the book meant letting them hear what God required of him. God's word can reach an intention nobody else sees (Hebrews 4:12). At the pillar, the people could hear what their king intended to do.

Street Level

Three Piles on a Packing Crate

The last box is off the truck. On the way out, the woman who hired the movers presses a folded tip into the crew chief's hand. Back at the yard, three men sit on the tailgate. He counts the bills into three piles on a packing crate. He's just tucked his share into his shirt pocket when he sees that the woman has left him a message.

He lifts the phone to his ear and listens. She's unpacked the blue cups that belonged to her mother. Could the three women who wrapped her dishes yesterday share in the tip too? He could tell the other men how much to hand back. Instead he sets the phone on the crate and plays her message again, loud enough for them all to hear. One of the movers looks up. “That's Rose and her crew. They get back at five. I'll wait for them.”

In the temple, the people heard Josiah read the words that bound him, too. Then he stood where they could see him and gave his word to God. At the tailgate all three men have heard the woman's request for themselves. The first bills back on the crate are the chief's.

Christ at the center

Josiah stood by the pillar and promised to keep the words he had just read aloud. What hope is there for someone who has made a promise to God just as sincerely and broken it? On the night of His arrest, Jesus knelt to pray. He asked if He might be spared what lay ahead. Even then He chose to do His Father's will (Luke 22:41-42). He kept faith all the way to the cross. After He rose from the dead, Jesus opened His followers' minds to understand what Scripture had said about Him. They could now tell people of every nation to turn to God for forgiveness in His name (Luke 24:44-47). We can bring Him even the promise we're ashamed to remember.

Application

Think of one instruction from Jesus that you would readily pass on, such as doing for others what you would want them to do for you (Matthew 7:12). Bring to mind someone affected by a decision you make. What would that person see if you acted on those words first?

Discussion Question

What did Josiah make possible for other people by taking the book seriously himself, and who might feel the difference when you act on a hard word from Scripture?

Closing Prayer

Father, give us patience with the words we'd like to hurry past. When we're ready to tell someone else what Jesus said, let us hear what He is asking of us. Help us keep our word to the people who will know tomorrow whether we meant it today. Thank You for the risen Jesus, whose messengers told a jailer they had good news for him and for everyone in his house. In Jesus' name, Amen.
