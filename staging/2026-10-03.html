The Call of a Prophet

The Call of a ProphetLesson 2Day 1 of 7

In brief

A man stands in the temple in the year his king died, sure that he is finished. He has seen the Lord, and he knows what his own mouth is like. Then a coal touches his lips, a question fills the room, and he hears himself volunteer. This week meets five people God sent. Abraham, Elijah, Isaiah, Daniel, and John the Baptist. None of them applied. God's messengers were not picked for their record. They were met, mended, and sent.

Last week

Last week ended at the edge of a field, with a God who keeps coming toward frightened people and never chases them. While He came, He sent prophets ahead. This week asks who they were. How does an ordinary person end up carrying God's words?

Historical Context

The Throne Was Never Empty

Every call in this week's pages came while somebody else seemed to be running the world. Abram's own family served other gods (Joshua 24:2). Elijah faced a king and queen who fed hundreds of prophets of Baal and Asherah at the royal table. Isaiah's vision came in the year King Uzziah died, after a reign of fifty-two years (2 Chronicles 26:3). Most people in Jerusalem had never known another king, and a few years earlier a general named Tiglath-pileser had seized the throne of Assyria and started pushing his armies west. Daniel was marched to Babylon as a young captive around 605 BC. John the Baptist preached under Rome and died in a prison that belonged to one of the Herods.

None of those powers looked shaky at the time. That is what makes the opening of Isaiah's vision so striking. The throne in Jerusalem stands empty, and Isaiah sees another one that does not. The Lord is seated, high and exalted, and the hem of His robe alone fills the temple (Isaiah 6:1). Kings kept prophets who told them what they wanted to hear (1 Kings 22:6). God called His own from that throne room, and He sent them to tell the truth.

This Week’s Memory Text

Isaiah has just seen the Lord and said he is ruined. A seraph has touched his lips with a coal from the altar and said his sin is atoned for. Only then does he hear this.

This Week’s Road Map

  • ☩Sunday: Abraham, Defender of the Covenant. A man with no child and no map takes God at His word, and God calls that righteousness.
  • ☩Monday: The Elijah Message. On Mount Carmel one prophet asks a wavering nation to stop limping between two gods.
  • ☩Tuesday: Isaiah, the Gospel Prophet. The man with the burned lips is shown a Servant who carries everyone else's sin.
  • ☩Wednesday: Daniel, the Faithful Seer. A decision about dinner grows into a witness that three kings cannot ignore.
  • ☩Thursday: John the Baptist, Preparing a People. The voice that announced Jesus ends up in a cell, asking whether he got it right.
  • ☩Friday: Further Thought. Five callings are gathered into one picture, and the question in the throne room turns toward us.

Christ at the center

Every prophet this week is sent ahead of Someone. Abraham climbs a mountain beside his son, saying that God will provide the lamb. Elijah rebuilds a broken altar and waits for heaven to answer. Isaiah describes a Servant pierced for other people's wrongs. Daniel watches One like a Son of Man receive a kingdom that never ends. John stands in a river and points. Set the five side by side and they begin to sound like witnesses describing one Person from different angles. So carry a question through the week. If God went to this much trouble to announce Him, how much did He want us to recognize Jesus when He came? And notice who finally answers the question in the throne room. God asked who would go for Us. In the end the answer was not another prophet. The Son came Himself, and Hebrews hears Him say it on His way into the world (Hebrews 10:5-7). Here I am.

Application

Sometime today, take two quiet minutes. Read Isaiah 6:8 slowly, and notice that God asks before Isaiah answers. Then tell God honestly how that question lands on you right now. Eager, tired, afraid, unsure. You do not have to volunteer for anything today. Just let Him hear where you are.

Opening Question for the Week

Isaiah heard God's question only after the coal touched his lips. Do you tend to think God uses people because of their record or in spite of it (picture someone you consider useful to Him)? What does Isaiah's order, cleansed and then sent, change?

Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, You are still on the throne, whatever else seems to be running the world this week. We come like Isaiah, aware of what our own lips are like. Thank You that You do not wait for clean people to show up. You make people clean. Open these five lives to us. Let us watch Abraham trust You, Elijah stand, Isaiah go, Daniel stay faithful, and John point away from himself. Show us the One they were all sent ahead of. And when we hear You ask who will go, give us the courage to answer honestly. In Jesus' name, Amen.
