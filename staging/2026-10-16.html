Further Thought

Old Testament ProphetsLesson 3Day 7 of 7

Week in Review

This week we met the prophets without their halos. One ran for his life, one went to prison, one was stoned in the temple court, one sang on a beach, and one lived across town. Around them stood hundreds of others who used the same Name and had never been sent.

  • ☩Sabbath: Old Testament Prophets. With an army at En-gedi and the choir in front, Jehoshaphat asked Judah to believe God and the prophets He sends.
  • ☩Sunday: The Humanity of Prophets. After the fire, Elijah asked to die, and God answered with bread, sleep, a quiet voice, and a corrected count.
  • ☩Monday: Identifying True Prophets. Micaiah would not match the four hundred, and a servant knew Samuel as a man whose words come true.
  • ☩Tuesday: Functions of Prophets. Prophets poured the oil on priests and kings, held them to God's word, taught, wrote, and prayed.
  • ☩Wednesday: Women Prophets. Miriam sang, Deborah judged, and Josiah's officials carried a lost scroll to Huldah's door.
  • ☩Thursday: False Prophets. In a drought the popular prophets promised peace out of their own minds, and nobody had to change.

Historical Context

The Word Kept Arriving Outside the Palace

Gather the week's settings. A road down toward En-gedi with singers at the front of the column. A broom tree a day's walk past Beersheba. Royal archives at Mari and Nineveh, full of prophets telling kings not to fear. A king's desk with a hand-copied law on it. A thick wall around a new neighborhood where a wardrobe keeper's wife lived. Cisterns with nothing in them.

Put them together and a pattern shows. Much of what those archives kept was good news for a throne. In Israel the word of the LORD kept turning up somewhere else. It came to a singer in a crowd, a runaway in the desert, a judge under a palm tree, and a man whom God told to stop praying. Kings inherited their thrones, and priests inherited their altars. Prophets inherited nothing. They had only what God gave them to say.

What This Week Clarifies

The Fire Never Came From Elijah

Sabbath asked us to believe God's prophets, and then the week showed them to us. Elijah asked to die under a bush. Miriam turned on her brother. Samuel raised sons who took bribes. If the messengers were this breakable, how could a whole nation be told to stake its life on their words? Was Jehoshaphat asking too much?

Four Hundred Voices Used the Right Name

Monday and Thursday left an uncomfortable fact on the table. The false prophets were not preaching Baal. They spoke in the LORD's name, they outnumbered the true ones, and some of them may have believed every word they said. So how was a farmer in Judah supposed to tell the difference? How are we?

Bread for the Broken and Warnings for the Comfortable

God's tone seemed to change from day to day. Elijah got hot bread, sleep, and a whisper. Judah got Huldah's announcement of disaster and Jeremiah's order to stop praying. Then Thursday warned us about prophets who comfort. Is the God who speaks through prophets gentle or severe? And how would we know which to expect?

Still Speaking

One of Them Drove Down to the River

Picture a neighborhood group chat on a night of heavy rain. Half the street backs up to the river. By nine o'clock there are sixty messages, and nearly all of them are good news. The river has crested. A screenshot says so. One neighbor posts a laughing face and tells everybody to go to bed. The messages agree with each other because most of them are the same post, passed along by people who want it to be true.

Then a text arrives from the woman on the corner, the one half the street has muted. She is seventy-four with a bad knee, and she has driven down to the boat ramp in the dark. Her photo is blurry, and her spelling is worse. Water is on the second step and still coming. Move the cars tonight. A minute later she sends another, to the young mother at the top of the hill who has been up two nights with a new baby. You are high and dry, honey. Go to sleep.

Nobody on that street had to read hearts or count messages. They had to ask where each message came from. Sixty came from other phones. One came from the river. It asked something of them, where the good news let everyone stay in bed. It cost her a drive in the dark and another round of teasing. And it held. By midnight the water was over the ramp. Look again at her two texts. She sent a warning to the people parked by the water and rest to the one who was worn out. It was the same river both times. She fit her words to what each neighbor needed, which is how God spoke to Ahab and to Elijah.

One blurry text is not right because it stands alone, and good news is not false because it is pleasant. For a prophet the question was never how many agreed. It was whether God had sent him. God has not left us guessing. He has given us His written word to hold every voice against, and He still sends that word through people with bad knees and poor spelling. The fire never came from Elijah. The river was never hers. All she had was what she saw, and it was enough to move the cars.

Christ at the Center of the Week

Every day this week leaned toward one Person. Sabbath remembered Moses' promise of a Prophet like him. Sunday found Jesus in Gethsemane, crushed with grief, and later cooking breakfast over coals for friends who had run. Monday heard Him say He spoke only what the Father taught Him, and watched Him struck for it as Micaiah was. Tuesday named Him the Anointed One, Prophet, Priest, and King together. Wednesday saw Anna announce Him and Mary Magdalene carry the news of His rising. Thursday heard Him offer the only peace that is not a lie. Then remember one more mountain. Jesus stood in blazing light, and Moses and Elijah stood talking with Him (Luke 9:28-36). The tired man from the broom tree was there to see what his work had been for. A voice from the cloud said, this is My Son, listen to Him. When the cloud lifted, the disciples saw Jesus alone. Every true prophet carried a word from God. Jesus is the Word.

Weekend Practice

Before Sabbath ends, take two minutes with 2 Chronicles 20:20. Ask God to bring to mind one true word you have been avoiding because it was unwelcome or because the messenger did not impress you. Tell Him you are willing to hear it. If you want to do more, read 1 Kings 19:1-16 aloud with someone this weekend.

Closing Prayer for the Week

Father, thank You for speaking, and for the ordinary people You have trusted with Your words. Thank You for bread under the broom tree and for warnings we did not want. Where we are worn out, feed us. Where we are comfortable in what is wrong, tell us the truth, and give us tender hearts like Josiah's. Keep us from the four hundred and from the prophets of our own minds. Teach us to weigh every voice by Your word. As this Sabbath begins, quiet the noise around us until we hear Your Son, and help us listen to Him. In Jesus' name, Amen.
