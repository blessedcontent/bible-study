Revelation and Inspiration

Revelation and InspirationLesson 5Day 1 of 7

In brief

John is on a small island called Patmos when a voice speaks behind him, loud as a trumpet. Write on a scroll what you see, it says, and send it to seven churches. So he writes, and the message sets out toward people he will never meet, including us. God uncovers what we could never find on our own. Then He carries His word through human voices, scrolls, and even acted-out signs until it reaches us.

Last week

Last week ended on a mountain trail with a map in a hiker's pocket. The map stood for the witness of the apostles and prophets, which no later voice redraws. But how did God's word get onto that map? This week traces how it was given and carried.

Historical Context

The Scroll Had Seven Stops

Patmos is a small, rocky island in the Aegean Sea, off the coast of the Roman province of Asia. Today that coast is western Turkey, and the island belongs to Greece. John tells us only that he was there because of the word of God and his testimony about Jesus (Revelation 1:9-11). Early Christian writers said he had been banished there. One of them, Irenaeus, writing around AD 180, placed John's visions near the end of the emperor Domitian's reign, which ended in AD 96.

The voice named seven churches, and the list follows a map. Start at Ephesus, travel north to Smyrna and Pergamum, then turn southeast through Thyatira, Sardis, and Philadelphia to Laodicea. Many scholars think that loop of Roman roads was the route the scroll was meant to travel. At each stop, someone would read it aloud. Most people in the Roman world could not read, so a church heard Scripture far more often than it held it. That is why the book opens with a blessing on the one who reads aloud and on those who hear (Revelation 1:1-3). God's word reached Laodicea the way most news did. It came down a road and was read to a room.

This Week’s Memory Text

Before John tells us anything he saw, the last book of the Bible tells us how it reached him.

This Week’s Road Map

  • ☩Sunday: General and Special Revelation. The sky tells everyone that God is great, but only His word and His Son tell us how He rescues.
  • ☩Monday: The Process of Inspiration. Visions, eyewitness memories, and careful research all became Scripture as the Holy Spirit carried the writers along.
  • ☩Tuesday: Oral Messages. God's word was spoken long before it was written, and Jesus, who left us no writings, spoke like no one else.
  • ☩Wednesday: Written Messages. God had His word written down so it would last, warn, and lead people to believe, and He kept the record honest.
  • ☩Thursday: Drama, Symbols, Parables. A prophet lays siege to a clay brick, and Jesus tells stories about seed, because some truths have to be seen.
  • ☩Friday: Further Thought. We gather the week's questions about how God's word reached us and why we can trust it.

Christ at the center

Look again at the first line. This is the revelation of Jesus Christ, which God gave Him. Even the Son receives the message and passes it on. That is how Jesus described His own work on the night before He died. He no longer called His disciples servants. He called them friends, because He had made known to them everything He heard from His Father (John 15:15). A few verses into Revelation, John breaks into praise for the One who loves us and set us free from our sins by His blood (Revelation 1:5). The One who hands God's word down to us is the One who paid for us to receive it as friends. So carry a question through this week. Why would God trust His word to voices, scrolls, and a prophet's aching body, with all their weakness? Watch who carries it first, straight from the Father. His hands have nail scars.

Application

Take two minutes. Read Revelation 1:1-3 aloud, slowly, even if you are alone. Notice that the blessing is promised to the one who reads aloud and to those who hear and obey. Then ask God for ears that do both this week.

Opening Question for the Week

God could speak to each of us directly, yet He usually sends His word through other people. Who first put God's word in front of you (a parent, a teacher, a friend, a Bible in a drawer)? Why might God work that way?

Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, thank You that You did not leave us to guess about You. You lifted the cover off what we could never have found, and You sent it all the way to us. This week, open Your word to us as it was spoken, written, and even acted out. Give us the ears of those first churches, who heard it read aloud and wanted to obey. Make us honest about what we do not understand and grateful for what You have shown. And on every page, let us meet Your Son. In Jesus' name, Amen.
