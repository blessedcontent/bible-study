Our Triune God

Grace, Love, and FellowshipLesson 13Day 6 of 7

In brief

A man raised as a strict Pharisee wakes before dawn and prays the sentence devout Jews said morning and evening. Hear, O Israel, the Lord our God, the Lord is one. Then he writes a blessing with three names in it. Today asks how those two sentences live in one man. Paul's letters refuse to split the blessing into departments. Grace, love, and fellowship come from Father, Son, and Spirit together, in one rescue told in three verses. God did not divide the work of saving you into departments. Father, Son, and Spirit came together for all of you.

Yesterday

Yesterday the Holy Spirit turned out to be a Someone, with a will and a voice and a family word on His lips. That completes the three names in Paul's blessing. Today we put them back together and ask the week's question. Is this three gifts from three separate givers, or one God, three Persons, giving together?

Historical Context

Paul Kept the Shema and Placed Jesus Inside It

Twice a day, morning and evening, a devout Jew recited the Shema, the sentence from Deuteronomy that begins Hear, O Israel, the Lord our God, the Lord is one (Deuteronomy 6:4). In a world where cities kept a dozen gods with a dozen departments, that one line was the fence around Israel. One God. No committee in heaven. Paul was raised a Pharisee and trained under a leading rabbi (Acts 22:3), so this was the confession he grew up on, and nothing in his letters suggests he ever let it go.

Then look at what he did with it. Writing to Corinth about idols, he echoed its two great words. For us there is one God, the Father, from whom all things came, and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom all things came (1 Corinthians 8:6). Many scholars read that sentence as Paul placing Jesus inside Israel's confession, God and Lord each given a name, and the second name belonging to a man crucified in Jerusalem. Paul never says he is adding a god. He writes like a man who believes he has learned who the one God is. The closing blessing does something similar with the Spirit included. The one God of the Shema is asked to be with His people under three names.

Into the Word

It is tempting to read Paul's blessing like an org chart. Grace is the Son's job, love is the Father's, fellowship belongs to the Spirit. Paul's letters will not let you keep that chart for a page. Grace comes from the Father. Love is the Spirit's too. Fellowship is with the Son. So today we chase each word across the letters, then read one of the shortest tellings of the whole rescue and count who is in it. What does it mean that grace, love, and fellowship come from all three together?

Love is the word we would most expect to belong to the Father alone. Paul will not let it stay put.

If all three are in every gift, how do they work together? Paul once told the whole rescue in three verses.

Paul does not leave it as a doctrine to admire. He kneels and asks for it to happen inside people.

Street Level

Nobody Was Responsible for Her

Imagine a woman two weeks after her husband's funeral, in a plastic chair at the electric company with a folder on her lap. She needs the account in her name. The clerk needs a death certificate. The bank kept her copy, and the bank needs the letter from the court, and the court needs a form from an office that is closed today. Everyone she talks to is telling the truth about their own department. Not one of them is responsible for her. By the fourth counter she has stopped saying that he died and started saying which form she is missing, and that is the real cost. She has become a set of pieces, and the pieces are being handled.

It is easy to read Paul's blessing as three counters. Grace at one window, love at the next, fellowship down the hall. Paul will not allow it. There are three Persons in that sentence and one God, and when He saves you the help does not arrive in fractions. The Father sends. The Son redeems. The Spirit cries Father from inside you. No one sends you to another counter, and no Person of the three stands in for another. You were never a set of pieces to Him. You were a person, and He came for a person.

Today’s World

Three Hundred Workshops, One Cathedral

When Notre-Dame de Paris reopened in December 2024, five years after the fire, two French ministries published the numbers behind the work. More than 300 companies and art workshops from across France, and more than 3,000 craftspeople and artisans, had rebuilt the cathedral. A stone cutter's job was not a carpenter's. A glazier's was not a roofer's. Each trade did work no other could do, and none of it was a cathedral until it stood together.

Three thousand interchangeable workers could not have done it. The building needed people who stayed different and pulled toward one thing. That is Paul's picture of a church, many kinds of work under one Lord, where difference is the design and not the damage. The cathedral says nothing about what God is in Himself. It shows what His people should look like. Many trades. One building.

Christ at the center

Nobody reasons their way to the Trinity. You meet it, and the door is Jesus. Whoever has seen Him has seen the Father (John 14:9). The week's blessing is what happened to people who met Jesus and could not describe God any smaller afterward. Look at the cross and you see all three. The Father gives His one-of-a-kind Son. The Son offers Himself without blemish, and Hebrews says He did it through the eternal Spirit (Hebrews 9:14), which many readers take as the Holy Spirit at the cross. Nobody stands back. The grace that became poor, the love that sent while we were enemies, and the fellowship that cries Abba from inside a person are one act of one God, and the act has a face. Paul kept the Shema, and he also knelt to the Father, in the name of the Son, by the strength of the Spirit, and his letters show no sign that he thought this was praying to three gods.

Application

Pray one prayer today that addresses all three by name. Thank the Father for one specific grace, thank Jesus for one specific way His love reached you, and thank the Spirit for one moment of fellowship you noticed this week. Keep it short. Then, before you say Amen, thank all three for all three gifts at once, because that is how they were given.

Discussion Question

Which of the three, Father, Son, or Spirit, do you pray to least, and what does that say about how you picture the work being divided (think of your last three prayers)? If a church's unity is meant to mirror God's oneness, what would have to change in how you treat one specific believer for the mirror to show anything?

Closing Prayer

Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, one God, we have handed You out in pieces, praying to one of You and forgetting the others, as if saving us had been divided among strangers. Forgive us, and fill us the way Paul prayed, strengthened by the Spirit, with Christ at home in our hearts, up to all the fullness of God. Make our churches one and many at once, so that people who watch us see something of what You are like. And when we cannot understand You, let us still trust You, because we have seen Jesus. In Jesus' name, Amen.
