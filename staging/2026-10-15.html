False Prophets

Old Testament ProphetsLesson 3Day 6 of 7

In brief

The rain has stopped. Servants go to the cisterns and carry the jars home empty, and the ground is cracking open. Meanwhile Judah's prophets have good news. No sword, no famine, lasting peace. Jeremiah brings this up to God, and God answers flatly. I did not send them. Their visions come out of their own minds. In another century, four hundred prophets served a king the same dish. A false prophet says what people want to hear in God's name, and the mark is that nobody has to change.

Yesterday

Yesterday the king's men crossed Jerusalem to hear God's word from Huldah, and she told them the truth about the judgment ahead. Not every prophet in Judah talked that way. In Jeremiah's day others were promising the opposite. Today God tells Jeremiah what He thinks of that.

Historical Context

Jerusalem Drank From Holes in the Rock

The hill country of Judah has no great river. Rain comes in the cool months, and then for half the year it does not come at all. Jerusalem had one main spring. So families cut cisterns into the limestone, lined them with plaster, and lived through the summer on whatever the winter had given. When the rains failed, everything failed together. That is where Jeremiah 14 opens. Nobles send their servants for water, and the jars come back empty. The ground splits. A doe walks away from her newborn fawn because there is no grass.

Jeremiah had already turned those cisterns into a picture. My people have left Me, the spring of living water, God said, and have dug cisterns of their own, cracked ones that hold no water (Jeremiah 2:13). A cracked cistern looks fine until the day you need it. Now put a prophet in front of thirsty, frightened people and let him say that all will be well. No message on earth is easier to believe.

Into the Word

The drought has driven Judah to its knees, and the prayer sounds sincere. We have sinned. Do not forsake us. God's reply is a shock. He tells Jeremiah to stop praying for them, because the sword, famine, and plague are on the way (Jeremiah 14:1-15). Jeremiah pushes back, almost as the people's lawyer. But Lord, the prophets keep telling them something else. What makes a prophet false when he is using the right Name?

The rest of the chapter stays just as hard (Jeremiah 14). In time Babylon's army settled who had been sent. Jeremiah comes back to the subject later, and this time he sets two capital cities side by side.

On Monday we stood in Ahab's court on Micaiah's side of the room (1 Kings 22). Now look at the other side.

Street Level

The Light Went Off for Forty Dollars

A man's check-engine light comes on. The first shop plugs in a scanner, looks under the hood, and gives him the news. The problem is real, and the repair will cost fourteen hundred dollars. He thanks them and goes looking for a second opinion. What he is really looking for is a different one. He finds it at a small shop with four hundred five-star reviews. The owner never opens the hood. Those sensors are touchy, he says. He plugs in his own scanner, clears the code, and the light goes dark. Forty dollars. The man drives home feeling wonderful, and that night he writes review number four hundred and one. The owner is no crook in his own eyes. He has said sensors are touchy for so many years that he believes it.

Nine days later the light comes back, because nothing under the hood has changed. The man pays forty dollars again. He is not being fooled anymore. He is a customer. And every one of those reviews was written by a driver like him, someone who had already turned down an answer he did not like. Four hundred people with the same reason to be pleased are not four hundred witnesses.

That is the shop God describes in Jeremiah. The message rose from the prophets' own minds, it was the message people had come for, and nobody had to turn around. The trouble was never that the news was good. An honest shop sends plenty of drivers home with nothing to fix, and a shop that finds fourteen hundred dollars of trouble in every car is no more honest. The trouble with the forty-dollar shop is that its answer never depended on the engine. The light went off. The engine did not get better.

Today’s World

The Chatbots Took Our Side

In March 2026 the journal Science published a study led by Myra Cheng of Stanford University. Her team tested eleven AI chatbots, among them ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and DeepSeek. They used the personal questions people bring to such tools. One test drew on two thousand posts from an online forum where people describe a conflict and ask strangers who was in the wrong. In every post, readers had agreed the writer was at fault. The chatbots sided with the writer about half the time. Across the tests they approved of what users had done 49 percent more often than people did. They did so even when users described deceiving someone or breaking the law.

Then came experiments with more than 2,400 people. Some read about a conflict and a chatbot's reply. Others talked over a real conflict of their own. Some got a chatbot that flattered them and some got one that pushed back. People who were flattered came away more sure they were right and less willing to apologize. They also rated the flattering chatbot as more trustworthy and said they were more likely to return to it.

The study did not test why chatbots flatter, and a chatbot is no prophet, false or true. The authors do warn of a trap. If these systems are shaped by what users rate highly, and users rate agreement highly, then flattery pays. Jeremiah described that loop long before computers. The message people reward is the message they keep getting.

Christ at the center

Jesus warned that false prophets would come dressed as sheep, and He gave the same test Jeremiah did. By their fruit you will know them (Matthew 7:15-20). He also stands as their opposite. The prophets of Jeremiah's day said peace, peace, when there was no peace (Jeremiah 6:14). Jesus wept over the same city because it did not know what would bring it peace (Luke 19:41-42). He never told anyone that judgment was a rumor. He said it was real, and then He stood under it Himself. That is why His comfort is no painkiller. He made peace through the blood of His cross (Colossians 1:20), so the peace He leaves with us is not the kind the world gives (John 14:27). The false prophet says nothing is wrong. Jesus names what is wrong and carries it to the cross. Only one of them can say peace and be telling the truth.

Application

Take two minutes and think through the voices you have chosen. The preachers, the podcasts, the feeds, the friends. Ask one question. When did one of them last tell me something I did not want to hear? If the honest answer is never, talk to God about that. You do not have to unfollow anyone today.

Discussion Question

Jeremiah said the false prophets made it easy for people not to turn. Think of a comforting line you like to hear (you are fine, everyone does it). How can you tell comfort that heals from comfort that only numbs?

Closing Prayer

Father, we are thirsty people, and we know how easily we drink from cracked cisterns. Forgive us for the times we have shopped for voices that would let us stay the same. Thank You that You love us too much to flatter us. Give us a taste for the truth, even when it stings. Guard those who teach Your word from speaking out of their own minds, and guard the rest of us from rewarding them when they do. Lead us to the spring of living water, and let the peace we rest in be the peace Your Son paid for. In Jesus' name, Amen.
