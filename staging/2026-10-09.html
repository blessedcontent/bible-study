Further Thought

The Call of a ProphetLesson 2Day 7 of 7

Week in Review

Five people were sent this week. A wanderer, a loner on a mountain, a man with burned lips, a captive, and a voice in the wilderness. None of them applied, and every one of them pointed past himself.

  • ☩Sabbath: The Call of a Prophet. The year a king died, Isaiah saw the throne that is never empty, was cleansed by a coal, and heard God ask whom to send.
  • ☩Sunday: Abraham, Defender of the Covenant. Abram believed a promise he could not see, God counted it as righteousness, and God alone walked between the pieces.
  • ☩Monday: The Elijah Message. On Carmel one prophet asked a limping nation to choose, and fire fell so that hearts would turn home.
  • ☩Tuesday: Isaiah, the Gospel Prophet. A forgiven prophet described a silent Servant on whom the wrongs of us all were made to meet.
  • ☩Wednesday: Daniel, the Faithful Seer. A settled decision about a meal grew into a witness that left kings praising the God of heaven.
  • ☩Thursday: John the Baptist, Preparing a People. The voice that announced Jesus doubted in prison, and Jesus answered with evidence, a blessing, and praise.

Historical Context

Every Power They Faced Is a Ruin Now

Lay the week's backgrounds side by side. A path between cut animals, where we expect two partners to walk and only God does. Clay tablets praising Baal as the rider on the clouds, and three years without a drop of rain. A leather scroll from the Dead Sea caves, carrying Isaiah 53 more than a century before Bethlehem. Babylonian receipts for oil handed to a captive king of Judah. A hilltop fortress where Herod locked up the Baptist.

Each one belongs to a power that looked permanent. Storm gods, empires, royal tables, prison walls. Every one of them is rubble or a museum piece today, and the words those prophets carried are still being read this morning. Isaiah saw it first. Earthly thrones empty out. The One above them does not.

What This Week Clarifies

Empty Hands That Still Get Up and Go

Sunday insisted that Abram earned nothing. He brought empty hands, and God supplied the rest. Yet the rest of the week was full of people doing hard things. Elijah stood alone against 450. Daniel refused the king's plate and later the king's decree. So which is it? Does God take us as we are, or does He expect a life like Daniel's?

Fire for Elijah and a Shut Door for John

Monday and Thursday sit uneasily together. Elijah prayed for half a minute, and fire fell from heaven in front of a nation. John, whom Jesus ranked second to no prophet before him, sent a question from a cell and never walked out. Both men were faithful. One received a miracle and the other a message. Does God answer His servants or not?

The Question Was Asked to the Whole Room

It would be easy to finish this week as a spectator. Abraham, Elijah, Isaiah, Daniel, and John were giants, called at turning points in history with visions and fire. Most of us will never see a seraph or stand before a king. So was any of this about us? Or is the call of a prophet something to admire from a safe distance?

Still Speaking

He Carried Out What Someone Else Cooked

Picture a Wednesday night supper in a church basement, the kind any town might have, free to whoever walks in. In March a man came through the line who had been sleeping in his car. Nobody asked him to explain himself. Somebody handed him a plate, and then a second one. He came back the next week, and the next.

One night in June the kitchen is short of hands. The woman who runs it leans out the door and asks the whole room, who can carry these out? She is not looking at anyone in particular. The man from March is on his feet before he has thought about it. He cannot cook, and he is still sleeping in that car. But he knows what a hot plate meant to him, and he knows the way from the kitchen to the tables. All evening he carries out food someone else made. Nobody at the tables will remember his face. They will remember the supper. That is what he is there for.

Set this week beside that room. Isaiah was cleansed before he was sent. The coal touched his lips, and only then did he hear the question (Isaiah 6:8). Abram came with empty hands. Elijah asked God to turn hearts, and then he stepped back. Daniel turned down the king's food because he already belonged to Someone else, and he made sure a king's praise landed on God. John said he must become less (John 3:30), and his cell never opened. None of them cooked the meal. They carried it. Then Jesus handed the same work to ordinary followers and called them witnesses (Acts 1:8).

So the call of a prophet is not a story about a different kind of person. If you have been fed at His table, you know the way from the kitchen to the tables, and someone near you is hungry. You do not need a spotless record or a finished life. The man in the basement had neither. God's question was never aimed only at giants. It was asked to the whole room. You are in the room.

Christ at the Center of the Week

Line the five up and they form one portrait. Abraham climbed a mountain saying God would provide the lamb, and the Father did, at the cost of His own Son. Elijah watched fire fall on the sacrifice while the guilty went free, and at the cross the judgment fell on Jesus. Isaiah, forgiven by a coal from the altar, described the silent Servant who went to that altar Himself. Daniel saw One like a Son of Man receive a kingdom that cannot be destroyed, and Jesus took that name at His trial. John pointed and said, look, the Lamb of God. They were all sent ahead of Him. Then He came, and He was better than every one of them. More trusting than Abraham. Bolder than Elijah. Cleaner than Isaiah. More faithful than Daniel. John had to ask, are You the One. Jesus never wavered about who He was or why He came. Every prophet said look ahead. Only One could say come to Me.

Weekend Practice

Before Sabbath ends, take two quiet minutes with Isaiah 6:8. First thank God for one specific way He has cleansed or carried you. Then answer His question honestly, even if your answer is, make me willing. If you want to do more, tell one person this weekend one thing you have seen Jesus do.

Closing Prayer for the Week

Father, thank You for the people You sent ahead of Your Son. Thank You for Abraham's trust, Elijah's courage, Isaiah's honesty, Daniel's long obedience, and John's willingness to grow smaller. Thank You most for the One they all announced. We bring You empty hands, and we ask You to fill them. Where we are limping, turn our hearts home. Where we feel unclean, touch our lips. Where our tests are small and hidden, keep us faithful. Where our door stays shut, keep us from stumbling over You. As this Sabbath begins, let us hear Your question without fear, and give us grace to say here am I. In Jesus' name, Amen.
