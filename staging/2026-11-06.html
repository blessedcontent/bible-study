The Prophetic WritingsLesson 6Day 7 of 7

Week in Review

This week opened the Bible's writing itself. It found songs and travel records, courtroom speeches and letters, scribes and archives and breakable messengers, and in all of it God was speaking.

  • ☩Sabbath: The Prophetic Writings. Everything God had written long ago was written for our instruction, so that we would have hope.
  • ☩Sunday: The Call to Write. God had His warnings written so people could turn, and His forgiveness was waiting on the other side.
  • ☩Monday: Literary Genres and Styles. A travel record, a heavy heading, a poem, a lawsuit, and a letter each carry God's voice in their own way.
  • ☩Tuesday: Literary Assistants. Baruch and Tertius held the pen, and the words stayed tied to their source.
  • ☩Wednesday: Literary Sources. Inspired writers searched out sayings and records and vouched for what they chose to keep.
  • ☩Thursday: Divine Message and Human Imperfections. God puts His treasure in clay jars so that everyone can see whose power it is.

Historical Context

The Word Moved Through Ordinary Paperwork

Gather the week's settings. A church in Rome, split by an emperor's order and by arguments over dinner. A scroll ordered in the year Babylon won at Carchemish. Treaties that began with a great king's kindness. A scribe taking dictation, and a sender adding the last line in his own hand. Persian archives searched for an old decree. A city that preferred polished speakers.

None of it was unusual for its day. Kings kept records, officials wrote letters, and scribes took dictation. God's word did not need a world of its own to travel in. It moved through the ordinary paperwork of the ancient world, and it reached the people it was meant for.

What This Week Clarifies

The Hard Pages Were Written for Hope

On Sabbath we read that everything written in the past was written to give us hope. Then the week handed us heavy pages. A vow against raiders who preyed on the weak. A scroll full of coming calamity. A heading that announced a burden, and a courtroom where God brought His case. How can writing like that give anyone hope?

Many Hands Held the Pen

Tuesday showed scribes writing out what prophets and apostles said. Wednesday showed writers gathering sayings, searching records, and quoting a line from outside the Bible. Monday found Paul marking some words as his own instruction. If so many hands and choices went into the Bible, whose word are we holding?

Still Speaking

The Shoebox Went to Sea

Picture a young sailor leaving on his first deployment. His ship will be at sea for months, and the internet on board will come and go. At the airport, his mother hands him a shoebox full of envelopes. For weeks she has been asking everyone who loves her son to write something. Then she sorted what came in and marked each envelope for the day it might be needed. Open when you can't sleep. Open on your birthday. Open when you want to quit.

Some of the letters are polished, and many are not. His father typed two pages and signed them with a pen that was running dry. His grandfather's hands shake too much to write now, so an aunt wrote down what the old man said while he talked. His best friend sent a list of every time they laughed until they cried. His six-year-old nephew sent a crayon drawing of the ship, with every porthole a different color. That first week on board, the box looks almost embarrassing next to the ship.

Then come the hard weeks, with long watches, short nights, and days without word from home. He starts opening envelopes, and the plain ones turn out to be the ones he needs. He reads his grandfather's letter in his aunt's handwriting until he can hear the old man's voice in it. The one marked Open when you want to quit is the hardest letter in the box, and he is glad somebody loved him enough to write it. By the time the ship comes home, the crayon drawing is taped inside his locker, and the box has gone soft at the corners.

This week we read pages like that. God did not hand us His word untouched by human hands. He sent it through people, and He meant every page of it for people facing long stretches of their own (Romans 15:4). A plain page can be the one that holds us up, and a hard page can be the one that keeps us from quitting. Some of those pages were written for a day you have not reached yet.

Christ at the Center of the Week

Every day this week led to Jesus. On Sabbath Paul read an old psalm and found Christ in it, and Jesus said the Law, the Prophets, and the Psalms were all written about Him. On Sunday the forgiveness Jeremiah's scroll held out belonged to the new covenant Jesus announced over the cup, and the cross paid for it. On Monday the case God brought against His people was settled by the Son who now speaks for us. On Tuesday Paul promised in his own hand to pay another man's debt, and Jesus' hands were nailed to the cross with the record against us. On Wednesday the Son of David opened a door that a register had shut. On Thursday the treasure in the clay jars was God's glory in His face, a face that was struck and then raised. Every kind of writing, and every hand that held the pen, was carrying word of Him.

Weekend Practice

Before Sabbath ends, take two minutes. Open to a page you usually skip, a list, a heading, or a hard warning. Read a few lines slowly and ask God what He wanted you to hear there. If you want to do more later, read Psalm 57, a song David wrote while he was hiding.

Closing Prayer for the Week

Father, thank You for every page You have given us, the songs and the lists, the burdens and the letters. Thank You for the scribes who held the pen, the writers who searched and chose, and the weak messengers who carried Your treasure. Forgive us for skipping what seemed dull and for fearing what seemed hard. Teach us to hear Your voice in every kind of writing and to find hope there, as You intended. As this Sabbath begins, bring us back to Your Son, the One every page was written to show us. In Jesus' name, Amen.
