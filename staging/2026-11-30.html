The Authority of Jesus

Prophetic Authority and RelevanceLesson 10Day 3 of 7

In brief

Water is coming into the boat where Jesus lies asleep. His friends wake Him because they think they're going to die. When the lake falls quiet at His word, they want to know who is in the boat with them. Later, in prayer, Jesus tells His Father what He does with the authority He's been given. The same Jesus the wind obeys gives eternal life to those the Father has given Him.

Yesterday

Aaron spoke words God had given to Moses. Jesus' listeners knew what it sounded like when a teacher passed on something entrusted to him. Jesus startled them by teaching with authority of His own (Matthew 7:29). His friends would hear that same voice speak to a storm.

Historical Context

A Warning Carved in Stone

A visitor could walk into the outer courts of the temple in Jerusalem and still come to a place where he had to stop. A low stone barrier marked the way farther in. A surviving piece of one of its signs, cut in Greek, warns foreigners against crossing it. Who had the right to enter was a serious question in that place. The men who later asked Jesus who gave Him the right to teach had real responsibilities there. They also had time to see what He did under their eyes.

Jesus had overturned the tables of people doing business in the temple courts and called it a house of prayer. Then people who were blind or lame came to Him, and He healed them. Children called out their praise (Matthew 21:12-16). Picture the difference between two people watching. A chief priest could ask who had given Jesus leave to do these things. Someone who had just received sight had another piece of evidence to bring to that question. Both had been there.

Into the Word

The crowd that heard the Sermon on the Mount was astonished at the authority with which Jesus taught (Matthew 7:28-29). The leaders in the temple want Him to say who gave Him that right. Listen to what they discuss when He asks them a question in return.

Jesus knew what was in people (John 2:24-25). He could leave those men with the question they'd declined to answer. In a synagogue at Capernaum, another crowd heard Jesus command an unclean spirit and watched it obey (Mark 1:21-27). Now His own friends hear Him give an order on a lake. See what happens to their question once the fear of drowning is gone.

When Jesus sent the Twelve out in pairs, they had already watched Him speak to a power they couldn't master. Mark traces their authority back to the day He appointed them and forward to the day He sent them out (Mark 3:15 and Mark 6:7). Luke says who gave them the right to command demons and the power to heal (Luke 9:1). Even a heavenly messenger knows where his authority ends. When John fell at an angel's feet, the angel told his fellow servant to worship God (Revelation 19:10). Now hear how Jesus speaks to His Father about the authority the Father has given Him.

Jesus once gave His listeners a picture of how they could receive a gift from Him. A thirsty person could come and drink, and the person who trusted Him would have living water to give (John 7:37 and John 7:38). John explains that Jesus meant the Spirit believers would receive (John 7:39). Imagine being the thirsty one in that crowd. What makes you welcome is the invitation of the One who can give what you need.

Street Level

He Took the Jars Down

A man spends Saturday morning putting up a kitchen shelf. By lunch the tools are back in the box and three jars are lined up on the new board. His sister stops in the doorway and tips her head. He knows that look. He asks her whether she thinks the shelf is straight. She offers to fetch the level. He starts telling her about the crooked tile behind the stove and how nothing in an old house looks quite square. She leaves the level where it is.

Later he catches sight of the shelf from the doorway himself. There's a little more daylight under one end than the other. He takes the jars down, clears a place on the counter, and calls to his sister. This time, when she comes in with the level, he's standing there with a screwdriver in his hand. It was a fair question both times. The first time he wanted her to like what he'd made. This time he's willing to move a screw.

When Jesus asked about John, the leaders weighed what it would cost to say what they believed. On the lake the disciples could admit how little they understood. The man in the kitchen has asked the same question twice, but his sister can tell what's changed before she even sets down the level. The jars are on the counter.

Christ at the center

When Jesus told a paralyzed man his sins were forgiven, the people standing around the mat had no way to see whether it had happened. Then Jesus told him to stand. They could see what that word did, and Jesus said He meant it to show them His authority to forgive (Mark 2:10-11). Long before His arrest, He spoke of laying down His life and taking it up again in obedience to His Father (John 10:17-18). Paul calls what guilty people receive through Jesus a gift of grace (Romans 3:24). The One who told that man to stand would bear the cost of his forgiveness.

Application

Bring to mind one question you would ask Jesus if you were in the boat. Give it to Him in its plainest words. Then name one answer you would find hard to hear. That's a place where an honest prayer can begin.

Discussion Question

What difference do you hear between the leaders' question and the disciples' question, and what would help you bring a hard question of your own to Jesus honestly?

Closing Prayer

Father, Your Son heard men call to Him with the wind in their faces. Give us that freedom to call His name when words fail us. Where we've wanted Your approval for a choice already made, help us listen long enough to hear Your answer. Thank You for sending Jesus to bring life within reach of people who need it. Teach us to trust the hands that bear the cost of loving us. In Jesus' name, Amen.
