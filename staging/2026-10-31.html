The Prophetic Writings

The Prophetic WritingsLesson 6Day 1 of 7

In brief

On the plains of Moab, Moses writes down a song in a single day and teaches it to the whole nation. God has told him why. When hard years come, the song will still be on their children's lips. Centuries later, Paul quotes the end of that song to believers in Rome. Everything God had written long ago, from songs to records to warnings, was written for our instruction, so that we would have hope.

Last week

Last week ended with a girl's backpack lying open and empty, after her new parents had said home every way they could. God has spoken that way too, aloud, in pictures, and in writing. This week opens the writing and asks what kind of writing it is.

Historical Context

An Emperor Had Sent Some of Them Away

Some years before Paul wrote to Rome, the emperor Claudius ordered the Jews to leave the city. A Jewish couple named Priscilla and Aquila were among those who had to go. Paul met them in Corinth and worked beside them making tents (Acts 18:1-3). By the time he wrote Romans, they were back in Rome, and a church met in their home (Romans 16:3-5).

The believers in Rome were a mix of Jews and Gentiles, and they disagreed about food and holy days (Romans 14:1-6). Many scholars think the years of the expulsion left the house churches more Gentile than before, which may have sharpened the strain when Jewish believers came home. So near the end of his letter, Paul reaches for the old Scriptures. He wants a divided church to see that God had planned all along for both groups to praise Him together. Those old writings belonged to everyone in the room.

This Week’s Memory Text

Paul has just quoted an old psalm and said it was about Christ. Before he quotes four more, he stops to explain why the old writings still speak.

This Week’s Road Map

  • ☩Sunday: The Call to Write. God told Moses and Jeremiah to write, and He aimed what they wrote at rescue and forgiveness.
  • ☩Monday: Literary Genres and Styles. A travel log, a poem, a courtroom complaint, and a letter each carry God's word in their own way.
  • ☩Tuesday: Literary Assistants. Baruch and Tertius held the pen, and every word still belonged to the one who sent it.
  • ☩Wednesday: Literary Sources. Inspired writers searched out sayings, records, and letters, and God guided what they kept.
  • ☩Thursday: Divine Message and Human Imperfections. God put His treasure in breakable people so that everyone could see whose power it was.
  • ☩Friday: Further Thought. We gather the week's questions about how human writing carries God's word.

Christ at the center

Look at the line just before the memory text. Paul quotes a psalm about insults that fell on someone who loved God's house, and he says the line is about Christ (Romans 15:3 and Psalm 69:9). Jesus read the old writings the same way. After He rose, He told His disciples that everything written about Him in the Law of Moses, the Prophets, and the Psalms had to be fulfilled (Luke 24:44). A few lines after the memory text, Paul quotes Isaiah's promise that the nations will put their hope in Him (Romans 15:12). So carry a question into this week. These writings came as songs and lists, through scribes and sources, from weak and ordinary people. Why does every kind keep leading to one Person? Watch for Him. He is the hope they were written to give.

Application

Before the week begins, take two minutes. Write Romans 15:4 on a card or in your phone. Under it, name one place where you need endurance or encouragement right now. Bring that one need with you to each day's reading.

Opening Question for the Week

Paul says the old Scriptures were written to give hope. What kind of biblical writing has helped you most in a hard time (a psalm, a story, a promise, a letter)? What did it give you that you needed?

Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, thank You that You did not leave Your word behind in the past. You had it written for us, in songs and records, warnings and promises, so that we would have hope. This week, as we look at how Your word was written, open it to us. Give us endurance where we are worn down and encouragement where we are discouraged. Help us read each kind of writing as what it is. And in every line, show us Your Son, the hope the nations were promised. In Jesus' name, Amen.
