Further Thought

The Creator SpeaksLesson 1Day 7 of 7

Week in Review

This week began with a Voice in the dark and ended with a Shepherd whose sheep know His voice. In between, people hid, God came looking, a gate closed, and the talking never stopped.

  • ☩Sabbath: The Creator Speaks. While kings read sheep livers for a word from heaven, Israel's God spoke first, in many pieces through prophets and at last by His Son.
  • ☩Sunday: Communication in Eden. God made living images, blessed them, and talked to them before they had done any work.
  • ☩Monday: Hiding From God. A serpent misquoted God, two people ate, and fig leaves and trees hid nothing from the One who fills heaven and earth.
  • ☩Tuesday: God Seeks Humanity. God's first words after sin were a question, and the same seeking showed up in a running father and a look across a courtyard.
  • ☩Wednesday: Expelled From the Garden. Adam and Eve heard a promise before their sentence, God clothed the guilty, and the guarded gate was not His last word.
  • ☩Thursday: God's Spokespeople. Creation, prophets, and Scripture each carried more of God's voice, until the Son could say that seeing Him is seeing the Father.

Historical Context

Israel's Neighbors Strained to Hear Heaven

Line up the week's backgrounds and one picture forms. Diviners bent over sheep livers, hunting for marks the gods might have left. Kings set up stone images of themselves in distant lands, images that never heard a word from the throne. A cobra on Pharaoh's crown claimed the gods' protection for his rule. In Babylon's story the gods sent a flood because people were too loud. Royal messengers stood outside city walls and announced what the king had said.

Into that world the Scriptures set a God who speaks before He is asked, talks to His images, walks toward the guilty, and sends His messengers with promises as well as warnings. The cherubim at His door were real, and so was His plan to open it. Israel's neighbors strained to hear heaven. Israel's God kept starting the conversation.

What This Week Clarifies

The Question Was Never for Him

Monday was blunt. No one can hide in secret places from the God who fills heaven and earth, and nothing in creation is hidden from His sight. Then Tuesday opened with that same God walking through the trees, asking where Adam was. If He already knew, why ask? Was the question a courtroom trick, or was something kinder going on?

The Wall Went Up From Our Side

Sunday showed a God who blessed people and spoke with them at eye level. By Wednesday His voice was thunder, and a fence stood around the mountain. A reader could fairly wonder whether God had changed. Did He grow colder after Eden? And when heaven feels quiet today, is that because He has stepped back from us?

Every Piece Belonged to One Face

Sabbath and Thursday both leaned on one sentence. God spoke through the prophets in many pieces, and in these last days He has spoken by His Son. That raises a fair question. If the Son is God's fullest word, what happens to all the other voices? Are the prophets out of date? Has God stopped talking since?

Still Speaking

She Sat Down in the Field and Sang

Imagine a dog that slips its collar in a thunderstorm and runs. By the third day it is living at the edge of a field, thin, and afraid of everything that moves. Its owner finally spots it and comes running across the grass, shouting its name. The dog bolts. People who search for lost pets say this is common. A panicked dog may run even from the person it loves most. Nothing about her has changed. The fear is all on the dog's side, and it has made her footsteps sound like danger.

A volunteer tells her what to do, and it is the opposite of what she wants. Do not chase. Do not even call. Sit down, look away, and let him hear you. So every evening she walks to the edge of that field, sits in the grass with his old blanket, and sings the songs she sings in the kitchen. She stays until dark. She comes back the next night, and the one after that. She is always there first. One evening the dog creeps close enough to catch the scent of the blanket, and something older than the fear wakes up. He knows that voice.

That has been the shape of this whole week. Adam heard God coming and ran (Genesis 3:8). God did not run him down. He asked a question and waited. When His voice at Sinai was more than sinful people could bear, He promised them prophets (Deuteronomy 18:16-18) and spoke one piece at a time, and then in words on a page that anyone can hold. At last He came as a Man who sat down to eat with sinners and said His sheep would know His voice (John 10:27).

If heaven has seemed quiet, that is not proof He has stopped coming. If God has felt like Someone hunting you, look again at how He comes. He is sitting at the edge of your field. He has been there every evening. And the voice is one you were made to know.

Christ at the Center of the Week

Every day this week turned out to be about one Person. Sabbath said God's fullest word is His Son. Sunday showed what people were made for, and Jesus is the image of the invisible God, the one human who never stopped listening to the Father. Monday left us in the trees holding leaves, and He was stripped and shamed in the open so that being seen by God could become safe. Tuesday heard God ask where are you, and Jesus came asking it in person, at a well, among the tombs, and across a courtyard. Wednesday closed a gate, and His death tore the curtain and opened a new and living way. Thursday lined up God's messengers, and He alone could say that whoever has seen Him has seen the Father. The voice Adam ran from has a face now. It is the face of Someone who would rather die than lose the people He made. He did.

Weekend Practice

Before Sabbath ends, take two quiet minutes and answer God's first question out loud. Tell Him where you are, in one or two honest sentences. Then read Hebrews 1:1-2 once and thank Him for one way He has spoken to you this week. If you want to do more, tell someone at your table what it was.

Closing Prayer for the Week

Father, You spoke first, and You have never stopped. Thank You for blessing us before we were useful, for coming to look for us when we hid, for the promise You spoke before the gate closed, and for every messenger You sent east of the garden. Thank You most of all for Your Son, in whom we have seen Your face. Forgive our fig leaves and our finger pointing. Take down what we have built between us. As this Sabbath comes, quiet the other voices. Let us hear the Shepherd call our names, and let us not be afraid. Keep us in Your hand until the day we are where You are. In Jesus' name, Amen.
