Mediating God's Authority

Prophetic Authority and RelevanceLesson 10Day 2 of 7

In brief

Two brothers stand before the elders of Israel. Aaron does the talking. Moses has given him the words. When the people hear that the LORD has seen what is being done to them, they bow down and worship (Exodus 4:29-31). Moses gets to see it all. Back at the burning bush he had asked God to send somebody else. The voice the people heard was Aaron's. The promise they could count on was God's.

Yesterday

Nathan's story brought David face to face with his own sin. That prophet could speak plainly to a king because the LORD had sent him. At a burning bush, we meet a man who hears that he has been sent and still wonders what on earth he can say.

Historical Context

The Work Moses Left Behind

On the wall of an Egyptian official's tomb, a worker bends over a wooden mold to shape a brick. Others carry wet mud or stack bricks to dry. The painting in Rekhmire's tomb shows captives among the workers. An order for more bricks reached all the way down to the hands filling each mold. The Israelites knew what it was to spend a day making bricks for people who could demand another day's work tomorrow (Exodus 1:13-14).

Moses had seen an Egyptian beating a Hebrew at work. He killed the man, and Pharaoh later sought his life (Exodus 2:11-15). Before Moses fled, another Hebrew had asked who put him in charge (Exodus 2:14). In Midian, the women he helped at a well took him for an Egyptian (Exodus 2:16-22). He made his home there while the people in Egypt still groaned over their work (Exodus 2:23-25). Years later, out with the sheep, Moses turned toward a bush that burned without burning up (Exodus 3:1-3). What could he take back to those workers that he hadn't had the first time?

Into the Word

Pharaoh will hear that he must release people whose labor he commands. The Israelites may ask why they should listen to a man who once fled Egypt. Moses is still out with the sheep when God tells him who will go with him.

Moses has his message for the elders. Now he tells God what he thinks of his own voice.

The brothers first gather the elders. Aaron passes on what the LORD told Moses, and the people bow down when they hear that God has seen their suffering (Exodus 4:27-31). Later, after Pharaoh has already refused to let the people go, God describes Moses' place before the king (Exodus 5:1-2).

Street Level

The Card Stayed Down

There's a half-moon of bare wood on the arm of an old red rocking chair. A retired teacher spots it from the back of a school auction. She read picture books to first graders in that chair for years. Now she'd like to put it beside her own window and read to her granddaughter there. The man taking bids keeps looking right past her in the crowd. Her friend on the aisle has a clearer view, so the teacher slips her the number card. “Watch me. If I nod, hold it up.”

Up goes the card. Someone across the room bids a little more. The friend catches the teacher's eye, sees another nod, and raises it again. One more bid across the room. The teacher shakes her head. Her friend can picture the rocker by that window and the little girl climbing into her grandmother's lap. The card stays down.

People would see Aaron speaking and hear his voice. They could trust him with God's words because God had given them to Moses to pass on. The auctioneer would see the same number if the friend raised the card once more. But she's seen that little shake of the head. Her hand stays still. Every bid she made belonged to the teacher.

Christ at the center

When the elders bow down, they're still in Egypt. The people God already calls His own will need someone who can actually bring them out. Hebrews remembers Moses with honor as a faithful servant in God's house, and it gives Jesus the place of the Son over that house (Hebrews 3:1-6). Consider what the Son did for people held in slavery by the fear of death. He took on our flesh and blood and went through death Himself to set us free (Hebrews 2:14-15). God raised Him from the dead (Hebrews 13:20). A friend sitting beside a hospital bed may barely get the words Jesus is alive past the lump in her throat. The news is just as true in a whisper.

Application

Bring to mind a piece of advice you might give someone you care about. In a sentence to yourself, name the part you can trace to Scripture. Own the rest as your best judgment. You can care for someone deeply and still say, “Here's what I think.”

Discussion Question

What do you hear in God's choice to keep Moses and give him Aaron, and where might accepting help make you more willing to do something you already know is right?

Closing Prayer

Father, You knew what Moses struggled to say. You also knew the people who needed to hear from You. When it's our turn to speak, give us enough care for the person listening to choose honest words. Give us courage to own our opinions and patience to listen when another voice falters. When a good thing lies plainly before us and we feel unequal to it, help us receive the person willing to stand beside us. In Jesus' name, Amen.
