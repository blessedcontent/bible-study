Female Prophets and Young Pastoral Associates

New Testament ProphetsLesson 4Day 5 of 7

In brief

A couple carries a baby into the temple courts to do what the law requires. An old widow who is always there walks up at that very moment, thanks God, and starts telling people who this Child is. Years later four sisters prophesy in a house by the sea, and Paul tells a younger coworker not to let anyone look down on him. God gives His gifts without checking age or sex first. The authority was never in the messenger. It was in the message.

Yesterday

Yesterday two prophets carried a letter to Antioch and stayed to strengthen the church. Paul said prophecy builds up, encourages, and comforts. That is what the gift does. Today we look at who received it, and the list is wider than we might expect.

Historical Context

A Young Man Could Be Nearly Forty

When Paul tells Timothy not to let anyone despise his youth, it is easy to picture a teenager. The timeline says otherwise. Timothy joined Paul's team on the second missionary journey, around AD 49 or 50 (Acts 16:1-3). The first letter to him is usually dated to the early or middle 60s. By then Timothy had more than a dozen years of travel and church troubleshooting behind him. He was most likely in his thirties.

In that world he could still be called young. Irenaeus, a church leader writing about AD 180, remarks that everyone agrees the first stage of life covers thirty years and extends to forty. Honor went to gray hair. The very word elder shows who was expected to lead. So a leader in his thirties might well be looked down on by people twice his age. Today's passages cover the whole range. Anna is at least eighty-four. Philip's four daughters are unmarried and living in his home. Timothy is a grown man who can still be treated as a youngster. The Spirit worked through all of them.

Into the Word

Mary and Joseph have brought their firstborn to the temple in Jerusalem, as the law required (Luke 2:22-24). An old man named Simeon has just taken the baby in his arms and blessed God. Before the family can move on, someone else steps up.

Years later Paul is on his way to Jerusalem and stops in the port of Caesarea. He stays with Philip the evangelist, one of the seven chosen years earlier in Jerusalem (Acts 6:5). The story is told as we at this point, so Luke appears to be traveling with Paul, and he notes one thing about the household (Acts 21:8).

The Spirit's gifts also reached a younger generation of leaders, and prophecy had a hand in that. Paul calls both Timothy and Titus his true child in the faith (1 Timothy 1:2 and Titus 1:4). True means genuine, the real thing. He is not their father by birth. He is vouching for them.

Street Level

The Umpire Was Sixteen

Picture a Saturday morning at a youth baseball field. The umpire behind the plate is sixteen. The league director trained him in March, signed his umpire's card, and told him why. You see the ball well, and you don't scare. That is the job. By the second inning a father behind the backstop is telling the bleachers that the kid is too young to be back there. Then a coach three times his age walks out to argue a called third strike.

The boy could pull out his card and demand some respect. It would not move that coach an inch. He does the other thing. It caught the corner, Coach, he says, and he pulls his mask back down. In the next half inning he calls the same pitch a strike against the other team. He does it that way every Saturday. The zone is the same in the first inning and the last. It is the same for the dugout that is kind to him and the one that is not. He hustles down the line on every close play. He never raises his voice. On the bad mornings he sits in the parking lot, takes out the card, and remembers that he did not give himself this job.

By August the coaches have stopped coming out. Nobody announced that he had earned it. They had simply watched him for four months. Not every summer ends that way, and Paul never told Timothy his would. His counsel is about the part a person can control. The strike zone was never the boy's opinion, and his age had nothing to do with where it was. Being young did not make his calls right, either. Getting them right did, and a whole summer of people could see it.

Today’s World

The Proof Did Not Ask Her Age

Hannah Cairo grew up in the Bahamas and was schooled at home. By eleven she had finished calculus with online lessons. As a teenager she enrolled in a graduate math course at the University of California, Berkeley. One homework problem was a simpler form of a question that had stood open for about forty years. She kept going past the homework and built an example showing that the answer was no. She was seventeen. She posted her proof online in February 2025.

Then she applied to ten graduate programs with no high school diploma and no college degree. Quanta Magazine reports that six turned her down over the missing degree. Two more said yes, and then administrators overruled them. Two universities took her, and she began a doctorate at the University of Maryland that fall. Meanwhile the people who knew the problem best were reading her paper. One of them had called it his favorite problem for nearly forty years. His word for her work was wow.

A proof is an unusual kind of message. It can be checked line by line, and the checking never asks who wrote it. Her age added nothing to the proof and took nothing from it. A degree would not have made it more correct. No mathematician is a prophet, and being turned down proves nothing by itself. But here a message was weighed for what it said, and so far it has held.

Christ at the center

Anna's whole recorded message has one subject. She spoke about the Child. Turn the page in Luke and the Child is twelve, sitting in those same temple courts among the teachers, and everyone who hears Him is amazed at His understanding (Luke 2:46-47). Years later critics would throw His age at Him. You are not yet fifty years old (John 8:57). In His hometown they shrugged Him off as the carpenter (Mark 6:3). Jesus knows what it is to be looked down on for the wrong reasons. And He is the pattern Timothy was told to become. His speech, conduct, love, faith, and purity were without a flaw, and He gave that life up for people who despised Him. Anna announced redemption over a baby. The Man He became paid for it.

Application

Take two minutes. Ask God to bring to mind one believer you have been quick to overlook, perhaps because they are young, or old, or quiet. Thank Him for one thing He has done through that person. If you are the one who feels overlooked, read 1 Timothy 4:12 as if it were addressed to you.

Discussion Question

Anna was past eighty, and Timothy was looked down on for being young. Where are you tempted to weigh a person's age or background before what they say (at church, at work, online)? What would setting an example look like at your stage of life?

Closing Prayer

Father, thank You that You do not sort us the way we sort each other. Thank You for Anna's long years of worship and prayer, and for four daughters who prophesied, though Luke kept none of their words. Forgive us for the people we have overlooked. Steady those among us who feel too young, too old, or too ordinary to be of use. Remind them what You have said. Make each of us a pattern worth following, in our speech and conduct, in love, faith, and purity. In Jesus' name, Amen.
