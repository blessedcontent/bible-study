Prophetic Authority and RelevanceLesson 10Day 7 of 7

Week in Review

When God's word finds something we'd rather keep out of sight, the way Jesus uses His authority gives us a reason to stay and listen.

  • ☩Sabbath: Prophetic Authority and Relevance. David could put a price on a stolen lamb before he could name what he had taken from Uriah.
  • ☩Sunday: Mediating God's Authority. Aaron did the talking, and the elders bowed in worship when they heard that God had seen their suffering.
  • ☩Monday: The Authority of Jesus. The disciples asked who could command a storm, and Jesus' prayer let us hear what He intends to give with such authority.
  • ☩Tuesday: Biblical Authority. Josiah read the book aloud to his people, Jehoiakim tried to silence a reading, and a jailer who heard about Jesus washed two prisoners' wounds.
  • ☩Wednesday: Organizational Authority. Some widows were missing meals, and a whole church took their complaint seriously enough to put seven people in charge.
  • ☩Thursday: Canonical and Noncanonical Prophets. Jeroboam received ten pieces of a prophet's cloak and a promise about his family's future that called for a lifetime of listening.

Historical Context

Alone in a Field and Heard by a City

Ahijah and Jeroboam were alone in the countryside when the prophet tore his cloak. There were two people there, and one was doing the speaking. Later, when the people made Jeroboam king, the writer of Kings recalled the word God had given through Ahijah (1 Kings 11:29-39 and 1 Kings 12:15-20). Much of Israel came to live with the result of a conversation those Israelites had never heard.

In Jerusalem it mattered that a complaint got a public hearing. The Twelve called the disciples together when the Greek-speaking widows were being overlooked. The gathering could choose people it trusted, and the apostles could put the work in their hands (Acts 6:1-7). A crowd couldn't have made Ahijah a prophet by a show of hands. Yet a crowd willing to listen could change who received food in Jerusalem. People who hear a word have decisions of their own to make.

What This Week Clarifies

The Brother Who Did the Talking

When someone says God has sent him, there are two ways to give him too quick an answer. We might take his confidence as proof. Or his ordinary voice, one we've heard a hundred times over supper, might make it hard to believe God would send him at all. Aaron spoke to Israel in a brother's voice.

The King Who Let the Reader Finish

Suppose we have done the careful work and found that the words really are there on the page. A harder question may still be waiting. David understood the injustice in Nathan's story. Jehoiakim heard enough of the scroll to know he wanted it gone. What becomes of our good judgment when the person under examination is us?

What the Son Does With His Hands

It takes some courage to let words like these get close. If we tell the truth about the thing we've been defending, what will the One who knows it all do with us? Before Jesus went to His death, His friends got to hear Him speak about them to His Father.

Still Speaking

The Last Letter of Her Name

An aunt is nearly finished with a quilt for her little niece. The blue squares came from a shirt the child loved so much she wore through both elbows. In one corner the aunt has stitched the child's name by hand. She spreads the quilt across the kitchen table when her sister comes by. Her sister runs a finger over the letters. There should be an e at the end. The aunt has carried an older relative's spelling in her head for years. She's sewn it that way in tiny stitches, the last letter snug against the border.

For a moment all she can see is how long it took to finish the border. She could fold that corner under. She could explain that the two names sound alike. Her sister pulls a chair up beside her. The little girl has just learned to write her name, she says. She's going to look for every letter. She offers to hold the cloth steady while her sister makes room for the e. Now the aunt can see the corner as the child will see it, one finger moving slowly from letter to letter. She reaches for her scissors and snips the first stitch.

There's a kindness we can miss when a word touches something we've worked hard on. In Jerusalem, somebody had given the food. Somebody had done the work of handing it out. It might have hurt to hear that a widow was still going hungry. But hearing her gave those people a chance to do what they'd meant to do when they first brought their gifts. That's one reason to stay at the table a little longer when Scripture calls us to look again. There may be a person on the other side of that correction who will finally receive what we hoped to give.

Christ at the Center of the Week

Nathan could tell David that God had put away his sin. Imagine hearing those words just after admitting whose death you had arranged. Where could a guilty person go with that much truth? Hebrews invites us to Jesus, the high priest who knows our weakness and brings us near for mercy (Hebrews 4:13-16). Moses served faithfully in God's house. Jesus is the Son over that house, close enough for frightened men to wake Him in a boat. The Lord who spoke and stilled the lake took up a towel at supper. Then He gave His life for people who needed forgiveness, and God raised Him from the dead. That's why two prisoners could speak about salvation to the man who had locked them up. They knew whom he could trust. Two of John the Baptist's own disciples heard him speak of Jesus and went to follow Him. When Jesus saw them, He invited them to come with Him (John 1:35-39). We can bring Him the part of our story that's hardest to tell. We've come to the One those messengers wanted us to meet.

Weekend Practice

As Sabbath begins, or the next time you have a quiet minute, recall one sentence from the week. Tell Jesus what makes it easy or hard to hear. Picture one person who might feel the difference if you took it to heart. What could that look like in the coming week?

Closing Prayer for the Week

Father, when a word of Yours stops us short, give us enough trust to stay with You there. Help us hear the neighbor who has tried to tell us that something hurts. Where we have been defending ourselves, give us words as simple as the truth. Where we're afraid the truth will cost us Your love, bring to mind Your Son and the mercy He invites us to receive. Let someone who needs our care have reason to be glad that we listened. In Jesus' name, Amen.
