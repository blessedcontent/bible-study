New Testament ProphetsLesson 4Day 7 of 7

Week in Review

This week a long quiet ended. Altar stones had waited in storage for a prophet, and then God sent His Son and gave His Spirit to a whole church. We met the Prophet who is more than a prophet, the witnesses under the house, voices that build, and fakes Jesus warned about.

  • ☩Sabbath: New Testament Prophets. Paul set three lines over the week, different gifts and the same Spirit, the same Lord, the same God.
  • ☩Sunday: Jesus as Prophet. The crowds guessed a prophet, Peter said the Christ, and John called Him the Word the prophets had only carried.
  • ☩Monday: Apostles and Prophets. God's household rests on the witness of apostles and prophets, and Christ Jesus is the cornerstone.
  • ☩Tuesday: Functions of New Testament Prophets. Prophets spoke to build up, encourage, and comfort, and Judas and Silas stayed to strengthen Antioch.
  • ☩Wednesday: Female Prophets and Young Pastoral Associates. Anna, Philip's four daughters, and Timothy showed that the authority is in the message, not the messenger.
  • ☩Thursday: False Prophets in the New Testament. Jesus warned that fakes would be impressive, pointed us to fruit, and gave us armor for standing.

Historical Context

The Waiting Ended and the Weighing Began

Gather the week's settings. Altar stones in storage, waiting for a prophet. A delegation with three names on its list. A wonder of the world resting on charcoal and wool. Two men carrying a letter to Antioch. A pastor in his thirties who could still be called young. Crowds following a confident man into the desert.

Put them together and a change shows. The writer of 1 Maccabees ached over silence and wondered when a prophet would come. After Pentecost the problem turned over. Now there were many voices, in house churches, in port cities, and on hillsides. Some were sent by God, and some were not. The first Christians did not have to wait for a word. They had to weigh the words they heard. They did it with a Person at the center and a foundation under their feet.

What This Week Clarifies

The Son Spoke, and Then He Gave Prophets

Sabbath opened with a line from Hebrews that seemed to close the subject. God spoke in the past through prophets, and now He has spoken by His Son. Sunday agreed. The prophets were a lamp, and Jesus is the day. Then the rest of the week filled up with prophets anyway. If the Son is God's full word, why did the prophets keep coming?

One Foundation, Many Voices Above It

Monday put the apostles and prophets under the whole house as its foundation. Then Tuesday had Paul telling the Corinthians they could all prophesy in turn, and Wednesday showed four daughters prophesying in one home, though Luke records none of their words. Do all these voices stand on the same level? If the foundation is already laid, what standing do the later voices have?

A Sign Is Only as Good as Where It Points

On Sunday a crowd saw Jesus feed thousands and said, this is the Prophet. The sign moved them toward the truth. On Thursday Jesus warned that false prophets would perform great signs and wonders too. So which is it? Should a wonder move us toward belief, or put us on guard?

Still Speaking

Different Hands Painted the Same Blue

Imagine climbing a mountain you have never been on. At the trailhead there is a wooden box of paper maps, drawn by the club that first cut this trail, and you put one in your pocket. After that the trail speaks in paint. Every few hundred feet there is a blue mark on a tree. They were not all painted by one person. Some are crisp, laid on with a stencil. Some wobble. One sits low on the trunk, about as high as a twelve-year-old can reach. A scout. A retired couple. A grandmother who took the flat stretch by the creek. You will never meet them. They all point the same way.

Near the top the trail forks in fog. On the left stands a handsome sign, carved and varnished, with a wide, smooth path behind it. On the right is one faded blue mark on a crooked birch. The sign is more impressive. That tells you nothing about where its path goes. So you take out the map, and the map says right. The handsome path, you learn later, ends at a fence around a lodge that was never built.

A carved sign is not wrong for being handsome, and the club has put up a few fine ones. A faded mark is not right for being humble. The question is whether a mark agrees with the map and gets people up the mountain. That is how we weigh the many voices around us. The witness of the apostles and prophets is the map in our pocket. It came from those who were shown the way first, and no later mark redraws it.

Do not miss the better half. Nobody paints a trail for her own sake. Every one of those volunteers was sent out with a can of blue by someone who wanted you to arrive. Paul says the same of the risen Christ. It was He who gave some to be prophets (Ephesians 4:11), until we all reach the full measure of His stature. Four sisters Luke never quotes. Two men who stayed in Antioch to talk. And before the cross, a widow of eighty-four speaking about the Child. They are not the summit. They are God's kindness along the way. So the week does not end in suspicion of every sign. It ends in thanks for every hand that held a brush.

Christ at the Center of the Week

Every day this week turned out to be about one Person. On Sabbath He was the Lord behind every gift, and the Spirit takes what is His and makes it known. Sunday showed the Prophet Moses promised and the Word through whom all things were made, nailed to wood He had made. Monday named Him the first Apostle, sent by the Father, and the rejected stone God made the cornerstone. On Tuesday He built, said take courage, wept at a tomb, and gave gifts as He ascended. Wednesday found Him as the Child Anna announced, then at twelve in the temple, then looked down on as a man, the pattern Timothy was told to become. Thursday watched Him answer the tempter with it is written, and heard Him tell His friends the danger in advance. Long ago a ruined altar's stones waited for a prophet to say what should be done with them. God answered with more than a prophet. He sent a living Stone, and Peter says we are being built on Him into a house (1 Peter 2:4-5). He is still building.

Weekend Practice

Before Sabbath ends, take two minutes with 1 Corinthians 12:4-6. Name one voice that has built you up in Christ, and thank God for it. Then name one voice you have given more weight than it has earned, and ask Him to help you hold it against His word. To do more later, thank that first person.

Closing Prayer for the Week

Father, thank You that the silence ended. You sent Your Son, and You have kept caring for us through the people He gives. Thank You for apostles and prophets whose witness lies under our feet, and for the quiet voices that have built us up, encouraged us, and comforted us. Keep Christ at the center of all we hear. Make us slow to be dazzled and quick to be grateful. Guard us from voices You did not send, and from contempt for the ones You did. As this Sabbath begins, build us together on Your Son until we look like Him. In Jesus' name, Amen.
