Drama, Symbols, Parables

Revelation and InspirationLesson 5Day 6 of 7

In brief

In Babylonia, an exiled priest draws a city on a clay brick and builds a tiny siege around it. Then he lies down on his side, day after day. Six centuries later Jesus sits in a boat and tells a crowd about seed. God did not only speak and write His word. He showed it, through acted-out signs, symbols, and stories, so that people who had stopped listening might still see.

Yesterday

Yesterday God told Moses to write, and John wrote down even his own mistakes. Writing makes a message last. But some messages God did not only say or write. He had them acted out.

Historical Context

Jerusalem Still Stood When He Drew It on Clay

In 597 BC, Babylon's army carried King Jehoiachin and thousands of Judah's leading people into exile (2 Kings 24:14-16). Ezekiel, a priest, was among them. In the fifth year of Jehoiachin's exile, by the Kebar canal in Babylonia, God called him to be a prophet (Ezekiel 1:1-3). The Kebar is usually placed near the old city of Nippur, where a clay tablet centuries older than Ezekiel shows the city's walls, gates, and canals. In that land people wrote on clay and even drew maps on it.

Back home, Jerusalem still stood. Prophets there were promising that the exiles would come home within two years (Jeremiah 28:2-4). So when Ezekiel drew the city on clay and laid siege to it, he was acting out what few around him wanted to believe. Jerusalem fell about 586 BC. Looking back, the writer of Chronicles said God had sent His messengers again and again because He had compassion on His people. They mocked the messengers until there was no remedy (2 Chronicles 36:15-21).

Into the Word

So far this week God's word has been spoken and written. Today it is shown. Sometimes God told a prophet to act a message out in front of people who had stopped listening to words. Ezekiel was told that a sign might help them understand, even though they were a rebellious house (Ezekiel 12:3). One of his signs began with a brick.

Ezekiel was not alone. Hosea's sign was his own household. God told him to marry Gomer, and their children's names announced judgment on the northern kingdom, Jezreel, No Compassion, and Not My People (Hosea 1:2-9). It was a sign for Israel in its own day, not a pattern for anyone's marriage now. And later in the book, God promises to call Not My People His people again (Hosea 2:23).

Some of God's pictures came as visions. A king dreamed of kingdoms as a great statue, and Daniel explained it (Daniel 2:31-45). Daniel himself saw kingdoms as beasts and horns (Daniel 7 and Daniel 8), and he heard of kings locked in long conflict (Daniel 11). Revelation pictures hostile powers as beasts (Revelation 13). Often the vision comes with its own explanation, as when Daniel is told that four great beasts are four kings (Daniel 7:17). That teaches us to let Scripture interpret its pictures instead of inventing our own. Jesus used pictures for Himself too.

Street Level

They Burned a Living Room in the Parking Lot

Every October a neighborhood fire station holds an open house. For years the firefighters have handed out flyers about smoke alarms, and too many alarms in town still have dead batteries. So this year they build a small living room in the parking lot, with a couch, a lamp, curtains, and a rug. Then they set a wastebasket on fire beside the couch.

For a moment it looks harmless. Then the curtains catch, black smoke rolls across the ceiling, and the whole room bursts into flame. The crowd steps back. Someone films it on a phone. A man says his own couch is newer than that one. The captain takes the microphone. That couch is brand new, she says. That room is yours. In a real fire you may have as little as two minutes to get out.

Some families go home and test their alarms that night. Others post the video and forget it. God's signs worked like that. He sent them to people who would not listen, and He said what they meant, but He did not force anyone to take them home. The firefighters burned a living room so that no one would have to lose a real one.

Today’s World

The Lungs on the Billboard Turned Black

In April 2023, local groups and the city's municipal corporation put up an unusual billboard at a busy junction in Amritsar, India. They called it the Billboard that Breathes. It held a pair of chalk-white lungs made from the kind of air filter used in operating rooms, and fans pulled the street's air through them the way a person breathes. In less than two weeks the white had turned brown, then black.

The idea had started years earlier. Brikesh Singh, one of its creators, explained why in 2021. Air-quality numbers are just jargon to most people, he wrote, and most people believe that if they cannot see something, it does not exist. The first lungs went up in Bengaluru in 2018. A pair at a Delhi hospital turned black in six days. In Amritsar, residents said the speed of the change showed the city needed more air monitors.

The filters are not lungs, and a billboard cannot show what is inside any one person's chest. Its builders had to say what it meant. Still, it let people see what the numbers had been saying all along. Ezekiel's brick met the same problem. The warnings had already been spoken, and people were not taking them in.

Christ at the center

Ezekiel lay on his side and bore his people's iniquity as a sign, for a set number of days. Jesus did more than picture it. He bore our sins in His body on the cross (1 Peter 2:24). The night before, He gave His own acted sign. He broke bread and said it was His body, given for His disciples (Luke 22:19). The next day the sign became the real thing. Hebrews says the old sanctuary was a copy and shadow, and Jesus is the high priest in the true one (Hebrews 8:1-6). Even after He returned to heaven, He kept speaking in pictures. To a church that thought it needed nothing, He offered gold, white clothes, and salve for their eyes (Revelation 3:14-18). Ezekiel lay down for a sign. Jesus laid down His life.

Application

Take two minutes. Pick one picture Jesus used for Himself (bread, light, or a vine). Hold it in mind and ask Him what it says He wants to be for you today. If you like, write the word down where you will see it tonight.

Discussion Question

God used acted-out signs, symbols, and stories, not only plain statements. Why do you think a picture can reach us when a direct warning does not? Which picture in Scripture has stayed with you longest?

Closing Prayer

Father, You have spoken to us in so many ways, and You kept sending messengers when people stopped listening. Thank You for prophets who carried Your word in their own bodies and homes because You had compassion. Open our eyes to see what Your pictures show us. Make our hearts good ground for Your word. Keep us from arguing about the bread while we stay hungry. And thank You for Jesus, who did not only show us a sign but gave Himself. In Jesus' name, Amen.
