Hiding From God

The Creator SpeaksLesson 1Day 3 of 7

In brief

Two people crouch among the trees, holding fig leaves together, listening to footsteps they never had a reason to fear until today. A serpent asks what God really said. A doubt becomes a bite. Opened eyes find only shame, and the sound of God walking sends them running for cover. Scripture is blunt about how well that works. No one hides from the God who fills heaven and earth. Hiding never takes us out of God's sight. It only takes us out of the conversation.

Yesterday

Yesterday God made living images and did something no king ever did with a statue. He blessed them and talked to them. His command about the garden's trees was mostly permission, with one boundary that treated them as free. Today someone else starts talking in the garden.

Historical Context

The Snake Sat on Pharaoh's Crown

Look at the gold mask of Tutankhamun, or at almost any statue of an Egyptian king, and you will find a cobra rearing up from the forehead, hood spread, ready to strike. It is known as the uraeus. It stood for the goddess who guarded the king and for his claim to rule by divine right. Anyone who had lived in Egypt had seen that snake on walls, thrones, and crowns. Across Egypt and Canaan, serpents were tied to wisdom, protection, healing, and danger all at once.

Genesis never tells us to read its serpent through Egypt. Still, Israel heard this story as a people brought out of slavery under that crown. To them a talking serpent was no cartoon. The snake was the badge of a throne that claimed the gods' backing and protection. So when a serpent opens the conversation in the garden (Genesis 3) by questioning God's word, the first hearers had reason to hear a challenge to God's authority. Later Scripture names the voice behind it, that ancient serpent called the devil and Satan (Revelation 12:9). A rival has walked into the garden, and he has come to talk.

Into the Word

Every word Adam and Eve had heard so far had been true. God spoke, and it was so. He called things good, and they were. So the two of them had no practice with a voice that sounded friendly and was lying. John would later write that God is love (1 John 4:8), and love had given them a real choice (Genesis 2:15-17). Today a second voice arrives to tell them what that choice means. Watch what it does to the way they hear the first one.

Slow down on the two verses at the center. The promise was that they would be like God, knowing good and evil (Genesis 3:5). The irony hurts, because they were already like God. They were made in His image. What the serpent offered was likeness without listening, wisdom grabbed and not received. Eve looked, and the tree seemed good, beautiful, and useful (Genesis 3:6). Nothing about the fruit looked like death. It rarely does.

So they hid among the trees. Centuries later, God asked a question of His own about hiding places.

Street Level

The Screen Door Used to Be Good News

Imagine two neighbors who have shared a fence for ten years. Most summer evenings, one of them hears the other's screen door creak and heads for the fence, because that sound means tomatoes, or a story, or his ladder finally coming home. Then one night he backs his truck out too fast and flattens the other man's mailbox. It is late. Nobody is around. He stands the post back up, wedges a brick against it, and goes to bed.

The next evening the screen door creaks, and his stomach drops. He goes inside. He does the same the following night. Within a week he is timing his trips to the trash can. Nothing about the neighbor has changed. It is the same door, the same walk, and the same man. The only new thing is a brick, and what the brick has done to the sound.

Here is what he does not know. The neighbor was at his kitchen window that night and watched the whole thing. He has been walking to the fence all week to say, I saw, it happens, we will set a new post Saturday and you are buying the concrete. The mailbox was never a secret. The brick fooled exactly one person, and all the ducking bought him was a week of lost evenings at the fence. That is the scene among the trees. The leaves hid nothing from God. They only kept two people away from the One who was walking toward them.

Today’s World

Most Patients Left Something Out

The exam room door closes, the doctor asks how things have been going, and the patient says fine. In 2018 a team led by Andrea Gurmankin Levy published what that fine can leave out. They surveyed more than 4,500 American adults in two online samples. In one sample, 81 percent said they had at some point held back medically relevant information from a clinician. In the other, older sample it was 61 percent. People kept quiet about disagreeing with the advice, not understanding the instructions, or not taking medicine as directed.

The reason chosen most often was not wanting to be judged or lectured. Many also said they were embarrassed. These are self-reports from online panels, and the authors warned that some people may have held back even on the survey. The study did not track what the silence cost anyone, and it says nothing about guilt. It shows fear of being judged getting in the way of an honest conversation with someone trained to help. The garden differs in one way that matters. A clinician may be missing what she is not told. God was missing nothing. Fear still cost Adam and Eve the same thing, an honest conversation with the One who had come to help.

Source JAMA Network Open

Prevalence of and Factors Associated With Patient Nondisclosure of Medically Relevant Information to Clinicians. JAMA Network Open, 2018. Read the source.

Christ at the center

We have been sewing fig leaves ever since. Some of us hide in busyness, some in jokes, some in church work, and some in being right. None of it has ever worked on God. Here is what He did about it. The One who wraps Himself in light was stripped by soldiers who gambled for His clothes (John 19:23-24). He hung exposed in public and endured the cross, scorning its shame (Hebrews 12:2). Jesus stood in the open, in the place of people who hide, so that being fully seen by God could become safe for us. That is why Hebrews can say in one breath that nothing is hidden and that we may come boldly. The eyes that see everything belong to Someone who died for what He sees.

Application

Name one thing you have been keeping out of your prayers, the topic you steer around. You do not need to fix it or explain it today. Say one plain sentence to God about it, something like, Lord, You already see this, and I am done pretending You do not. Then stay quiet for one minute.

Discussion Question

Adam and Eve hid from a God who could already see them. What is your usual hiding place when you feel guilty (busyness, distance, humor, extra effort)? What would it look like this week to be seen by God and stay put?

Closing Prayer

Lord, You fill heaven and earth, and nothing in us is hidden from You. We confess that we still try. We change the subject, we keep busy, and we hold our leaves together and hope You will walk past. Thank You that You do not walk past. Forgive us for believing the old lie that You are holding out on us. Give us courage to step out from behind the trees, because the One who sees us is the One who was stripped and shamed in our place. Teach us to hear Your footsteps as good news. In Jesus' name, Amen.
