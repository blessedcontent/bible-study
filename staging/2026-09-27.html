Communication in Eden

The Creator SpeaksLesson 1Day 2 of 7

In brief

Two people stand in a brand-new garden, and before they have done one useful thing, God blesses them and speaks to them. Today walks through the first conversations in the Bible. A blessing. A generous rule with a single boundary. A verdict that being alone is not good. A whole day set apart for company. You were not made to be a tool that works. You were made to be a person God talks to.

Yesterday

Yesterday opened the week with a God who talks. Hebrews said He spoke through the prophets in many pieces and then through His Son. Today goes back to the first conversation of all. What did it sound like before anyone had a reason to hide?

Historical Context

Kings Were Called God's Image

Travel far enough from an Assyrian king's palace and you could still meet him in stone. Assyrian kings set up carved images of themselves in lands where their armies had marched. Lesser rulers had images made too. In 1979 a statue of a local governor was uncovered by accident at Tell Fekheriye in northern Syria. It dates to around 800 years before Christ, and he had set it up as a gift to his storm god. Its inscription, in two languages, calls the statue the image and the likeness of the man it portrays. The Aramaic words are close cousins of the two Hebrew words used when God decides to make humankind (Genesis 1:26).

In the writings that survive from Egypt and Mesopotamia, image of a god is nearly always said of a king. One Egyptian wisdom text says it of people in general, but the phrase mostly lived at court. Genesis sets that royal language on every man and every woman (Genesis 1:27). Then it adds something no statue could manage. A stone image stands silent. God's living images get spoken to, and they can answer.

Into the Word

Listen to how much God talks in the creation story (Genesis 1-2). Let there be light. He calls for seas, for stars, for living things. Again and again He speaks, and every time He is speaking about the world. He even blesses the fish and the birds. (Reading both chapters out loud is a good project for another day. Nothing here depends on it.) But on the sixth day something new happens. For the first time, the text says God spoke to someone. What He says first, and what He says next, shows what people are for.

So what does God say once the blessing is spoken? The next chapter slows down and lets us hear the first instructions.

Then God says something nobody expects to hear in a perfect world.

One more gift belongs to that first week, and it is made of time.

Street Level

He Never Talked to the Tools

Picture a Saturday morning in a grandfather's garage. His tools hang on a pegboard, each one inside its painted outline. He takes them down, uses them, and hangs them back up, and in forty years he has never said a word to one of them. Then the side door opens and his eight-year-old grandson walks in. The old man starts talking before the boy has touched a thing. Good morning. Come see what we are building. Look at that, you have my hands.

Then come the rules, and nearly all of them are yes. Everything on this bench is yours. The hand saw, the sandpaper, the whole coffee can of screws. Not the table saw. It will take a finger before you feel it. The boy gets a reason because he can understand one, and because he could walk over and flip the switch anyway. A wrench gets picked up. A grandson gets told why.

Here is what gives the morning away. The boy is no help. He bends nails. He asks questions. He sweeps sawdust into the wrong corner, and the birdhouse takes three times as long with him there. His grandfather does give him real jobs, and he means them. But if finished birdhouses were the point, the old man would have worked alone. Genesis describes the first people the same way. Their work was real, and the blessing was spoken first. The tools were in that garage to be used. The boy was there to be talked to.

Today’s World

The Babies Who Got Answered

Researchers at the University of Washington recorded ordinary days at home for a group of babies at 6, 10, 14, 18, and 24 months. They counted two things. One was how many words adults spoke around the baby. The other was conversational turns, the moments when a baby made a sound and a parent answered, or a parent spoke and the baby answered back.

Around age two, 22 of the children had brain scans. Elizabeth Huber and her team reported in 2023 that the children who had shared more of those turns had more myelin, the coating that speeds signals along, in two pathways tied to language. The link held even after the team accounted for all the adult talk a baby simply heard. The study is small. It shows a link and cannot prove a cause, and most of the families were also in a parent coaching program.

Still, think about who was on the other end of those exchanges. A six-month-old cannot do one useful thing for anyone. Parents talk to her anyway and wait for the gurgle that comes back, and that exchange was what tracked with the growing brain. Genesis says God blessed the first people and spoke to them before they had done any work. A parent leaning over a crib still starts there.

Source Journal of Neuroscience

Christ at the center

The saddest detail in the next chapter is a small one. When the man and woman hear God walking in the garden in the breeze of the day (Genesis 3:8), nobody has to tell them whose steps those are. They know the sound, and they hide. God calls out anyway (Genesis 3:9). The rest of the Bible is that call repeated, until the day it arrives in person. Paul says Jesus is the image of the invisible God (Colossians 1:15), the one human being who never stopped listening. He said He spoke just what the Father taught Him (John 8:28). Then He turned to people like us and taught us to begin with two words, Our Father (Matthew 6:9). In Jesus the first conversation is open again, and He is the one holding the door.

Application

Before the day ends, give God two unhurried minutes with nothing to accomplish. Bring no list. Read the first sentence of Genesis 1:28, and then say one honest sentence back to Him about your day. You are practicing being someone God talks to. If you want more later, stretch it to five minutes somewhere quiet.

Discussion Question

God blessed the first people and spoke to them before they had done any work. Do you tend to treat time with Him as a task to finish or a conversation to enjoy (think of your last prayer)? What would move it toward the second?

Closing Prayer

Father, You made us to be spoken to. Before we had done anything useful, You blessed us and started the conversation. We confess that we often treat You like a job, something to finish so we can get on with the day. Slow us down. Teach us to hear Your commands as the words of Someone who trusts us with a choice. Thank You for the people You have given us to talk with, and for a day each week made for company with You. Make us glad to hear You coming. In Jesus' name, Amen.
