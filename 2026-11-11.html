Hypocrisy

Now Hear This!Lesson 7Day 5 of 7

In brief

In Jerusalem, people honored the prophets by building their tombs and decorating their monuments. Jesus told the religious leaders who did this that they were sons of the men who had killed the prophets, and that they would treat God's messengers the same way. He called them whitewashed tombs. It is easy to honor God's messengers from the past. The test is whether we listen to His word now.

Yesterday

Yesterday Chronicles looked back on centuries of messengers sent in compassion and mocked by God's people. Jesus told the same story with an owner's son at the end. Today He speaks to leaders who built monuments to the prophets. Honoring the dead, He warns, is not hearing the living.

Historical Context

Graves Were Whitened Before Passover

A Jewish law collection written down around AD 200 says that graves were marked in the month before Passover, and it describes marking them with lime. The reason lay in the law of Moses. Anyone who touched a grave became unclean for seven days (Numbers 19:16), and crowds came to Jerusalem before the feast to make themselves clean (John 11:55). A white mark warned travelers away.

Jesus spoke of people who built tombs for the prophets and decorated the monuments of the righteous (Matthew 23:29). Many scholars think His picture of whitewashed tombs recalls this practice of marking graves with lime (Matthew 23:27). The lime made a grave look clean and bright. It also announced that something dead was inside.

Into the Word

Picture the hillside graves outside Jerusalem, freshly whitened before the feast. From the road they almost shine. In His last days in Jerusalem, Jesus stands in the temple courts and speaks to people who would never dream of harming a prophet. They are sure of it. What could be wrong with honoring the dead?

The woes come one after another in this chapter. Jesus speaks of crossing land and sea to win a single convert, only to make him worse (Matthew 23:15). He speaks of cleaning the outside of the cup and dish while the inside is full of greed (Matthew 23:25). He speaks of whitewashed tombs (Matthew 23:27). Then He turns to the prophets.

Street Level

Miss Ruby Got Honked At Too

Think of a small town that loved its crossing guard. For thirty years Miss Ruby stood at the corner by the elementary school in an orange vest. She stepped into the street with her stop sign and did not move until the last child was across, no matter who was late for work. When she died, the town put her name on that corner. Half the cars in the drop-off line now carry a sticker that says Slow Down for Miss Ruby's Kids.

This fall there is a new guard on the corner, a young man in the same vest, holding up the same sign. He waits for the last child too. And every morning somebody leans on a horn. One driver rolls down her window to tell him he is making everyone late. The sticker is on her bumper. She would tell you she loved Miss Ruby, and she probably means it. But the old-timers remember that Miss Ruby got honked at every morning for thirty years.

The leaders Jesus warned built tombs for prophets their fathers had killed, and they were sure they would have listened. Jesus said they would do the same to the messengers He was sending them. The sticker is not the problem. Remembering Miss Ruby is a good thing. The trouble starts when honoring her takes the place of stopping for the man who holds her sign. Before deciding which driver you would be, check your own bumper. Miss Ruby never asked for a sticker. She asked the cars to stop.

Today’s World

Behind the Promise Were Ordinary Machines

Theranos, a company started by Elizabeth Holmes, promised to change blood testing. In all, it raised more than $700 million from investors. They were told, among other things, that its small machine could run a full range of blood tests on a few drops from a finger.

In 2018 the Securities and Exchange Commission charged the company, Holmes, and its former president with what it called a massive fraud. According to the SEC, the machine could do only a small number of tests. Most tests for patients, it said, were run on machines from other makers, some of them altered. Holmes settled without admitting or denying the charges. In January 2022 a federal jury found her guilty of defrauding investors. It did not convict her on the separate charges tied to patients.

One company's fraud does not make every bold promise a lie. Still, Matthew 23 describes the same split between what is displayed and what is real. The promise was a new kind of machine. Behind it, the SEC said, most patient tests ran on ordinary ones.

Christ at the center

Right after these woes, Jesus grieves over Jerusalem, the city that kills the prophets. He says how often He longed to gather its children as a hen gathers her chicks, but they were unwilling (Matthew 23:37). The One pronouncing woe was also the One grieving over the city. Within days He was handed over to be crucified, and His blood joined the line that began with Abel. Abel's blood cried out to God from the ground (Genesis 4:10). Hebrews says that Jesus' sprinkled blood speaks a better word than the blood of Abel (Hebrews 12:24). Abel's blood cried out for justice. Jesus' blood speaks mercy to all who come to God through Him.

Application

Take two minutes. Read Matthew 23:27-28 again. Ask God to show you one place where your outside looks cleaner than your inside. Do not try to fix your image. Tell Him the truth about the inside, and thank Him that He cleans what paint only covers.

Discussion Question

Jesus' hearers honored the prophets of the past but resisted God's voice in the present. Why is it easier to admire faithful people from long ago than to listen to a hard word today? Where might that be true for you?

Closing Prayer

Lord, it is easier for us to honor Your word than to obey it, and we confess it. Forgive us for keeping a clean outside while the inside goes untended. Keep us from reading about hypocrites and thinking only of other people. Take the beam out of our own eyes first. Thank You for the grief of Your Son over people who would not come to Him, and for the blood that speaks a better word. Make us clean on the inside. In Jesus' name, Amen.
