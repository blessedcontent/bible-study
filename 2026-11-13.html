Week in Review

This week listened for God's messengers and watched what people did with them. A crowd turned on them, a king burned their words, a nation mocked them, and leaders honored them only after they were dead. Every one of those stories was written down for us.

  • ☩Sabbath: Now Hear This! Paul says Israel's wilderness failures were written down as family warnings, so that we would hear in time.
  • ☩Sunday: Anger at God's Messenger. At Kadesh, a frightened crowd talked of stoning the scouts who trusted God, and He counted it as contempt for Himself.
  • ☩Monday: Destroying the Message: Part 1. A king tore the scroll instead of his clothes, and the fire did not take Babylon off the road.
  • ☩Tuesday: Destroying the Message: Part 2. Judah mocked God's patient messengers until no remedy was left, and in Jesus the remedy came again.
  • ☩Wednesday: Hypocrisy. Honoring dead prophets cost nothing, and Jesus' warning to the leaders became a mirror for us.
  • ☩Thursday: For Our Admonition. Israel's failures are patterns to recognize, and our footing rests on God's faithfulness and the cross.

Historical Context

Every Setting Offered a Reason Not to Listen

Gather the week's settings. Dining rooms beside a Corinthian temple. A camp on the border of the promised land. The court of a king who had already killed a prophet. An outpost watching for the fire signals of Lachish, perhaps in Babylon's final siege. Graves whitened for the crowds coming to the feast. A city that crowned its runners with pine.

Each place offered its own reason not to listen. Friends at dinner, walled cities ahead, a dangerous king, an enemy at the gates, a reputation to keep, a race that seemed to be going well. God's word reached people in every one of those places. It still reaches us in ours.

What This Week Clarifies

The Messengers Paid, and the Word Still Arrived

The week was hard on God's messengers. Joshua and Caleb heard a crowd talk of stoning them. Uriah was hunted down and killed, and Jeremiah and Baruch had to hide. Jesus said some of the prophets He sent would be killed and others flogged. If God sends people with His word, why does He let them pay so much for carrying it?

The Messengers Came Early, and the Time Still Ran Out

On Tuesday, Chronicles said God sent His messengers again and again because He had compassion, and Peter said the Lord is patient, not wanting anyone to perish. Yet Chronicles also says Judah's mocking went on until there was no remedy. If God is that patient, how can it ever be too late? And if it can be too late, how patient is He?

A Mirror, Not a Scoreboard

All week we read about people who failed. On Wednesday Jesus warned leaders who were sure they would never have done what their fathers did. On Thursday Paul warned the one who thinks he is standing firm. Yet the Bible is full of such failures. How are we supposed to read them, if not as other people's mistakes?

Still Speaking

The Voice Kept Calling the Next Turn

Picture a corn maze on a farm in late October. The stalks stand nine feet tall, and every path looks like the last one. In the middle of the field, a wooden bridge rises above the corn. A father climbs up there while his twelve-year-old son runs in with two friends. Before they go, he gives them one piece of advice. If you get turned around, stop and listen for me. I can see the whole thing from up here.

The boy sprints ahead, sure he has it figured out. When his father calls, Left at the scarecrow, he laughs and turns right, and his friends laugh with him. An hour later the friends have found the exit, and the light is going. The boy has passed the same broken stalk three times. He sits down in the dirt, tired and scared, sure he has ruined the whole night. Then he hears it. His father's voice is still coming over the corn, not angry, just steady. Stand up. Walk to the end of this row. Now go left.

There are two ways to read this week's stories, and the boy tried both. We can read them like the boy who laughed and went right, sure we would never have shouted down Caleb or burned a scroll or mocked a prophet. Or we can read them like the boy in the dirt, sure we have already turned wrong too many times to be found. Both ways stop us from listening. Paul says these things were written down as warnings for us (1 Corinthians 10:11), and they come from a Lord who is patient, not wanting anyone to perish (2 Peter 3:9).

The voice over the corn never said I told you so, and it never went quiet. It gave the next turn, and then the next one. God is still speaking that way to people who did not listen the first time. He does not leave us to find our own way back. He calls the next step, and the time to take it is today. The maze closed at dark, and the time was real. But the boy walked out with minutes to spare, following a voice that had never stopped calling.

Christ at the Center of the Week

Every day this week pointed to one Person. On Sabbath, Paul said the ages have reached their goal, and God sent His Son in the fullness of time (Galatians 4:4). On Sunday, Hebrews set the faithful Son over God's house and called us to hear His voice today. On Monday, the king who cut up God's scroll could not cancel God's promise of a King on David's throne (Luke 1:32-33). On Tuesday, the owner sent servant after servant and then his beloved son, and the remedy Judah laughed away was offered again through Him. On Wednesday, Jesus grieved over the city that killed the prophets, and His blood speaks a better word than Abel's (Hebrews 12:24). On Thursday, He stood in the wilderness where Israel fell, and His cross became Paul's only boast. The prophets were pointing ahead to Him (Acts 10:43). God's last word is not a warning. It is His Son.

Weekend Practice

Before the Sabbath begins, take two minutes. Read 2 Peter 3:9 and thank God for the time He has given you to turn. If you have more time this weekend, read the rest of Numbers 14 and notice what Moses asks God to do for the people who wanted to replace him.

Closing Prayer for the Week

Father, You kept speaking to people who did not want to listen, and You wrote their stories down for us. Forgive us for the times our fear has turned to anger, for the warnings we have set aside, and for honoring Your word outwardly while our hearts stayed far away. Thank You for Your patience, and for the Son You sent last of all. Keep us from reading the hard stories as if they were about someone else, and keep us from despair. Steady us by Your faithfulness, and help us hear You today. In Jesus' name, Amen.
