Destroying the Message: Part 2

Now Hear This!Lesson 7Day 4 of 7

In brief

An officer at an outpost writes that his men are watching for the signal fires of Lachish. His letter may come from Judah's last months, as Babylon's army was closing in. Chronicles looks back and explains how it came to this. Again and again God had sent His messengers, because He had compassion. Again and again His people mocked them. God's patience was long, and it was aimed at their turning. Mocking His messengers, year after year, left Judah with no remedy.

Yesterday

Yesterday a king cut up God's scroll, and God had the words written again. The fire took the scroll, but it did not take Babylon off the road. Today that road reaches Jerusalem. Chronicles looks back and explains how Judah got there.

Historical Context

The Last Letters From Lachish

Zedekiah became king at twenty-one and reigned eleven years (2 Chronicles 36:11). He did evil. He would not humble himself before Jeremiah, who spoke for the LORD (2 Chronicles 36:12). Late in his reign, Babylon's army moved through Judah. At one point only two fortified cities were left besides Jerusalem, Lachish and Azekah (Jeremiah 34:7).

In 1935, at the ruins of Lachish, the archaeologist James Leslie Starkey found letters written in ink on broken pottery. In one, an officer tells his commander that his men are watching for the fire signals of Lachish, because they cannot see Azekah. The last word is damaged, but most scholars read it that way. The letter does not say why Azekah could not be seen. It may come from Babylon's final siege, shortly before Lachish fell. If so, the warnings had become the news.

Into the Word

Before Chronicles tells how the temple burned (2 Chronicles 36:17-19), it stops to explain why. The explanation is not mainly about Babylon's army. It is about years of messages from God, and what His people did with them. How did the people of a patient God end up watching their city burn?

That line of messengers was long. God sent Moses to tell Israel that the God of their fathers had sent him (Exodus 3:15). A psalm remembers that He sent Moses His servant and Aaron, whom He had chosen (Psalm 105:26). Isaiah heard God ask whom He should send and answered, Send me (Isaiah 6:8). And Jeremiah, the prophet of Judah's last years, said it in the very phrase Chronicles would later use.

The sending did not stop with Jeremiah. Malachi promised a messenger who would prepare the way, and then the Lord Himself, the Messenger of the covenant (Malachi 3:1). Jesus said all the Prophets and the Law prophesied until John (Matthew 11:13). He warned that God would send prophets and apostles, and that some of them would be killed (Luke 11:49). Why does God keep sending to people who refuse?

Street Level

She Called While the Path Was Still Open

Some small islands can be reached on foot only around low tide. For about three hours, a bar of sand and gravel joins one of them to a seaside town, and people walk across to explore. Imagine a woman who has lived above that beach for forty years. She knows how fast the bar goes under. One afternoon she sees the water start sliding back into the low spots. Three visitors are still out on the island, taking pictures.

She walks down to the edge and calls to them. They wave. She sends her grandson running along the sand to shout. They laugh and keep taking pictures. She calls once more, and then again, the whole time the water over the bar is only ankle-deep. One of them jokes that the tide must pay her by the hour. When they finally start back, the water is past their knees and pulling hard, so they turn around. The bar will not show again for nine hours, and it is a cold night on the rocks.

Each call was time she was giving them while the path was still open. Chronicles says God kept sending His messengers because He had compassion, and Judah kept mocking them until there was no way back. God is not like her in one way. The tide was never hers to hold back, but the day Peter writes about is God's, and He holds it back on purpose. Those visitors were not stranded for missing one call. They waved at the first, and then they laughed.

Today’s World

The Reports Asked for Repairs for Years

Early on a January morning in 2022, the Fern Hollow Bridge in Pittsburgh gave way. The 447-foot bridge fell about 100 feet into the park below, carrying a transit bus and four other vehicles. A fifth vehicle drove off the east end after the collapse began and landed on its roof. At least four people were hurt, two of them seriously. No one was killed.

The National Transportation Safety Board traced the collapse to a corroded steel plate on one of the bridge's legs. Clogged drains had let water and debris collect at the bottoms of the legs, and over the years the steel rusted thin. Inspection reports from 2005 through 2021 repeatedly recommended maintenance and repairs. The city did not act on them. The board also faulted the quality of the inspections and the state's oversight, and it found that a correct load rating would have closed the bridge.

The warning never went missing. It came back report after report, and the repairs never came. Chronicles describes Judah's last years in the same shape, with one difference. Judah's warnings came from God Himself.

Christ at the center

Jesus told a story that sounds like Chronicles told from the owner's side. A man planted a vineyard and leased it to tenants. At harvest he sent servant after servant to collect his share, and the tenants beat some and killed others. He had one left to send, his beloved son, and he sent him last of all (Mark 12:1-8). Within days, what the tenants did to the son was done to Jesus at the cross. But about seven weeks later, Peter stood in Jerusalem and told a crowd that God had made this Jesus, whom they had crucified, both Lord and Christ. When they asked what to do, he told them to repent and be baptized for the forgiveness of their sins (Acts 2:36-38). Judah mocked the remedy until none was left. In Jesus, God offered it again, even to the people who had crucified Him.

Application

Take two minutes. Read 2 Peter 3:9. Thank God for one way His patience has reached you. Then name one thing you have been putting off turning from, and ask Him for the grace to turn from it today.

Discussion Question

Chronicles says God kept sending messengers because He had compassion. How does knowing that change the way a warning sounds? Why is it tempting to treat God's patience as permission to wait?

Closing Prayer

Compassionate Father, thank You for sending Your word again and again to a people who did not deserve it. We confess that we have treated Your patience as if it meant there was no hurry. Keep us from laughing off the messages You send. Thank You for sending Your Son last of all, and for offering forgiveness even to those who rejected Him. Give us humble hearts that listen, and turn us while there is time. In Jesus' name, Amen.
