Grace, Love, and Fellowship

Grace, Love, and FellowshipLesson 13Day 1 of 7

In brief

Somewhere in Corinth a reader reaches the last lines of a letter that has argued, pleaded, and warned for thirteen chapters, and the room waits to hear how it ends. It ends with a blessing. Grace from Jesus, love from God, fellowship from the Spirit, handed to all of them, the loyal and the stubborn alike. This week walks through that one sentence a name at a time and finds the whole gospel folded inside it. The last word Paul had for a divided church was not a rule. It was a gift with three names on it.

Last week

Last week ended on a bridge with the trucks parked in the middle of it, the measured give under weight being the sound of a strength that was never ours, holding. Christ in you was the steel. This week Paul writes the closing sentence of his last surviving letter to Corinth, and it is a blessing with three names in it.

Historical Context

Greek Letters Usually Ended With Be Strong

Read through a stack of ordinary Greek letters dug out of the Egyptian sand from Paul's century and many of them end the same way, with a one-word farewell that means be strong. It worked the way our sincerely or take care works, a formula so ordinary that nobody read it twice. It was common enough that the Jerusalem church used it when it wrote to the Gentile believers in Antioch (Acts 15:29). Be strong. Then the letter was rolled up and handed to whoever was sailing that way.

None of Paul's surviving letters ends that way. He had been writing to Corinth for several years by this point. The two letters we have mention others that did not survive, and a visit so painful he did not want to repeat it (2 Corinthians 2:1). Thirteen chapters of this letter have just told the church to examine itself. So the reader in the house church reaches the final paragraph (2 Corinthians 13:11) braced for one more warning and hears five quick commands instead. Rejoice. Be put back together, the same word Paul used at the start of his first letter when he begged them to mend their divisions (1 Corinthians 1:10). Encourage one another. Agree. Live in peace. Then, where be strong should go, he writes a sentence with three names in it. Strength was never going to come from the Corinthians. It was going to come to them.

This Week’s Memory Text

Every one of Paul's surviving letters ends with grace. Only this one ends with all three names at once, and it went to the church that had given him the most grief.

This Week’s Road Map

  • ☩Sunday: The Grace of Jesus. Grace opens and closes every surviving letter Paul wrote, and Sunday asks what it cost.
  • ☩Monday: The Love of God. God is love, and the proof is a sending, a Son handed over while we were still sinners.
  • ☩Tuesday: The God of Love. Paul gives God a title found nowhere else in Scripture, and its peace is the kind that mends a whole person.
  • ☩Wednesday: The Fellowship of the Holy Spirit. The Spirit is a Person who searches, teaches, and testifies, and fellowship with Him is where fellowship with each other begins.
  • ☩Thursday: Our Triune God. Grace, love, and fellowship come from Father, Son, and Spirit together, in one rescue told in three verses.
  • ☩Friday: Further Thought. The week gathers around one blessing and asks what changes for people sent out under it.

Christ at the center

Watch where Jesus stands in this blessing. He is named first, ahead of the Father, which is not the order anyone expects, and it is hard to believe it is an accident. The Corinthians did not come to know the love of God by reasoning their way up to it. They met it in a Man who was rich and became poor for them, and only afterward could they say the Father is love and the Spirit is with us. Grace is the door. This week is going to ask what that grace cost, why the Father sent Him, and how the Spirit makes His company real in a room full of ordinary people. Paul had already learned that his own hard days were for their comfort (2 Corinthians 1:6), because that is the shape of the One he served. So keep one question open until Friday. If the blessing begins with Jesus, what does it tell you about God that He introduces Himself through a crucified Son?

Application

Before Sunday, read 2 Corinthians 13:14 out loud once. Go slowly, and put your own name in place of all of you. Then sit with whichever of the three names you needed most. If you are able, think of one person you expect to see this week who could use some of what you just received. Comfort is given so it can be passed along (2 Corinthians 1:4). Hold that person's name through the week.

Opening Question for the Week

Of grace, love, and fellowship, which one feels most like something you have already received and which feels most like something you are still waiting on (think of the last time you needed each)? What would it take for the missing one to feel like a gift rather than a requirement?

Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, You end Paul's hardest letter with a blessing, and we would like to begin our week under it. We come as the people that letter was written to, some of us loyal, some of us stubborn, all of us tired in places we do not talk about. Open these texts to us. Show us what grace cost, what love sent, and what fellowship with the Spirit feels like on an ordinary Tuesday. Where we have turned You into a rule, make Yourself a gift again. And be with all of us, the way Paul asked. In Jesus' name, Amen.
