Paul, a God-called Apostle of Jesus

Paul's Ministry in CorinthLesson 1Day 2 of 7

In brief

Paul sits down to write a church that is starting to doubt him, and the first thing he does is state his credentials. Not his education. Not his track record. He writes that he is an apostle of Christ Jesus by God's will. The man who once dragged believers off to prison now signs his name as someone Jesus personally drafted and sent. His steadiness never came from feeling impressive or qualified. It came from knowing he was called, and that the call was God's idea before it was ever his. What makes a life count is not that you were impressive, but that you were chosen and sent.

Yesterday

Yesterday we stood back and took in the whole city, that brash, restless, status-hungry Corinth where everyone had something to prove. We met a missionary worn thin and a promise spoken in the dark, that God already had people there. Today the camera moves off the skyline and lands on one person. Before we can understand the letters Paul wrote to fix a broken church, we have to understand the man holding the pen. Who was he? Where did he get the nerve to correct a whole congregation from a distance? The answer he gives in his very first line is not what a status-obsessed city would expect. He does not lead with his resume. He leads with his calling.

Historical Context

A Messenger Was as Good as the King Who Sent Him

In the ancient world, when a king needed to act in a distant province, he did not go himself. He sent an envoy. And the rule everyone understood, in Roman courts and Jewish law alike, was that the envoy carried the full authority of the one who sent him. To insult the messenger was to insult the king. The Jewish world had the same idea, a legal maxim that a person's authorized agent is treated as the person himself. The envoy had no authority of his own. He had something better, the backing of the one whose word he carried.

That is the freight loaded into the word "apostle." Paul is not claiming to be brilliant or self-made, the very things Corinth prized. He is claiming something a self-made city could barely understand, that he speaks not on his own behalf but as the personal representative of the risen Jesus. When the Corinthians read his corrections, they are not hearing one more opinion to weigh against Apollos or Cephas. They are hearing the King through His envoy. That single claim is why a letter from a tentmaker could outrank the loudest voices in the room.

Into the Word

Watch how Paul opens his most difficult letter. The Corinthians are fracturing into fan clubs, each one rallying behind a favorite leader, and Paul could have answered by listing everything he has done for them. Instead he reaches for one word in his very first sentence, and then spends the rest of his life unpacking where that word came from. So we start with that opening line and let the question it raises pull us out through his other letters. Who actually called this man, and what did the calling cost him?

Street Level

The Kid Who Can Stop the Whole Job

On a big construction site there is one person everybody waits on, and it is usually not the most impressive person there. The building inspector might be half the age of the foreman and could not lift one end of a steel beam. But when that inspector walks the site with a clipboard and finds something wrong, the whole project stops. Cranes go still. A crew of seasoned men who have been pouring concrete since before the inspector was born stand around and wait, because one signature is the difference between a building people can move into and one that gets chained shut.

Here is the strange part. The inspector's power has almost nothing to do with the inspector. It is borrowed entirely from the city and the code behind them, and that is exactly what sets the inspector free. They do not need the foreman to be impressed, they do not need the crew to like them, and they do not have to win by being the smartest voice on the site. They only have to be the one the authority was handed to. That is the ground Paul is standing on when he calls himself a sent one. His weight in a divided church never came from being the cleverest apostle or the smoothest speaker. It came from Who sent him, and a person who knows they were sent does not need the room to admire them.

Today’s World

What Keeps a Doctor From Burning Out

At three in the morning, deep into another brutal shift, two emergency room doctors can look identical from the outside. Same patients, same exhaustion, same flickering fluorescent lights. Yet one of them is quietly burning out while the other is somehow still steady. A 2015 study of emergency physicians went looking for that difference, and it did not come down to the number of hours or the size of the caseload. The doctors who lasted were the ones anchored to a clear sense of purpose, a reason they were there that ran deeper than the schedule. Strip that away and even a light load starts to crush a person. Hold onto it and you can carry a heavy one.

That is Paul's secret, centuries early. His steadiness never came from feeling impressive or from the room going well. It came from knowing he was called and sent, that his being there was God's idea before it was ever his. The same holds for the tired and the criticized today. When your sense of who you are is borrowed from your last performance, every bad shift and every harsh word can flatten you. When it is rooted in being chosen and sent by God, you can take the hit and still be on your feet when the sun comes up.

Christ at the center

Every word Paul uses for himself was true first of Jesus. Paul is a sent one, but Jesus is the Sent One, the true apostle, the envoy the Father dispatched into our world carrying the Father's full authority and the Father's exact heart. Paul was set apart from the womb, but Jesus stepped out of eternity on purpose, born for a mission He had agreed to before the foundation of the world. Paul calls himself a servant, but Jesus is the Servant who did not cling to His rank and instead emptied Himself all the way down to a cross. And here is the mercy that undid Paul. The very One he had been persecuting turned around and drafted him. The risen Jesus did not pay back the man who hunted His people. He commissioned him. The apostle's whole authority traces back to a moment of pure grace, when the One he wronged looked at him and said, in effect, I have work for you. That is the gospel before Paul ever preaches a word of it.

Application

Today, write down the one line you reach for when you need to feel like you matter. Maybe it is your job title, your follower count, your role in the family, or the good you have done. Then write underneath it the line Paul reached for, that you are loved, called, and sent by God, not because you earned it but because grace got to you first. Carry that second line into the next moment someone makes you feel small, and let it, not the first line, be the thing you stand on.

Discussion Question

"Where do you still go looking for proof that you matter (your work, your reputation, your usefulness to others), and what would it change about this week if you truly believed your worth was settled by God's call before you did anything to earn it?"

Closing Prayer

Father, we spend so much of our energy trying to prove we are worth something, building little resumes to hand You and everyone else. Thank You that Paul's confidence, and ours, was never supposed to come from being impressive. You called him before he was born, met him while he was at his worst, and made him useful by sheer grace. Do the same in us. Quiet the part of us that needs to be admired, and root us instead in the simple, unshakable fact that we are Yours and You have sent us. When we feel unqualified, remind us that being called was always Your decision, not our achievement. In Jesus' name, Amen.
