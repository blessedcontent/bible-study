The Power of Christ's Resurrection

The Power of Christ's ResurrectionLesson 8Day 1 of 7

In brief

The road into Corinth ran between tombstones, and a common Roman epitaph was a shrug. I was not. I was. I am not. I do not care. Into that city Paul mails a paragraph that puts the whole faith on the line and dares anyone to check his facts. If Christ is still dead, he says, then the preaching is hollow, the faith is hollow, and the grave wins everything. This week walks his argument all the way to its verdict. Christ has been raised, and every promise you are living on stands up with Him.

Last week

Last week ended in front of a polished bronze mirror, squinting at a dim almost-image and waiting for the day the seeing goes both ways, face to face. That hope has a hidden load-bearing wall. A portrait of Jesus painted in verbs is only good news if the Man in the portrait is alive to keep loving that way. A dead man's love, however perfect, is a museum piece. So Paul spends the longest chapter of this letter on the question every other promise leans on. Did Jesus actually walk out of His grave? This week we stand where Corinth stood and hear his answer.

Historical Context

The Graves Outside the City Gate

You learned a city's beliefs about death before you ever reached its gate. Roman law kept the dead outside the walls, so the tombs crowded both shoulders of the highway, and the last mile of any journey was a corridor of stone monuments where the dead introduced themselves as you passed. Some epitaphs begged the traveler to stop and read, so the name would live a moment longer in a stranger's mouth. And one line was common enough that stonecutters shortened it to seven letters, the way we reduce our commonest phrases to initials. Written out, it said this. I was not. I was. I am not. I do not care.

That shrug was the empire's best answer to the grave. The philosophers offered a loftier one, a soul slipping free of the body at last, but out on the road where ordinary people buried their children, hope mostly read like the stones. Gone is gone. Into one of those cities came a letter insisting that a man executed by Rome had been seen alive, eating breakfast, holding out scarred hands, and that His rising was not a ghost story but a first installment. Paul knew exactly which world he was writing into. That is why he does not whisper the claim. He builds the entire Christian faith on top of it, in public, and invites the reader to test the foundation.

This Week’s Road Map

  • ☩Monday: The Risen Christ, Our Only Hope! Paul runs the honest math of a dead Christ and counts everything that collapses if the tomb stayed sealed.
  • ☩Tuesday: Christ, the Firstfruits. The resurrection is a harvest, and Jesus is its first armful, the guarantee that everyone belonging to Him follows.
  • ☩Wednesday: The Resurrected Body. How are the dead raised, and with what body? A seed, a night sky, and a promise about these very bones.
  • ☩Thursday: Final Victory Over Death. The trumpet sounds, the sleepers wake in a blink, and death is swallowed by the victory it thought it had won.
  • ☩Friday: Further Thought & Discussion. The week lands on witnesses, on hope with evidence underneath it, and on the reunion no grave can cancel.

Christ at the center

Here is the seed to carry into Sunday. Last week you learned to recognize Jesus by how He moves, patient, kind, keeping no record of wrongs. This week's question is blunter. Is He moving now? Everything the portrait promised depends on tense. A dead teacher leaves behind a philosophy, and you can admire a philosophy your whole life without ever being surprised by it. A living Lord makes appointments. He forgives in the present tense, intercedes in the present tense, and intends to raise you in the future tense. Watch how this chapter keeps refusing to let Jesus become a beautiful memory. By Wednesday you will see why your own body is written into His victory, and by Friday the question will have turned personal. If He stood up, then nothing about your life, your grief, or your grave is stuck where it is.

Application

Before Sunday, take a small piece of paper and write one name on it. The name of someone death has taken from you, the one you still catch yourself missing at odd hours. Put the paper in your Bible at 1 Corinthians 15 and leave it there all week. Every day of this study will be read over that name. Do not rush past the ache, and do not explain it away. Just let the week's argument stand next to your loss, and watch what a risen Christ has to say to it.

Opening Question for the Week

"If you learned beyond all doubt that Christ never rose, what in your ordinary week would actually have to change (the prayer before meals, the money you give, the hope you spoke at a graveside, the way you face your own aging)? And if He truly did rise, what should change by this time next week?"

Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, we have recited the resurrection in creeds and sung it at Easter, and still we live many of our days as if the tomb were only a metaphor. This week, plant our feet at the actual grave in the actual garden. Where our faith has gone soft with familiarity, make it load-bearing again. Bring our honest questions out of hiding, because You are not afraid of them, and walk them patiently through the evidence Paul left us. Sit with us beside the names we have written and the griefs we still carry. Teach us the difference between wishing and hoping, and anchor our hope to something that happened. And when the week is over, let us say with whole hearts that Christ has been raised, and let that truth change how we face Monday, and the doctor's office, and the cemetery. In Jesus' name, Amen.
