Stewardship and Mission

Stewardship and MissionLesson 11Day 1 of 7

In brief

The strangest fundraiser in the ancient world is stalling in Corinth, and Paul restarts it without one word of guilt. He points north instead, where churches too poor to be asked are begging for the chance to give, and then he hands over the whole gospel in money language. Jesus was rich. For your sake He became poor. Through His poverty you became rich. Two chapters about an offering keep turning into two chapters about grace, and the collection plate keeps turning into a mirror. Giving is not a bill from God. It is the family resemblance of a Giver.

Last week

Last week ended in a clothing store, with factory-ripped jeans accidentally proving that everybody trusts marks of real use over polish. Authentic ministry turned out to be the dying-and-rising pattern of Jesus repeating in a spent life, sealed by the scars the risen Christ still shows. Then Paul turns the page, and the next subject is money. That is not a letdown. His résumé already held the strangest arithmetic in the letter, poor, yet making many rich, having nothing, and yet possessing everything. This week the letter asks what that math does to a wallet. Two chapters about an offering turn out to be a portrait of grace, and the spending is where the story shows.

Historical Context

The Offering Traveled Eight Hundred Miles by Hand

Start with the practical problem, because the first churches had to. In Paul's world there was no way for ordinary people to send money anywhere. A gift for believers in Judea had to travel the way everything valuable traveled, as coin, in a bag, in somebody's grip, across hundreds of miles of roads and sea lanes that nobody patrolled. Paul knew those miles personally, the rivers, the bandits, the shipwrecks. So the relief project he had been organizing among his churches for years ran on deliberate structure. Gifts set aside week by week. Couriers chosen and named by the churches themselves. Companions for the road. Everything counted in the open, where God and men could see it.

Now the harder problem. The money was moving from Greek hands to Jewish ones. The believers in Jerusalem, poor and famine-worn, belonged to a people who had spent centuries on one side of the world's oldest religious wall, and the givers in Macedonia and Achaia had grown up on the other side, pagan, uncircumcised, unclean. Some in Jerusalem had honestly wondered whether such converts could be family at all. Paul saw what no sermon could accomplish sitting in a bag of Greek coins. When Gentile money bought bread on Jewish tables, the argument about whether they were one family would be over. These two chapters are the paperwork of that audacity. Which means this week is never really about money. It is about what grace does to the people who know they have received it.

This Week’s Road Map

  • ☩Sunday: The Example of Jesus. One verse holds the whole story of redemption in financial language, and every gift this week traces back to it.
  • ☩Monday: Motivation. Paul names the reasons underneath real giving, gratitude, imitation, blessing, and love, and not one of them is guilt.
  • ☩Tuesday: Planning. God decided to give before the world fell, and giving that looks like His is decided on purpose, in proportion, ahead of time.
  • ☩Wednesday: Attitude. The poorest churches on the list gave with overflowing joy and begged for the privilege, and God says that is the kind of giver He loves.
  • ☩Thursday: Unity. The offering travels by trusted, appointed hands and lands as proof that Jew and Gentile have become one family in Christ.
  • ☩Friday: Further Thought & Discussion. The week gathers into one question, what the grace of Jesus turns giving into.

Christ at the center

Here is what to watch as the week unfolds. Paul is about to ask a church for money, and he refuses every lever a fundraiser normally pulls. No quota. No guilt. No comparison charts. Instead, every time the appeal needs weight, he reaches for the same Person. You know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ. The Macedonians gave themselves first to the Lord. God loves giving that looks like His own. By Friday, the collection plate will have quietly turned into a mirror, and the face in it will be Christ, the rich One who chose the manger, the borrowed boats, the borrowed tomb, so that beggars like us could inherit everything. So carry one question into every page this week. What did my becoming rich cost Him? Answer that honestly even once, and nobody will ever have to talk you into generosity again.

Application

Before Sunday, find one real number. Look back over the last month and add up what you actually gave away, to God's work or to people in need, and write it on a slip of paper. This is not a guilt exercise. It is a starting point, and nobody will see it but you and the Lord. Then pray one sentence over it each day this week. Lord, show me what Your giving cost You, until my giving feels like a privilege. Keep the paper somewhere you will find it again on Friday.

Opening Question for the Week

"When the subject of giving comes up at church, what is your honest, unedited first reaction (bracing for a guilt trip, quietly running your budget, remembering a season when giving felt joyful, tuning out)? What would it take for two chapters about an offering to read like good news to you by Friday?"

Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, we come to this week honestly. Talk of money and giving makes something in us tighten. We brace for the ask, we rehearse our reasons, and we would rather keep this one subject between us and our bank. So before anything else, remind us that You are the Giver in this story. You so loved that You gave. Your Son was rich and chose poverty so that we could be rich, and He did it gladly, not through gritted teeth. As we walk through two chapters about an ancient offering, do the deeper work in us. Untangle our security from our balances. Show us the difference between giving that pays a bill and giving that says thank You. Make the grace of Jesus so real to us that open hands stop feeling dangerous. We give ourselves to You first, before anything in our pockets. In Jesus' name, Amen.
