Christ, the Firstfruits

The Power of Christ's ResurrectionLesson 8Day 4 of 7

In brief

Yesterday's walk ended at a cliff, a world where the dead stay dead and hope deserves pity. Today Paul spins around with the sentence that carries all the weight. But Christ has indeed been raised from the dead. Then he reaches for a farmer's word to say what that means. Firstfruits. The first armful cut from the field, lifted up as proof and promise that the whole harvest follows. Jesus did not rise away from us. He rose ahead of us, and everyone who belongs to Him is standing in a field He has already claimed.

Yesterday

Yesterday Paul walked us to the edge of the world his opponents were proposing and made us look over. Seven consequences, one darker than the next, down to the unbearable last line about believers who have perished, their graves keeping them forever (1 Corinthians 15:12-19). He left us standing at that edge on purpose, long enough to feel the cold coming up from below. Today he takes us by the shoulder and turns us around, and what he shows us is not a philosophy. It is a field at harvest time, and the first armful is already cut.

Historical Context

The First Armful of the Harvest

Early on a spring morning in Israel, a farmer stood at the edge of his barley field with a sickle and cut exactly one armful. Not the whole crop. One sheaf, the first to ripen, carried to the priest and lifted up before the Lord on the day after the Sabbath of Passover week (Leviticus 23:10-11). Until that sheaf went up, nobody in the land ate a bite of the new grain. The first armful stood in for every field in Israel, consecrating a harvest that was still rooted in the ground.

The sheaf did two jobs at once. It declared the harvest holy, and it guaranteed the harvest was coming, because what had happened to the first armful would happen to the rest. Same crop. Same soil. Same life. Now count the calendar. Jesus of Nazareth walked out of His tomb on the morning after the Sabbath of Passover week, the exact slot where the wave sheaf was lifted. When Paul calls the risen Christ the firstfruits, he is not decorating his sentence with farm imagery. He is reading the festival calendar out loud. God had been rehearsing Easter every spring for fifteen hundred years.

Into the Word

After eight verses inside the world where the dead stay dead, Paul has earned the right to his turn, and he takes it in a single ringing sentence. But Christ has indeed been raised from the dead (1 Corinthians 15:20). Every expectation about the future darkens or brightens on that hinge. The delusion Paul dangled in front of Corinth evaporates, and in its place he sets a picture borrowed from the nearest farm. Firstfruits. One harvest word, and suddenly the resurrection is not only something to believe about Jesus. It is something to expect for everyone who belongs to Him.

Notice where the risen Christ carries it all. As the ultimate Adam in the plan of redemption (1 Corinthians 15:45), He succeeds exactly where the first Adam failed, reigning until every enemy is under His feet and then delivering the kingdom back to His Father (1 Corinthians 15:25-28). Even His final act is submission, the Son Himself gladly subject to God (1 Corinthians 15:28), so that God may be all in all. Adam grasped. Christ hands everything home.

Then Paul calls a second witness, his own calendar. Why would he stand in danger every hour (1 Corinthians 15:30-32)? If the dead are not raised, the sane schedule is the old drinking song, let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die (1 Corinthians 15:32). Nobody faces wild beasts in Ephesus for a metaphor. Paul bet his body on the harvest.

Street Level

The First Band Around the Corner

Every small town knows the moment. You stake out a curb spot early, unfold the chairs, and the kids start asking the only question kids bring to a parade. Is it coming? You cannot see anything yet. Then you hear it, drums bouncing off the buildings from somewhere out of sight, and finally the first band swings around the corner in full uniform, and the asking stops. Notice what nobody in the crowd thinks. Nobody thinks one band happened to march today and maybe more will follow. The front of a parade is not a preview of the parade. It is the parade, arriving. One procession, stretched blocks out of sight, and every float, fire truck, and Scout troop is already in motion behind the lead, in the exact order the lineup assigned.

Paul says Christ's resurrection works like that front band. He is the firstfruits, the first of one connected company to come up the street, and the rest follow in order. Christ first. Then, at His coming, everyone who belongs to Him. So His empty tomb is not an isolated miracle to admire from the curb, the way the world treats it. It is the head of a procession turning the corner, and if you are His, you are in the lineup, along with everyone you have ever loved who fell asleep holding on to Him. We live in the strange, wonderful stretch where the drums are already audible and our block has not seen the front yet. Waiting is not wondering. The parade has turned the corner. The rest is order of march.

Today’s World

One Shot Turned Someday Into a Date

For seventy years, fusion power was the running joke of the energy world, always thirty years away, always someday. The sun runs on it, but no lab on earth had ever coaxed the reaction into giving back more energy than it took to start. Then, in the early hours of December 5, 2022, at the National Ignition Facility in Livermore, California, 192 lasers delivered 2.05 megajoules to a fuel capsule the size of a peppercorn, and the capsule gave back 3.15. Ignition. For the first time, a fusion reaction had produced more energy than the laser energy delivered to the target capsule that sparked it. That is a scientific line, not a commercial one, and the difference matters. Running the whole facility still took far more energy than the shot gave back. And it was no fluke. NIF has now reached ignition eleven times, and in April 2025 a single shot returned 8.6 megajoules, more than four times the laser energy that went in. One morning moved fusion from someday to demonstrated, repeatable fact, the kind of result that advances the prospects for fusion energy. But a working power plant is a different summit, still years of substantial engineering away.

Paul's word for Easter morning works the same way, and it worked first. The aparchē, the firstfruits, is the first armful that settles the question for the whole field. For all of human history the grave had kept everyone it claimed. A few had been called back before, but only to mortal life, and the grave took each of them again. Every hope of beating death for good was filed under someday. Then one morning outside Jerusalem a body came back, seen, touched, and watched eating, never to die again, and the question changed tense forever. But watch where the parallel deepens and then breaks in our favor. A proof of physics guarantees only that a thing can be done. Everything after still depends on funding, engineering, and decades of follow-through. The Firstfruits guarantees more, because resurrection is not a technology waiting to scale. It is a harvest already owned. What binds you to that first morning is not a roadmap but a Person, and He does the raising Himself, each in his own turn, on a date already set on His calendar. Fusion turned someday into a prospect. Christ turned it into a promise with your name in it.

Christ at the center

Follow the two Adams home. The first Adam stood in a garden, grasped at being like God, and pulled the whole family down with him. Death through a man. The last Adam knelt in a garden, surrendered, and now pulls the whole believing family up with Him. Resurrection through a Man. Here is the part worth sitting with today. Christ did not rise away from us, the way a rescued miner climbs out and leaves the shaft behind. He rose ahead of us, the first of the crop, and firstfruits means the rest of the field is not a wish. It is a schedule. Each in his own turn. He has already walked through death and come out the far side holding the keys to it. If you belong to Him, your resurrection is not pending approval. It is pending arrival.

Application

Name the one piece of your future you grip hardest, your health, your kids' faith, the money that has to last, the person you cannot imagine losing. Today, formally hand it over, entrusting it to Him by name. Then say out loud, in the old prisoner's words, I know whom I have believed, and I am convinced that He is able to guard what I have entrusted to Him for that day. Say it once this morning and once before you sleep. Deposits belong with the only Guard who has already beaten death.

Discussion Question

"Where does your confidence about the future actually live right now (a savings balance, a health report, a person you lean on, Christ Himself)? And what would have to shift this week for you to say about the thing you most fear losing, with an old prisoner's calm, I know whom I have believed?"

Closing Prayer

Father, thank You for the sentence that turns the whole chapter, and thank You that it is true. But Christ has indeed been raised from the dead. Thank You for writing Easter into Your calendar centuries ahead of time, a sheaf lifted every spring until the morning Your Son fulfilled it in person. Teach us to live like people standing between the firstfruits and the harvest, neither despairing as if the field were lost nor grasping as if the crop depended on us. Remind us, every time we witness a baptism, that we have already rehearsed resurrection morning. Take the futures we clutch so tightly and hold them for us until that day. And when fear whispers that tomorrow is uncertain, teach us to answer with a name instead of an argument, because we know whom we have believed. In Jesus' name, Amen.
