Prophetic Authority and Relevance

Prophetic Authority and RelevanceLesson 10Day 1 of 7

In brief

A poor man's lamb slept in his arms. A rich man with a flock of his own took that lamb to feed a guest. When the prophet Nathan tells the story, King David knows what the rich man owes. Four lambs. Then Nathan names the wrong David himself has done. He had Uriah killed and took his wife (2 Samuel 12:1-9). God used David's own sense of justice to reach him.

Last week

John urged believers to test what they heard so they could recognize a voice worth following (1 John 4:1). Last week, a pilot picking out her own call sign gave us a picture of that. What happens when the voice we trust calls our own conduct into question?

Historical Context

The Song Changes Speakers

Halfway through Psalm 95:1-11, the voice changes. At first the worshipers speak to one another. Come sing. Come kneel before the God who tends us like sheep. Then another voice breaks in. God remembers the people He led through the wilderness and the day they refused to trust Him. Imagine getting that far into the song with your congregation. A moment ago you were inviting the person next to you to worship. Now both of you are being asked to listen.

Hebrews introduces that old song in the present tense. The Holy Spirit says these words (Hebrews 3:7-8). The writer singles out today. Joshua had brought Israel into the land long before God spoke through David. If arriving in the land had given Israel all the rest God intended, why offer another day to hear His voice? The promise was still open to the people hearing Hebrews, and so was the choice their ancestors had faced (Hebrews 4:6-11). The next time they heard the song, today would mean the very day they could still answer.

This Week’s Memory Text

We may know the wilderness story well enough to tell it to a child. What helps us recognize ourselves in it? Listen for what God's word can reach.

Christ at the center

When Nathan finishes speaking, David can finally say whose sin it is (2 Samuel 12:13). It takes courage to speak that plainly. Hebrews gives us a reason we can do it. Just after saying that God sees everything in us, the writer names Jesus as our great high priest, the One who brings us before God. Jesus knows what it costs to face temptation. He has faced it Himself without giving in. And He invites us to come for mercy while we still need it (Hebrews 4:13-16). Think of the moment a familiar verse first makes you afraid to pray. You can begin the prayer right there. Jesus already knows the part you dread having to explain.

Application

Think of a recent moment when you were sure you were in the right. Try describing just your part in it to Jesus in one honest sentence. If you find yourself starting with what the other person did, give yourself a moment and begin again.

Opening Question for the Week

What made it easy for David to recognize cruelty in Nathan's story, and where might God be inviting you to look at your own actions with that same clear eye?

Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, some words of Scripture come easily to our lips when we have somebody else in mind. As we read this week, give us ears for the words that come close to home. Where we've kept a ready excuse, give us the courage to tell You what happened in plain words. Where we've carried shame, help us hear Your invitation to come near. Thank You for giving us a place to bring the truth once we've seen it. In Jesus' name, Amen.
