Further Thought & Discussion

All to the Glory of GodLesson 5Day 7 of 7

Week in Review

This week Paul answered a menu question with a compass, walking Corinth from the meat market to the Lord's table and insisting that knowledge bows to love, rights bow to the gospel, and everything, down to eating and drinking, points at the glory of God.

  • ☩Sabbath: All to the Glory of God. In a city where dinner came with a god attached, the church asked for a food rule and Paul handed them a compass instead, do everything for the glory of God.
  • ☩Sunday: Knowledge Versus Love. The strong were right that idols are nothing and wrong about what their rightness was doing, because knowledge puffs up while love builds, and a brother Christ died for was being ruined by a trophy.
  • ☩Monday: Selfless Love. Paul read out his rights, meals, a wife's travel, a working wage, proved every one legitimate, then left them all uncollected so the gospel would never look like it was for sale.
  • ☩Tuesday: Learning From the Past. Israel had the cloud, the sea, and the Rock, and still fell at a festival meal, so their story was pressed as a mold for confident believers, with a built-in way out promised to the tempted.
  • ☩Wednesday: Warning Against Idolatry. The statue is hollow but the table is not, and no one can drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons, because tables join people to hosts.
  • ☩Thursday: Overcoming Idolatry. Jesus welded love for God to love for the near one, and a rich young man walked away grieving, proof that an idol is anything you would walk away from Jesus to keep.

Historical Context

The Gods Came Bundled With Everything

Step back and the week had one setting. In Corinth, religion was not a compartment. It came bundled with the groceries, since the meat passed an altar on its way to the stall. It came bundled with the calendar, since weddings, birthdays, and business deals were catered in temple dining rooms. It came bundled with the air itself, which everyone agreed was crowded with unseen powers wanting to be fed. A believer could not run errands in that city without brushing against somebody's god. The question the church sent Paul was practical for exactly that reason. There was no neutral square inch to stand on.

And Paul, astonishingly, never tells them to find one. No separate market, no Christian quarter, no wall. The church stayed at the city's tables, ate the city's food, kept the city's friendships. What changed was the aim. One God, loved entirely, glorified in the smallest verbs, and one neighbor, guarded even at the cost of a steak. The believers' defense was never distance. It was direction. Corinth's gods came bundled with everything, and the church answered by bundling everything, eating, drinking, whatever you do, with the glory of the God who is actually there. The week leaves us the same assignment in a different city.

What This Week Clarifies

Walk back through the six studies and the week keeps saying two things at once. The idol is nothing, and the table is deadly serious. The rights are real, and Paul will not collect them. The privileges were enormous, and the desert is still full of graves. Each pair sounds like a contradiction until the week's compass is laid over it. All three deserve a settled answer before Sabbath.

An Empty Statue Can Still Host a Dinner

Sunday handed the confident crowd a complete victory on doctrine. An idol is nothing at all in the world, and Paul signed his name under their slogan. Then Wednesday walked into the temple dining room and called the dinner demonic. A reader can finish the week unsure which Paul to believe, the one who emptied the statue or the one who named the host.

The Open Hand Was Part of the Sermon

Monday spent most of its space proving Paul's rights were real. Soldiers eat from the army's budget, the ox eats the grain it treads, and the Lord Himself prescribed that preachers live from the gospel. Then Paul refused to collect, and the refusal can look like false modesty, or worse, like quiet disobedience to the very command he had just quoted.

The Graves and the Door Are in the Same Chapter

Tuesday set the week's hardest photograph next to its gentlest promise. A whole rescued nation, baptized in the sea and fed from heaven, lies buried in the desert, and the confident are told to be careful. Two verses later God is faithfully building exits into every temptation. Warning and comfort arrive in the same breath, and it is tempting to hear only whichever one your temperament prefers.

Still Speaking

The Last Idol Standing Is the Self

Strip away the marble and the myths, and idolatry's job description has not changed in twenty centuries. Something that is not God collects what belongs to God. The only real update is packaging. Our idols rarely need a statue anymore, because they have a mirror. Self-serving, love of ease, the gratification of appetite and passion, these are idolatry in street clothes, and they explain why a person can own no incense and still keep a crowded shrine. The feed we cannot stop refreshing, the comfort we cannot be asked to give up, the image we polish, the ease we defend like territory. The rich young ruler has heirs, and most of them have profiles.

Against all of that, the week hands us one compass and one sobering fact about influence. The compass turns worship outward, whatever you do, even the eating and the drinking, aimed at making God look like who He is. And the fact is this. Nobody's liberty is private. Somebody is always reading your freedom, a child, a newer believer, a watching skeptic, and what you sanction with a shrug can become the permission slip for someone else's collapse. So the mature believer accepts a strange, glad duty, to do nothing, however innocent in itself, that would dim another person's path, and to treat every association as a chance to shed light on it instead. Freedom, used that way, stops being a possession and becomes a ministry.

Here is the promise hiding under the whole week. The self does not shrink because we scold it. It shrinks the way stars fade at sunrise, outshone by something bigger. People who truly love God simply run out of appetite for the idolatry of self, not because they tried harder but because the throne is occupied. The week began with a bull walking up a temple ramp. It ends in front of the last working altar in the modern world, the one with a mirror over it. Love is how that altar closes.

Christ at the Center of the Week

Trace the thread and every day of this week lands on the same Person. Sabbath gave us the compass, do everything for the glory of God, and Jesus is the only human who ever walked that line without a wobble, glorifying the Father in meals, miracles, and silence alike. Sunday hung a price tag on the weakest conscience in the room, the brother for whom Christ died, and knowledge learned to kneel. Monday found Paul leaving his rights uncollected, a small photocopy of the Son who left heaven's riches unclaimed and stretched out His hands instead of summoning angels. Tuesday put Christ in the wilderness itself, the Rock that traveled with a complaining people and was struck so they could drink. Wednesday handed us the cup of blessing, which holds blessing only because Jesus drank a darker cup to its bottom in the dark. And Thursday showed us both great commandments kept to the last drop, love for God saying not My will, love for neighbor absorbing the nails. A week that started with a question about meat ends staring at a Host with scarred hands. It always does.

Weekend Practice

Set a glory table this weekend. Share one unhurried meal with a near one, family at home, a friend who eats alone, someone newer in faith than you, and quietly aim the whole ordinary thing at God. Before it, pray the week's compass over the table, whether we eat or drink, or whatever we do. During it, seek the other person's good on purpose, phone away, questions asked, attention spent, and let go of one small preference you would normally defend. Afterward, write down what changed when a plain meal had a direction. You practiced the entire week in one sitting.

Closing Prayer for the Week

Father, You have spent a week teaching us what to do with our forks, and it turned out to be a week about our hearts. Thank You for a compass sturdy enough for gray areas, for the reminder that knowledge is for building and rights are for laying down, and for the old stories written in the desert so that our confidence would learn to hold the railing. Forgive us for the second tables we have kept, and for the good gifts we promoted into gods while nobody was watching except You. Fill the vacancies. Teach us to love You with all our heart, soul, mind, and strength, and to love the near ones You have set within arm's reach, until the idolatry of self starves quietly in a heart that is already full. This weekend, make our ordinary tables point at You. And when we lift the cup of blessing, let us taste what it cost. In Jesus' name, Amen.
