Spiritual GiftsLesson 6Day 7 of 7

Week in Review

This week Paul walked into the loudest living room in Corinth and reintroduced the church to itself, one Spirit handing out many gifts, one body wearing many parts, love as the only current strong enough to run any of it, and every word in worship aimed at raising the house.

  • ☩Sabbath: Spiritual Gifts. In a borrowed living room humming with gifts and rivalry, Paul issued the week's command, chase love first, then welcome every gift the Spirit gives.
  • ☩Sunday: Varieties of Gifts. The Spirit's first gift is the nerve to say Jesus is Lord in Caesar's world, and behind that confession comes a catalog of grace gifts handed out exactly as He decides.
  • ☩Monday: Unity Through Diversity. Against a city that filled temple shelves with clay body parts, Paul insisted the church is a living body, every part arranged by God and the weakest ones indispensable.
  • ☩Tuesday: A More Excellent Way. Without love the grandest gifts are Corinthian bronze, expensive noise, so Paul measured love in fifteen verbs and crowned it greater than everything it carries.
  • ☩Wednesday: The Gift of Tongues. Real languages from the Spirit were being fired into the air, so Paul aimed the gift back at meaning, two or three, each in turn, interpreted, everything building up.
  • ☩Thursday: The Gift of Prophecy. The neglected gift speaks for God to strengthen, encourage, and console, it runs until the church is finished, and every claim to it faces Scripture's tests.

Historical Context

One Room, Every Gift

Step back and the whole week happened in one room. Outside its door, an empire ran on the phrase Caesar is lord, and advancement belonged to whoever said it loudest. Up the hill, a healing god's shrine displayed its shelves of clay hands and feet, perfect parts with no body. Down in the foundries, the city poured the most expensive noise in the world, and across the gulf a priestess proved that incomprehensible speech could be a growth industry. Along every road walked somebody claiming to speak for heaven. Corinth was not short on spiritual experiences. It was drowning in them, ranked, priced, and performed.

And in the middle of all that, a borrowed living room filled up each week with a dock worker, a patron, a slave, a seamstress, each one carrying something the Spirit had handed them. Every pressure of the city followed them through the door, the ranking, the volume, the hunger to be impressive. Paul's three chapters were written for exactly that room, and his remedy was not a better performance. It was a body, knit by love, speaking to be understood, testing every voice, building one house. The church did not need to out-shout Corinth. It needed to out-love it. That was true of one living room in AD 55. It has stayed true of every room the church has met in since.

What This Week Clarifies

Set the six studies side by side and three pairs of truths seem to pull against each other. The Spirit alone decides who gets which gift, yet wanting more is commanded. A room humming with gifts gets handed house rules. The God who still speaks tells His church to test every voice that claims Him. None of the three is a contradiction. Each is the week finishing a thought, and each rewards one slow look before Sabbath.

The Signed List Still Says Want More

Sunday closed every argument about who earned what. The Spirit hands out the gifts exactly as He decides, and complaining about yours criticizes His judgment. Then Monday's chapter ended with a command that sounds like the opposite. Eagerly desire the greater gifts. The week seems to fix the list with one hand and stir up wanting with the other.

Order Is the Sound of a House Going Up

The week opened in a living room humming with gifts, everyone arriving with something, a hymn, a teaching, a prayer in another tongue. It closed with what reads like a set of meeting rules. Two or at most three. Each in turn. Someone must interpret. A reader can feel the temperature drop, as if Paul heard the fire and reached for a clipboard.

The Test Begins With the Word Beloved

Thursday made two claims that sit awkwardly in the same pew. God still speaks, and His gifts stay until the building is finished. And every voice claiming His name must be tested. Most of us quietly pick one, swallowing every confident word because doubting feels like unbelief, or waving the whole gift away because fakes have burned us.

Still Speaking

The Names at the End of the Movie

Stay in your seat when the movie ends sometime. The screen fills with names, hundreds of them, scrolling faster than anyone can read. Gaffers. Riggers. Drivers. The person who kept track of the hero's scarf between takes so the story would not fall apart. You were moved twenty minutes ago because every one of those people did their job, and almost none of them will ever be recognized in a grocery store. The faces on the poster did not make the film. The credits did.

The church has always run on credits. One member is a ready speaker, another writes, another prays like the room changes when they start, another sings, another can walk a teenager through a hard verse in plain words, another just shows up with a truck on moving day. The same God works all of it in each person (1 Corinthians 12:6), which means nobody gets to despise the supposedly lesser gifts, least of all in the mirror. Folding your hands because you think you can do no mighty work is not modesty. It is a body volunteering to be shorter one limb. The week's cure for both pride and shrinking is the same motion, eyes off yourself, eyes on your Leader, then do what you can, in meekness, sincerity, and love.

And do it together, because none of us carries enough alone. God works through other minds than ours on purpose, and the gifts were designed to blend, like voices, like ingredients, like crew departments, for the building up of the body of Christ (Ephesians 4:12). There will be obstacles. There always are, on every set, in every church. We meet them the way a body does, unitedly, hand in hand. The story God is telling will roll credits longer than anyone expects, and no name on that scroll will have been filler. Bring what you carry. The house is still going up.

Christ at the Center of the Week

Sabbath opened with a Giver, the ascended Christ stocking His church like a builder stocking a job site before the crew arrives. Sunday handed us His name, Jesus is Lord, the confession no one can truly speak without the Spirit and the first gift every believer already holds. Monday He was the Head, so identified with His body that persecuting the church made Him ask, why are you persecuting Me. Tuesday He was the portrait, fifteen verbs of love that read like a biography because they are one, patient with Peter, kind to lepers, keeping no record from the cross. Wednesday He was the Word translating Himself into our language, God refusing to speak over our heads. Thursday He was the subject of every true prophecy, the testimony of Jesus, the voice on the Emmaus road interpreting the things concerning Himself. A week that began with a question about gifts ends holding one Gift that contains the others. He gave gifts to people because He had already given Himself.

Weekend Practice

Hold a gift audit this Sabbath, out loud, with your household or a friend from church. Three moves. First, name each other's gifts, especially the ones nobody claps for, and be specific about where you have watched them build somebody. Second, each of you pick one place to spend your gift in the coming week, a person, a task, a corner of the congregation, and say it so someone can ask you about it later. Third, thank God by name for one gift He gave somebody else, without a single word of comparison. Bodies grow where gifts get named, aimed, and celebrated.

Closing Prayer for the Week

Father, we walked into this week asking which gifts matter and we leave it asking a better question, whether love is running the ones You gave us. Thank You for the Spirit who signed our distribution list, for the body that makes our small parts matter, for the fifteen verbs that showed us Jesus, and for a voice that still strengthens, encourages, and consoles. Forgive us for the rankings we kept, the words we aimed at nobody, the gifts we buried because they seemed small, and the voices we swallowed or dismissed without testing. This Sabbath, gather us like a body and not a crowd. Help us name the gifts in each other out loud, spend our own where You point, and keep every voice in our life under Your Book and aimed at Your Son. We bring what we carry. Raise Your house with it. In Jesus' name, Amen.
