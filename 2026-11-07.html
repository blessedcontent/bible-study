Now Hear This!

Now Hear This!Lesson 7Day 1 of 7

In brief

Picture a new believer in Corinth holding an invitation to dinner in a temple's dining room. His friends will be there, and he knows an idol is nothing. Paul answers with an old story about Israel in the wilderness. They walked through the sea and ate the food God sent, and most of them still fell in the desert. God kept the record of people who would not listen to Him, and He kept it for us, so that we would hear Him in time.

Last week

Last week ended with a sailor's shoebox of letters from home, some written for a day he had not reached yet. Scripture holds pages like that. This week turns from how God's word was written to how people heard it, and how often they refused.

Historical Context

Corinth's Temples Served Dinner

At Corinth, archaeologists found three dining rooms at the sanctuary of Asklepios, the Greek god of healing. Each room once held eleven stone couches around seven tables. In a Greek or Roman city, a temple meal could be a party with friends and an act of worship at once. A papyrus from Egypt, written in the century after Paul, invites a guest to dinner. The meal is at the table of the lord Sarapis, in that god's own temple.

Some believers in Corinth saw no harm in such meals. An idol is nothing, they reasoned (1 Corinthians 8:4), and some were eating in an idol's temple (1 Corinthians 8:10). Paul answered with history. Israel, too, had been rescued by God and fed by Him. Yet some of them ended up at a feast for an idol, where the people sat down to eat and drink and got up to indulge in revelry (1 Corinthians 10:7). Then Paul explained why that story had been written down.

This Week’s Memory Text

Paul has just walked the Corinthians through Israel's years in the wilderness. Now he steps back and tells them why those years were written down.

This Week’s Road Map

  • ☩Sunday: Anger at God's Messenger. At Kadesh, fear turned into fury at the scouts who said go, and God called it contempt for Him.
  • ☩Monday: Destroying the Message: Part 1. A king cut up God's scroll, and God had every word written again.
  • ☩Tuesday: Destroying the Message: Part 2. God sent messenger after messenger out of compassion, and Judah laughed until no remedy was left.
  • ☩Wednesday: Hypocrisy. Jesus told people who decorated the prophets' tombs that they would treat God's living messengers no better.
  • ☩Thursday: For Our Admonition. Paul says Israel's failures were written down as warnings, so that we would not fall the same way.
  • ☩Friday: Further Thought & Discussion. We gather the week's warnings and listen for the voice behind them.

Christ at the center

Look again at the last words of the memory text. Paul says the fulfillment of the ages has come to us. The long story of God speaking to His people has reached its goal. Elsewhere Paul says that when the time had fully come, God sent His Son (Galatians 4:4). This week's stories show God sending messenger after messenger, and people turning them away. A crowd talked of stoning two faithful men. A king fed a scroll to the fire. A nation laughed at the prophets. So carry a question into the week. What happens when the last Messenger God sends is His own Son? Watch for Him. The warnings were never the last word God meant to send.

Application

Before the week begins, take two minutes. Read 1 Corinthians 10:11 slowly. Then ask God one honest question. Is there a warning from You that I have been treating as if it were meant for someone else? Write down whatever comes to mind, and bring it with you into the week.

Opening Question for the Week

Paul says the Bible's hard stories were written as warnings for us. Why is it easier to hear a warning as meant for someone else? What kind of warning is hardest for you to hear (about money, anger, time, or trust)?

Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, thank You for speaking to us, and for keeping the record of people who would not listen, so that we might. This week, as we read about fear and fury, a burned scroll, mocking crowds, and decorated tombs, open our ears. Where we have treated Your warnings as meant for someone else, show us, gently and clearly. Keep us from thinking we could never fall. And in every story, show us the Son You sent to people who had refused so many messengers. In Jesus' name, Amen.
