Power to Those Who Are Being Saved

The Message of the CrossLesson 2Day 4 of 7

In brief

Same cross, two completely different verdicts. To people clinging to their own wisdom it looks like ruin, and Paul uses a word that means they are actually destroying themselves. To people willing to come with empty hands it is rescue. Two short verses spell out what that rescue cost. Jesus reconciled us to God by His blood, bore our sins in His own body, and healed us by His wounds. The debt we could never pay was simply paid. Salvation is not something we achieve by being clever or good. It is something we receive, because Jesus already paid it in full.

Yesterday

Yesterday the cross was a punchline. The same crowd that prized wisdom looked at a crucified Savior and saw only foolishness. But Paul has been holding back the other half of the sentence. The very message that sounds like nonsense to people heading for ruin is raw power to the people being rescued. Same cross, opposite reactions, and the difference is not how smart you are. It is which way you are facing. Today we stop talking about how the cross sounds and start looking at what it actually did, the real work Jesus finished there on our behalf.

Historical Context

The Debt That Could Cost You Everything

In the ancient world, debt was not just a number on a statement. It could cost you everything. A person who could not pay what they owed might lose their land, their freedom, even their children, sold off to settle the account. Lenders kept a written record of the debt, a signed certificate that hung over a person like a sentence waiting to be carried out. Everyone in Corinth understood the dread of owing more than you could ever repay, and the desperate relief of having someone step in and clear it for good.

That is the world Paul reaches into when he describes what the cross accomplished. Sin is a debt we ran up against God and can never pay down. The wages we have earned are death, plain and simple. What happened at the cross is that the bill was paid in full by Someone else, the certificate cancelled, the prison door swung open. This is why Paul can call the same cross that destroys the proud the rescue of everyone who stops pretending they can cover their own account. One group keeps insisting they are good for it. The other lets the debt be paid. Only one of them walks out free.

Into the Word

Paul keeps saying the cross is the power of God to those being saved (1 Corinthians 1:18). Power to do what, exactly? Today two short verses answer that, one from Paul and one from Peter, and they are worth slowing all the way down on. Remember the danger Paul named, that clever words can quietly drain the cross of its power (1 Corinthians 1:17). So before he says one more thing about wisdom and foolishness, he shows us the actual rescue, the concrete work Jesus did on the cross that no human cleverness could have pulled off.

Street Level

The Rescue You Have to Stop Fighting

Lifeguards are trained for a terrible, backwards fact. A drowning person will often fight the very people trying to save them. Blind with panic, they climb up onto the rescuer, shove them under, and claw toward the air, dragging both of them down. So a guard is taught to wait for the thrashing to slow, or to come in from behind, because the thing most likely to get a drowning person killed is their own frantic effort to save themselves. The rescue cannot really begin until the fighting stops. They have to go still, quit clawing, and let themselves be carried in by someone else's strength.

Two people can go under in the same water. One keeps fighting, burns himself out trying to stay on top, and the rescue that was right beside him becomes the thing he drowns next to. The other stops, lets go, and is towed safely to shore by an arm that was never his own. That is the line running straight through the cross. To the proud, still kicking, still certain they can keep their own head above water, grace looks like one more thing to fight off. To the person who has finally stopped thrashing and put up empty hands, it is the grip that pulls them home. Same water. Same rescuer. The only difference is who finally quit trying to save himself.

Today’s World

The Debt Someone Else Bought

Imagine opening your mail to find a plain letter telling you a debt you could never pay is simply gone. Hundreds of thousands of people have received that exact letter. A nonprofit now called Undue Medical Debt buys up the crushing hospital bills of strangers, the kind that pile up after a cancer diagnosis or a long illness, and then cancels them outright, asking nothing back. It works because old medical debt sells for almost nothing, so a single donated dollar can wipe out around a hundred dollars of what someone owes. Since 2014 the group has erased more than 15 billion dollars in debt for over 10 million people. In 2024, New York City alone put up 18 million dollars to clear roughly 2 billion dollars owed by its residents.

You cannot do this for yourself, and that is the whole point. The people whose debt vanishes did not negotiate it down or earn it off. Someone with the means they lacked stepped in and paid what they never could. That is the cross in miniature. We ran up a debt against God we were never going to settle, and the wages of it were death. Then Jesus paid the account in full with His own blood and handed us the letter that says it is gone. The drowning man who keeps clawing at the water cannot tow himself to shore. He can only stop fighting and let himself be carried. Some gifts are too big to buy. They can only be received.

Christ at the center

Everything today circles one quiet truth. Jesus did the saving. We keep slipping back into thinking we contribute something, that we meet God halfway, that our effort tops off what the cross started. Today's verses leave no room for it. He reconciled us. He bore our sins. By His wounds, not our willpower, we are healed. The cross is not a down payment we finish off with good behavior. It is the whole price, paid in full, by the only One who could pay it. And the posture that receives it is not strength but surrender, the empty hands of someone who has finally stopped trying to save themselves. That is the real divide running through this passage. Not the smart and the stupid, but the proud who insist on covering their own debt and the humble who let Jesus cover it all.

Application

Somewhere in your life there is probably an account you are still trying to settle with God, a sin you keep re-apologizing for, a failure you feel you have to make up for before you can feel close to Him again. Today, name that one thing, and then say out loud what the cross already says about it. "Paid in full." You are not adding to what Jesus did. You are receiving it. Live one whole day as someone whose debt is genuinely, completely cleared.

Discussion Question

"Where are you still trying to pay off a debt that Jesus already settled in full, and what would change in how you live this week if you truly believed the cross meant 'paid,' not 'partially covered'?"

Closing Prayer

Father, thank You for doing what we could never do for ourselves. We come with empty hands today, because that is the only honest way to come. Thank You that Jesus bore our sins in His own body, that He made peace by His blood, that by His wounds we are healed. Forgive us for the times we try to earn back what You have already given, as if the cross still needed our help. Teach us to stop striving and simply receive. And let the relief of a debt fully paid make us gentle with everyone else who is still trying to pay their own. In Jesus' name, Amen.
