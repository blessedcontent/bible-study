Interpreting Prophetic Writings

Interpreting Prophetic WritingsLesson 8Day 1 of 7

In brief

Two of Jesus' followers are walking away from Jerusalem on the third day after His death. A stranger falls in beside them and asks what they are discussing. They stop, faces downcast. We were hoping He was the One who would redeem Israel, they tell Him. Then the stranger begins with Moses and goes through all the Prophets. They had the right Scriptures but only part of the reading, and Jesus showed them how all of Scripture, from Moses on, speaks about Him.

Last week

Last week ended in a corn maze, where a boy who had laughed at his father's directions sat in the dirt until the same voice called the next turn. God keeps speaking. This week asks how to read the words He has already given.

Historical Context

Some Prayed for a King With an Iron Rod

In 63 BC a Roman general captured Jerusalem. In the decades that followed, Jewish writers produced a set of songs now called the Psalms of Solomon, though Solomon did not write them. They are not Scripture, but they show one hope that was in the air. One song asks God to raise up a son of David who would shatter unrighteous rulers and clear Jerusalem of the nations trampling it. It pictures him breaking them in pieces with a rod of iron.

Luke lets us hear a hope for Israel's rescue from two of Jesus' followers. On the road out of the city they told a stranger, we were hoping He was the One who would redeem Israel (Luke 24:21). Their hope was in the past tense. Luke does not list everything they expected, but a crucified Redeemer was not part of it. The stranger named what was missing. They had been slow to believe all that the prophets had spoken, including that the Christ had to suffer before entering His glory (Luke 24:25-27).

This Week’s Memory Text

Luke sums up the rest of that seven-mile walk in a single sentence.

Christ at the center

That same hour the two got up and returned the seven miles to Jerusalem (Luke 24:33). While they were describing what had happened, Jesus Himself stood among them (Luke 24:36). Then He opened their minds to understand the Scriptures (Luke 24:45). Notice what the risen Lord does. He does not leave His followers alone with a hard book, and He does not set the book aside. He stands with them and explains it. This week will look at careful ways to read, such as asking about setting, context, and meaning. Carry a question beneath all of them. What changes when we read expecting to meet Him? On the road to Emmaus, two discouraged readers learned that the Scriptures had been speaking about the Stranger beside them all along.

Application

This week, before you read any passage, pray one short sentence. Lord, show me Your Son here. Try it now with Luke 24:27. Later, if you have ten more minutes, read the whole road story in Luke 24:13-35 and notice the moment the two begin to understand.

Opening Question for the Week

The two on the road knew the Scriptures and still missed what they said about suffering. Why is it easy to read the Bible for the parts we already expect? Which passages do you tend to skip (warnings, long lists, prophecy, or hard stories)?

Opening Prayer for the Week

Father, Your Son walked beside two discouraged followers and explained the Scriptures until their hearts burned. Walk with us through Your word this week. Where we read only the parts we expect, show us the rest. Where we pull Your words out of their setting, give us patience to read them whole. Give us careful minds and humble hearts, and open our understanding the way He opened theirs. Above all, let every passage we read lead us to Him. In Jesus' name, Amen.
