Centered on Jesus

Unity in ChristLesson 3Day 3 of 7

In brief

Yesterday's question left a hole. If the church is not supposed to rally around Paul or Apollos, what actually holds it together? Today Paul answers with a Person. We were called into fellowship with the Son of God, baptized into His name and no leader's. The word he uses for "united" means mending a torn net or setting a broken bone, not filing everyone down until they match. He wants a body, not a set of clones, where wildly different people are anchored to the same Lord. Unity is not everyone becoming the same. It is everyone facing the same Jesus.

Yesterday

Yesterday Paul emptied the factions with a single question. Was Paul crucified for you? No, and neither was Apollos or Cephas. But a question like that leaves a hole behind it. If the church is not supposed to gather around its favorite teachers, then what does hold it together? Today Paul fills the hole, and the answer is a Person. We were never called into a fan club for Paul. We were called into fellowship with the Son of God Himself. The problem in Corinth was never that they loved their leaders too much. It was that they had let a leader slide into the one place where only Christ belongs.

Historical Context

Rome Told the Body Story to Keep People in Their Place

Long before Paul, the Romans already had a famous story about a body. As the legend went, the common people of early Rome once went on strike and marched out of the city, furious at being used by the elite. A senator coaxed them back with a fable. Once upon a time, he said, the hands and the mouth resented the lazy stomach and refused to feed it, until the whole body wasted away and they learned their lesson. The moral was blunt. Know your place, do your job, and stop complaining, because the people at the top are the stomach and you are the hands. For generations the "body" was a tidy image for keeping the lower ranks quiet and content at the bottom.

Paul knew that image, and he detonated it. When he tells the Corinthians they are one body with many members, he is not telling the little people to keep serving the important people. He says the opposite. The parts that look weakest are the ones you cannot live without, the members that seem least honorable get the most honor, and nobody gets to say to anyone else, "I don't need you." In Rome the body kept people in their lane. In Christ the body makes the lowliest member essential. A church busy ranking its teachers had the picture exactly upside down, and Paul flips it right side up by pointing them past every member to the Head.

Into the Word

So what does Paul actually want in place of the factions? His words sound almost impossible at first, that a church full of different people would be "united in the same mind and the same judgment." It sounds like he is asking everyone to become identical. He is not. Read him closely and you find something far better than uniformity, a real unity that runs underneath our differences because it is anchored in one shared Lord. The center of the church was never meant to be a personality. It is a Person, and everything Paul says here is working to move Christ back to the middle where He belongs.

Street Level

Why Strangers Talk Around a Fire

Think about the last time you stood around a real fire at night, a backyard fire pit or a ring at a campground. Look at who is there. People who would have nothing to say to each other in a bright kitchen. Different ages, different politics, some loud, some shy, a few of them strangers. And yet a fire on a cold night does something a lit room never quite manages. It turns a random cluster of people into a circle. Nobody had to agree on anything first. Nobody became like anyone else. They just turned their chairs toward the same warmth, and somewhere in facing the same center, they quietly became an "us."

That is the unity Paul is begging the Corinthians to recover, and notice what it is not. He is not asking a room full of very different people to become identical. The word he reaches for means mending a torn net or setting a broken bone, putting something fractured back into working order, not filing everyone down until they match. A church is a circle around a fire, and the fire is Christ. Face Him together and a hundred differences stop dividing you, because you are all being warmed by the same thing. Turn away from the center to argue about who is sitting where, and the circle breaks and the cold comes back in. Unity was never everyone becoming the same. It is everyone leaning toward the same fire.

Today’s World

The Eye as Wide as the Earth

A black hole sits so far away and looms so small in our sky that no single telescope on Earth could ever photograph one. So in 2019 a team did something stranger. They took radio dishes scattered across the planet, from Hawaii and Arizona to Chile, Spain, Mexico, Greenland, and the South Pole, run by different countries and institutions, and aimed every one of them at the same spot in the sky at the same instant, all synchronized by atomic clocks. When their separate streams of data were combined, the scattered dishes acted like one telescope the size of the whole Earth, and it captured the first image ever taken of a black hole. In 2022 they did it again for the giant one at the center of our own galaxy. Behind that work stood a sprawling collaboration, some 760 scientists and engineers from nearly 200 institutions, spanning 32 countries or regions.

Notice what actually made it work. Nobody melted the telescopes down and rebuilt them as identical machines. They stayed exactly what they were, wildly different instruments in different nations, run by people speaking different languages. Their unity came from one thing only. Every last one of them was fixed on the same center. That is the picture Paul is reaching for. He is not asking the Corinthians to become copies of one another, matched in shape and size and opinion. He is asking them to aim their lives at the same Christ. A church becomes one body the way those scattered dishes became a single eye, not by sameness but by a shared center. Turn the instruments toward each other and you get nothing. Turn them all toward the same point and suddenly they see what none of them could see alone.

Christ at the center

Every problem in Corinth traces back to one misplaced center. A church is only ever as unified as the Person it is built around, and the Corinthians had quietly swapped the Son of God for their favorite spokesmen. Paul's fix is not to find a better leader or broker a cleverer compromise. It is to move Christ back to the middle. Think about what that means. The thing that binds Christians together is not agreement, not personality, not shared taste in music or politics or preachers. It is a shared Lord who was crucified and raised for every one of us. Two believers who cannot agree on much of anything are still joined at the deepest possible point, because the same Jesus died for both and the same Spirit lives in both. When He is the center, the differences become a body. When He is not, they become a battlefield. Unity was never something the church had to manufacture. It was something they already had in Him and kept forgetting to live.

Application

Take an honest inventory today of what actually sits at the center of your faith. Sometimes it is a favorite teacher, podcast, author, or tradition, and it has quietly become the lens you judge every other Christian by. Name it. Then deliberately set Christ back in that spot. Here is one practical test. Before you share, repost, or argue for your favorite voice this week, ask whether you are pointing people to Jesus or to your team. And take two minutes today to thank God not for your preferred leader, but for the Lord you share with believers who are nothing like you.

Discussion Question

"What has quietly drifted into the center of your faith in the place that belongs to Christ (a favorite teacher, a tradition, a cause, a way of doing church), and how would your attitude toward other believers change if Jesus, and not that thing, were unmistakably at the center?"

Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, we confess how easily You slip from the center. We build our faith around voices we admire and wake up having quietly made them more important to us than You. Forgive us. You are the One who was crucified for us, the One we were baptized into, the Lord we all share. Draw us back to the middle where You belong. Teach us to hold on to You so tightly that the smaller loyalties lose their grip, and to recognize as family everyone else who belongs to You. Make our differences into a body again, joined to You as our Head. In Jesus' name, Amen.
