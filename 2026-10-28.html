Written Messages

Revelation and InspirationLesson 5Day 5 of 7

In brief

The battle at Rephidim is over when God tells Moses to write it down. Centuries later John explains why he wrote his Gospel, and Paul says Israel's worst days were recorded on purpose. God had His word written so it would outlast its first hearers. It helps us remember, it warns us, and it leads us to believe in Jesus and live. And He kept the record honest, even about the writers.

Yesterday

Yesterday a nation heard God speak at Sinai, and officers came back saying no one ever spoke like Jesus. Spoken words carried God's full authority. But voices fade, and the people who heard them die. Today God says, Write it down.

Historical Context

The King Burned the Scroll One Strip at a Time

Jeremiah was barred from the temple, so he dictated his messages to a scribe named Baruch. In the fifth year of King Jehoiakim, Baruch read the scroll aloud in the temple for the crowds (Jeremiah 36:4-10). Officials heard it and took the scroll to the king. It was winter, and Jehoiakim sat in his winter house with a fire burning in a brazier in front of him.

As an official read three or four columns, the king cut them off with a scribe's knife and threw them into the fire, until the whole scroll was gone (Jeremiah 36:22-23). Then God told Jeremiah to take another scroll and write it all again. Baruch did, and many similar words were added (Jeremiah 36:32). Writing gave God's word a body that could travel to a king who would not come to hear it. A king could burn the scroll. He could not burn the word.

Into the Word

The battle at Rephidim lasted all day. Joshua fought in the valley, and Moses stood on a hill with the staff of God in his hand. When his arms grew tired, Aaron and Hur held them up until sunset, and Israel won (Exodus 17:8-13). Then God told Moses to do something no one in the Bible had yet been told to do. Write it down.

Moses kept writing. He recorded the stages of Israel's journey (Numbers 33:2) and wrote the law in a book (Deuteronomy 31:24). One psalm carries his name in its heading, a prayer of Moses, the man of God (Psalm 90). Centuries later, John explained why he wrote his Gospel.

An honest record also contains words it does not approve. Scripture reports the serpent's twisting questions (Genesis 3:1-5) and the devil quoting Scripture against Jesus (Matthew 4:1-11). Reporting words is not endorsing them. Paul also borrows lines from Greek poets when they happen to say something true (Acts 17:28, 1 Corinthians 15:33, and Titus 1:12). The poets were not inspired. Paul's use of them was. The writers even recorded their own weakness. Daniel says a vision left him exhausted, sick for days, and unable to understand it (Daniel 8:27). And John, who was told to write what he saw (Revelation 1:19), wrote down this.

Street Level

He Wrote Down What the Rain Cost Him

In her first week in a house she bought on her own, a woman finds a folded note in a kitchen drawer, under a pile of takeout menus. It is from the man who sold her the house, whom she knows only as a name on the deed. To whoever lives here next. When it rains hard for two or three days, the basement takes on water. The man I bought from told me so, and I didn't believe him. I lost my father's photographs that way. Keep anything you love off that floor.

She almost throws it out with the menus. Then in April it rains for three days straight. On the second night she remembers the note and carries her boxes up the stairs, the photo albums, her daughter's baby clothes, a box of old letters. By morning there are two inches of water on the basement floor and nothing on it she cannot replace.

Two things made that note work. It was written down, so it stayed behind when he moved out and spoke to a stranger. And he told the truth about himself. A cheerful note about friendly neighbors would have been easier to write and useless in April. His own loss is what made the warning worth heeding. Paul says Israel's failures were written down as warnings for us, and the Bible leaves even its writers' mistakes on the page. Telling what went wrong is not approving it. One man's ruined photographs kept a stranger's dry.

Today’s World

His Numbers Led Them to the Ship

In 1915, Ernest Shackleton's ship Endurance was trapped and crushed by sea ice in the Weddell Sea, off Antarctica. Her twenty-eight men had to abandon ship, and Endurance went to the bottom. Her captain, Frank Worsley, recorded the position where she sank. Shackleton later brought his men home without loss of life.

No one saw her again for more than a century. Then in March 2022, an expedition searching the sea floor found Endurance nearly two miles down. She sat upright and intact, with her name still arced across the stern. She lay about four miles south of the position Worsley had recorded. The expedition's director of exploration, Mensun Bound, paid tribute to Worsley's navigation and said his detailed records had been invaluable in the search.

Worsley was not writing for a search team a century away. He was keeping careful numbers in brutal conditions, and the wreck was not exactly where he put it. But because he wrote them down, what he knew in 1915 was still there to read in 2022. God had Moses write on a scroll for a reason like that, so Israel would still have the story after the people who stood at Rephidim were gone.

Christ at the center

The angel refused worship. Jesus did not refuse Thomas. A week after the resurrection, Thomas saw the wounds and called Him, My Lord and my God, and Jesus did not correct him. Instead He blessed everyone who would believe without seeing (John 20:28-29). The very next lines tell us why John wrote at all. These are written so that you may believe. Moses wrote so Israel would remember God's rescue. Paul wrote so we would not fall where Israel fell. John wrote so that people who never saw the wounds could say what Thomas said. The written word is not the goal. Jesus is. Every page was written so that you could say it too.

Application

Take two minutes. Write one sentence about something God has done for you, on paper or in your phone, as a reminder like the one Moses wrote. Put today's date on it. Someday you may need to read it again.

Discussion Question

The Bible keeps its heroes' failures, and even its writers' mistakes, on the page. Why might that make it more trustworthy rather than less? Which honest scene in Scripture has helped you most?

Closing Prayer

Father, thank You for writing things down. Thank You that Your help to Israel was not forgotten, that their failures were kept as warnings for us, and that John chose his pages so we could believe. Thank You for a book that tells the truth about its writers and about us. When we read it, keep us from worshiping anything but You. Make us people who remember what You have done. And bring us to say to Your Son what Thomas said. In Jesus' name, Amen.
