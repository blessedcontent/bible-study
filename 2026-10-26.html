The Process of Inspiration

Revelation and InspirationLesson 5Day 3 of 7

In brief

A doctor named Luke gathers eyewitness reports and checks every detail before he writes. A fisherman remembers what his own hands touched. A prophet sees the Lord on a throne. God's word reached the page in more than one way. The Holy Spirit carried real people as they saw, remembered, and researched, so that what they wrote is God's word and can equip us for every good work.

Yesterday

Yesterday the night sky told us that God is great, and His Son came to make Him known. But how did God's words reach the page? Today several writers each give part of the answer.

Historical Context

Luke Opened His Gospel Like a Historian

Luke was a doctor who traveled with Paul (Colossians 4:14). He opened his Gospel the way careful writers of his day opened a serious history. In Greek, Luke's first four verses are one long, polished sentence. It names earlier accounts, says where the facts came from, explains his method, and addresses one reader by name with a title of honor. The Jewish historian Josephus began one of his books the same way. He called his patron most excellent Epaphroditus.

Luke writes to most excellent Theophilus, and he does not say whether Theophilus was a patron like Epaphroditus. Luke uses that same title in Acts for the Roman governors Felix and Festus (Acts 23:26 and Acts 26:25). Then, at verse 5, his style suddenly changes, and the story of Zechariah and Elizabeth sounds like the old Scriptures of Israel. Luke wanted his first reader to know that this book was not a rumor. It was researched.

Into the Word

Count the hands that wrote the Bible. A shepherd who became king wrote songs. A doctor wrote careful history. Fishermen wrote down what they had seen. A man who once persecuted the church wrote letters. Some saw visions, and some did research. So how can a book with so many human fingerprints be God's word? Paul gives Timothy the claim and the reason it matters.

How did God's breath reach the page? Not in one way only. God told Moses He would make Himself known to prophets in visions and dreams (Numbers 12:6). Isaiah saw the Lord on a throne (Isaiah 6:1) and heard Him ask whom He should send (Isaiah 6:8). Ezekiel was told to act a message out (Ezekiel 4:1-8). Luke did something different again.

Street Level

She Built the Dance Out of His Moves

A choreographer is making a solo for a young dancer who learned to move at family parties and in a parking garage, not in a studio. The idea is hers from the start. She does not hand him a list of steps, and she does not try to turn him into a ballet dancer. For six weeks she watches how he already moves. She asks him to try things, keeps some, sets others aside, and builds the whole piece around what she sees. She is in the room for every rehearsal, counting with him and shaping each phrase. She never steps into the dance herself.

On opening night nobody can mistake who is dancing. The low spins, the sudden freezes, and the way he lands without a sound are all his. Afterward a friend asks him whether the dance is his or hers. He laughs, because the answer is both. Every move came out of his body, and the program is right to print her name. He did not disappear into her dance. He has never looked more like himself.

That is close to how the Scriptures were given. Isaiah saw, John remembered, and Luke investigated, and each of them wrote like himself. The Spirit did not make them write like someone else, and He was not one more writer crowding onto the page. He carried each of them through the whole work. Luke is no less Luke for being carried. His Gospel is no less God's word for being researched.

Today’s World

Only Games With a Box Score Count

Josh Gibson's plaque in the Baseball Hall of Fame says he hit almost 800 home runs. Gibson played in the Negro Leagues, because segregation kept Black players out of the major leagues for decades. Larry Lester, a historian of those leagues, says stories, folklore, and embellished truths have long been part of how they are told.

Researchers spent decades on what MLB.com calls box-score archeology, working through African American newspapers printed at the time. In May 2024, after a committee of historians and statisticians reviewed that work, Major League Baseball added the records of more than 2,300 players from seven leagues, 1920 to 1948, to its official books. It counts only league games for which a box score exists. Many of Gibson's home runs came in independent and barnstorming games, so they are not in it. MLB says experts put the surviving records at nearly 75 percent complete, and that this first version of the record will not be the last.

The official numbers are smaller than the legend, and each one can be traced to a box score made at the time. A record book is not Scripture. Still, Luke told Theophilus that his Gospel was put together with that kind of care, from the word of people who were there.

Christ at the center

Peter adds something remarkable about the prophets. They searched and studied carefully, trying to learn what time and circumstances the Spirit of Christ in them was pointing to when He predicted the sufferings of Christ and the glories to follow (1 Peter 1:10-12). The Spirit who carried the writers is called the Spirit of Christ, and He was carrying them toward a cross. They even knew they were serving people who would come later. That means us. The Jesus John touched is the One the prophets strained to see. That is why the Bible holds together across so many hands and centuries. Every writer was carried toward the same Person.

Application

Take two minutes with 2 Timothy 3:16. Choose one of the four things God's word does there (instruction, conviction, correction, or training). Ask God where He wants His word to do that in you today, and write down one sentence about it.

Discussion Question

The Bible came through visions, eyewitness memories, and careful research, yet Christians call all of it God's word. Does knowing that real people with real methods wrote it make it harder or easier for you to trust? Why?

Closing Prayer

Father, thank You for the prophets who saw, the witnesses who touched, and the writer who checked every detail. Thank You that Your Spirit carried them and did not set them aside, and that every one of them was carried toward Your Son. When we open the Scriptures, make us wise for salvation through faith in Him. Show us where we have left the way, set us back on it, and train us to keep walking. Make us people Your word has fully equipped. In Jesus' name, Amen.
