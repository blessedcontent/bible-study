Oral Messages

Revelation and InspirationLesson 5Day 4 of 7

In brief

A whole nation stands at the foot of a smoking mountain and hears God speak, and the people beg Moses to speak for God instead. Centuries later, three fishermen hear that voice again on another mountain. In between, prophets keep saying, The word of the LORD came to me. God's word was spoken long before it was written, and spoken, it was fully His. Jesus left us no writing, yet no one ever spoke like Him.

Yesterday

Yesterday we watched God's word reach the page through visions, eyewitnesses, and a doctor's careful research. But many of God's messengers wrote nothing that we have. Their words were heard before they were ever read. Today we listen.

Historical Context

Water Was Poured at the Altar Every Morning of the Feast

John 7 takes place during the Feast of Tabernacles, also called Booths. Each autumn Israel lived in shelters for a week to remember the years in the wilderness (Leviticus 23:39-43). Jerusalem was packed with pilgrims. A book of Jewish law written down around AD 200 describes one of the feast's rituals. A golden pitcher was filled at the Pool of Siloam and carried up to the temple, and its water was poured out at the altar.

On the last and greatest day of the feast, Jesus stood up and called out. Anyone who is thirsty should come to Him and drink (John 7:37-38). Picture hearing that at a feast where water had been poured out at the altar every day. And the temple officers had already been sent with orders to arrest Him (John 7:32).

Into the Word

Before there was a Bible, there was a voice. God spoke to the first people in a garden (Genesis 2:16-17), and after the first sin He spoke again, to the man, the woman, and the serpent (Genesis 3:9-19). At Sinai He spoke the Ten Commandments aloud to a whole nation (Exodus 20:1-20). The people heard thunder and a trumpet and saw the mountain smoking, and they begged Moses to speak to them instead, afraid they would die if God kept speaking. God said they had spoken well (Deuteronomy 5:28). From then on, His word usually reached His people through human voices. Peter was one of the few who heard God's own voice again.

The prophets were careful to name that Source. The word of the LORD came to me, Jeremiah says (Jeremiah 1:4), and Ezekiel says it again and again (Ezekiel 3:16 and Ezekiel 6:1). God told Moses plainly, I am the LORD (Exodus 6:2). Isaiah says the LORD spoke to him with a strong hand (Isaiah 8:11), and once told him to write a name on a large scroll (Isaiah 8:1). God touched Jeremiah's mouth and put His words there (Jeremiah 1:9). Then He asked Jeremiah what he saw.

Some of God's messengers have no book under their name at all. Noah was a preacher of righteousness (2 Peter 2:5). Nathan walked into a king's palace with a story and a verdict (2 Samuel 12:1-7). Elijah's words thunder through the books of Kings, and John the Baptist preached by the Jordan. What we have from them is mostly what they said, and it was no less God's word for being spoken. Then there is Jesus.

Street Level

The Marriage Did Not Wait for the Mail

Picture a backyard wedding, with folding chairs and lights strung through the trees. The couple says their vows out loud. Then the officiant, a family friend authorized to perform marriages, says the line everyone has been waiting for. By the authority vested in me, I now pronounce you married. The yard erupts. Later, at a card table by the gate, they sign the marriage license, and on Monday it goes in the mail to the county.

Ask that couple years later when they got married, and they will not name the day the county recorded the license. They will name the evening the words were said. The license matters. It is how the bank, the hospital, and everyone after them will know. But it records a marriage that began with words spoken out loud, in front of someone with the authority to declare it.

The prophets' words worked like that. When Jeremiah said what God had put in his mouth, it was God's word the moment he said it, because God had sent him. The scroll that came later kept those words for people who were not there, and that mattered. But it recorded what God had already said through the man He sent. That is also why a voice God never sent binds no one, however sure it sounds. An actor can say the officiant's line perfectly and marry no one.

Today’s World

The Lullaby Tells Him Where to Run

On the Indonesian island of Simeulue, a storyteller named Ahmadi sings a lullaby to his small grandson. It is not about fairies or angels. It is about what to do when a tsunami comes, and his island's answer is simple. If the sea pulls back from the shore, run for higher ground. Simeulue lived through a tsunami in 1907, and its people have passed that warning from one generation to the next ever since, in the stories and songs they call smong.

In December 2004 the story was put to the test. A huge earthquake struck, and the tsunami that followed killed more than 167,000 people in Indonesia alone. On Simeulue, when the sea drew back, people knew to head uphill. A 2024 United Nations report puts the island's death toll at six. A local leader credits the smong, and it is now taught in every primary and junior high school on the island.

A lullaby is not Scripture, and a story could not stop the wave. Many things shaped who lived that day. But words told and retold for almost a century, in voices and songs, did their work on the day they were needed. The prophets' spoken words were like that in one way. They carried their whole weight before anyone wrote them down.

Christ at the center

The Father said, Listen to Him, and Jesus promised that His words would outlast heaven and earth (Matthew 24:35). Some of His most important words were spoken from a cross, in very few breaths. He asked His Father to forgive the men who were crucifying Him (Luke 23:34). He told a dying criminal that he would be with Him in paradise (Luke 23:43). He cried out that it was finished (John 19:30). He did not write His forgiveness on a scroll. He said it out loud, over the people who needed it most, while they were still doing their worst.

Application

Take two minutes. Read one saying of Jesus aloud, such as Matthew 11:28, slowly and twice. Hear it as spoken to you. Then answer Him with one honest sentence of your own.

Discussion Question

The same words of Jesus led some people to call Him the Prophet and sent others into an argument. What makes the difference in how people hear God's word? What helps you actually listen when He speaks?

Closing Prayer

Lord, You have never been silent. You spoke in a garden and on a mountain, through prophets in streets and palaces, and through Your Son by the water and from a cross. Forgive us for the times we heard You clearly and walked away. Thank You that You watch over Your word until it is done. Give us ears that listen and hearts that answer. And when we hear You, give us the will to obey. In Jesus' name, Amen.
