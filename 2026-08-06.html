The Gift of Prophecy

Spiritual GiftsLesson 6Day 6 of 7

In brief

A traveling stranger stands at the church door claiming to speak for God, and the first believers kept a rule book ready, because this gift was too precious to abandon and too easy to fake. Paul ranks prophecy above the gifts Corinth loved loudest, then defines it by what it does to people. Strengthening. Encouragement. Consolation. Scripture supplies the tests for anyone claiming it, words that come true, agreement with what God already said, a life that looks like Christ, fruit that feeds. God still speaks to build His people, and every voice claiming His name gets measured against His Book.

Yesterday

Yesterday closed with a service a stranger could finally walk into, tongues taken in turn, someone interpreting, everything raising the house. But turning one gift's volume down was never Paul's whole project. All along he has been clearing the floor for a different gift, the one the Corinthians kept shoving to the back while chasing the showpiece. Prophecy. Every time Paul compared the two, the comparison tilted the same direction, and today we find out why. The gift Corinth neglected is the one that speaks to people for their strengthening. The loudest room in Corinth was starving for the quieter gift.

Historical Context

Every Road Had a Prophet on It

The first-century Mediterranean was crawling with people who spoke for the gods, at a price. Oracle-mongers worked the festival crowds with books of ready-made predictions. Dream interpreters set up tables in the market. And once the gospel began to spread, a new figure appeared on the roads between churches, the traveling Christian prophet, arriving at the door with a greeting in the Lord's name and, sometimes, an appetite. Most were sincere. Enough were not. The young churches faced a problem Israel had known for a thousand years, wrapped in fresh clothes. What do you do when a stranger says, God sent me?

One of the earliest Christian handbooks outside the New Testament, written close enough to the apostles to remember them, answers with rules so practical they still make you smile. A visiting prophet who stays three days without working is false. A prophet who orders a meal in a trance and then eats it himself is false. A prophet whose life does not match the Lord's ways is false, however impressive the words. The early church was not being cynical. It was being biblical, reaching back to the tests Moses and the prophets had already written. Prophecy was too precious to throw away and too dangerous to leave unweighed. So the church did what love always does with precious things. It refused to abandon the gift, and it refused to stop testing it.

Into the Word

Say the word prophecy and most people picture a crystal ball with a religious paint job, someone predicting next year's headlines. Scripture's picture is at once bigger and closer to home. Today's passages, Paul's definition of the gift (1 Corinthians 14:3) and the risen Christ's staffing plan for His church (Ephesians 4:11-13), show a gift that has less to do with forecasting the future and everything to do with carrying God's voice to people who need it now.

Notice first where prophecy sits in Paul's mind. In list after list it comes before tongues (1 Corinthians 12:10, 1 Corinthians 12:28), even among the gifts that will one day retire (1 Corinthians 13:8). And when tongues does get named first, the order only sharpens the contrast. The uninterpreted tongue builds its own speaker while prophecy builds the church (1 Corinthians 14:4). Paul would rather the whole congregation prophesied (1 Corinthians 14:5). A visit full of impressive sound profits nothing next to a word of revelation or teaching (1 Corinthians 14:6), and even as signs the two gifts serve different audiences (1 Corinthians 14:22). The gift Corinth benched was the one Paul kept moving to the front.

Street Level

The Question Only the Real One Can Answer

The phone rings at two in the morning and it is your son's voice. Not a voice like his. His. The crack in it, the way he says Mom, the panic. There has been an accident, he needs money wired now, please do not tell Dad. Except it is not him. With a few seconds of audio lifted from a video online, cheap software can now wear a loved one's voice like a mask, convincingly enough to fool the people who raised that voice from a whisper. So families have started doing something almost old-fashioned. They agree on a code word at the kitchen table, ahead of time, a question no stranger could ever mine from the internet. When the desperate call comes, you ask it. Panic can be faked. Tears can be faked. The voice itself can be faked. The answer agreed on in advance cannot.

Voices claiming to come from God still arrive all the time, from pulpits, podcasts, and confident friends, and Scripture treats them the way those families treat the phone. You do not swallow every voice, because fakes exist and they are skilled. You do not stop answering either, because some calls really are from home, and God still speaks to strengthen, encourage, and console His people. You test, against the standard written down long before the phone rang. The words come true. The message agrees with what He already said. The life behind the voice confesses Christ. The fruit feeds somebody. And there is one tell that settles most calls on the spot. A voice that really comes from your Father's house is never offended that you checked.

Today’s World

The Year the Fakes Got Pulled

In 2023 the scientific world set a grim record. More than 10,000 published research papers were retracted in a single year, the most ever counted. More than 8,000 of them came from the journals of a single publisher, Hindawi. Behind the number sat an industry of counterfeit knowledge, paper mills that sell bogus studies and even authorships to order, and peer review that had been compromised or manipulated to wave the fakes through. Many wore the outward costume of real science, the citations, the confident tone, and still slipped past the editors who should have caught them.

What gives them away is patient testing against a fixed standard. The counterfeits leave tells, incoherent text, references that have nothing to do with the argument, and tortured phrases twisted just enough to slip past plagiarism software, along with signs that a paper was quietly written by AI. Catch the tells and the costume falls off. That is precisely the posture Scripture asks of the church toward every voice that claims to speak for God. Do not swallow it because it sounds authoritative, and do not dismiss it because fakes exist. Test it, against the words God has already given, against the life behind it, against whether it actually strengthens and feeds anyone. A true word survives the checking. A counterfeit only survives until someone looks closely.

Christ at the center

The angel's definition settles what prophecy is for. The testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy, which means every true prophetic word ever spoken has been carrying Him. On the road to Emmaus, the risen Christ walked two discouraged disciples through Moses and all the Prophets and interpreted for them the things concerning Himself (Luke 24:27), a Bible study proving the whole prophetic tradition had one subject. He is also prophecy's fulfillment in person, the Prophet like Moses that God promised to raise up (Deuteronomy 18:15), and more than a prophet, the Son in whom God has spoken His final word (Hebrews 1:1-2). That is why the tests all point one direction. Does the voice agree with His word, confess His flesh-and-blood coming, produce His fruit? Prophecy that strengthens, encourages, and consoles is simply Jesus still doing with human mouths what He did on that road, coming alongside the buckling and setting their hearts on fire with Himself.

Application

Practice the gift's job description today at its street level. Think of one person who is buckling, the friend waiting on test results, the parent running on fumes, the member nobody has seen in a month. Speak to them on God's behalf, one sentence of strengthening, encouragement, or consolation anchored to something God actually said. Not advice. Not a prediction. Something like, God has not forgotten you, and He finishes what He starts. Send it, say it, or write it before the day ends.

Discussion Question

"When someone claims to speak for God today, what is your honest reflex, swallowing it whole or dismissing it outright (the podcast prophet, the viral prediction, the friend who says God told them something)? And how would applying Scripture's four tests, fulfillment, agreement with the Bible, a Christ-confessing life, and fruit that feeds, change what you do with the next claim that reaches you?"

Closing Prayer

Father, thank You for being a God who still talks. You revealed Your counsel to Your servants the prophets, You spoke Your final word in Your Son, and You have promised Your church every gift it needs until the building is finished. Forgive us for the two ways we mishandle Your voice. Sometimes we swallow every confident claim because it thrills us, and sometimes we dismiss the whole gift because fakes have burned us. Teach us instead to test, patiently, by Your word, Your Son, and the fruit. Guard Your church from voices that flatter and voices that frighten but never feed. And make us carriers of the real thing this week, mouths You can trust with strengthening, encouragement, and consolation for the people buckling beside us. Let every word we pass along point to Jesus, because that is what Your voice has always done. In Jesus' name, Amen.
