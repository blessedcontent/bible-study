Varieties of Gifts

Spiritual GiftsLesson 6Day 2 of 7

In brief

A believer stands up in a Corinthian gathering to say the shortest creed the church owns. Jesus is Lord. On the street those words sound like treason, because Lord is Caesar's title, and yet Paul says no one can truly speak them without the Holy Spirit. That confession is the Spirit's first gift, and behind it comes a whole catalog, different gifts, different ministries, different workings, one God behind all of it, handed to each person exactly as He chooses. Being spiritual starts with whose you are, not how gifted you look, and no gift on the list is optional.

Yesterday

Yesterday ended in a crowded living room, a hymn from one corner, a teaching from another, and Paul answering the noise with a single command. Chase love first, then welcome the gifts. Today he starts the welcome by opening the church's own letter and taking up their question about spiritual things. Corinth wanted to know who ranked as spiritual, and the city's oldest reflex was already at work, sorting members into impressive and ordinary. So Paul begins at the foundation, beneath tongues, beneath healing, beneath every ability on the coming list. What makes a person spiritual at all? His answer fits in three words.

Historical Context

Two Words Could Cost You Everything

Every city in the empire owed Caesar something, but Corinth owed him everything. Julius Caesar had rebuilt the ruined city and stocked it with Rome's freedmen, so Rome's favor was Corinth's entire business model, and gratitude had a religious address. Shrines to the imperial family stood in the city center. Civic festivals ran on the emperor's calendar. The surest ladder for an ambitious Corinthian was sponsoring the games and honors that celebrated Caesar, and the empire's loyalty shorthand, the phrase that greased contracts and courtrooms alike, was simple. Caesar is lord.

Then a tentmaker's congregation began closing its prayers with a different sentence. Jesus is Lord. To modern ears it is a chorus lyric. To Roman ears it was a rival coronation, and the movement already had a rap sheet. Remember how the priests cornered Pilate at Jesus' trial, warning that releasing Him would make the governor no friend of Caesar (John 19:12), until the crowd shouted the empire's creed outright, we have no king but Caesar (John 19:15). Saying the opposite in public could cost a contract, a friendship, a life. That is the room where Paul chooses to talk about gifts. Whatever else the Spirit was handing out in Corinth, the starter gift was nerve.

Into the Word

Paul is answering the church's mail again. Now concerning spiritual gifts, he begins (1 Corinthians 12:1), and you can feel the room lean in, because in Corinth the gifts had become a scoreboard. Scholars still discuss whether his opening phrase means spiritual gifts or spiritual people, and the chapter answers by redefining both. Before naming a single gift, Paul resets what spiritual means at all, and he starts with the shortest, most dangerous sentence a Corinthian could say out loud. Jesus is Lord. From there the passage (1 Corinthians 12:1-6) opens into a variety wider than Corinth had categories for.

One gift deserves a spotlight before the list grows. Faith. Paul will shortly name faith among the Spirit's distributed gifts (1 Corinthians 12:9), which sounds strange until you remember what it costs to keep trusting God with a death sentence hanging over your confession. Faith under fire is something the Spirit supplies, not something grit produces. It even stands first in the trio that outlasts everything else, faith, hope, and love (1 Corinthians 13:13). The most basic gift is the one that holds on.

Street Level

The Piece Nobody Would Have Picked

A thousand-piece puzzle takes over the kitchen table somewhere around the second week it sits there. Sky in one corner, barn in the other, three people hunched over it at ten at night insisting they are going to bed and not going. Then comes the moment. The picture is finished, the family gathers, and there is a hole. One piece, missing. Everybody drops to the floor. Couch cushions come off. The dog's bed gets searched. Nobody stands back admiring the 999 pieces already in place. Every eye in the room is locked on the one that is gone, and it is not even an interesting piece. It is a brown one. Part of a shadow. The piece nobody would have picked out of the box as special.

Paul says the Spirit stocked your congregation the way a puzzle gets cut. On purpose, one of a kind, every shape an address. Nobody asks why a piece is not shaped like its neighbor, because a piece shaped like every other piece would fit nowhere and hold nothing. And nobody cuts a thousand pieces and includes filler. The quiet gift you keep discounting because it looks brown and shadow-shaped is the exact piece the picture cannot finish without. Sit on it long enough and the whole room ends up on the floor, looking for you. The Spirit decided what went in the box. The picture is not done until every piece He cut is on the table.

Today’s World

The Field That Beats Its Best Single Crop

For decades ecologists have run the same quiet experiment on thousands of plots. Plant one species alone, plant a mix of many, then come back year after year and weigh what grew. In 2024 a team gathered 65 of these long-running experiments into one analysis in Nature Communications, 39 grasslands and 26 forests, 166 kinds of herb and 134 kinds of tree, some plots tracked for eighteen years. The result was consistent. The diverse plots outgrew the average of their component monocultures, producing more than those same species yielded on average when each was grown alone, and that advantage kept strengthening the longer the plots ran.

Here is the part that reads like Paul. The mixed field wins not because one superstar plant carries it, but because species with different strengths contribute at different times, one thriving in the drought year, another in the wet one, a plain root doing slow work underground while a showier neighbor rests. As the plots age, which species drives the advantage keeps shifting, and members that looked minor early on come into their own later. The Spirit stocked the church the way creation stocks a thriving field, not with one impressive crop but with variety, each member carrying the season that is theirs, the whole growing richer the longer they stay rooted together.

Christ at the center

Follow the gifts upstream and you find a Giver with scars. The confession that opens everything, Jesus is Lord, is only sayable because the Lord in question went to a cross and walked out of a tomb. And Scripture paints the ascension not as a departure but as a coronation with gifts thrown from the platform. When He ascended on high, He took the captives captive and gave gifts to people, apostles, prophets, evangelists, shepherds, teachers (Ephesians 4:8-11). Sit with what that means. The risen Christ celebrated His victory by equipping us. The Spirit distributes as He wills, but the supply line runs straight from the throne of the Crucified. That is why the first gift is a sentence about Him. Every grace gift that follows, every act of service, every surge of power, exists to make the Lordship of Jesus visible in a world still bowing to lesser lords. The gifts were never about us. They are the risen King, still handing things out.

Application

Today, say the confession somewhere real. Not in your head. Out loud, in a place where it costs at least a little. A text to the friend who knows you don't usually talk that way. A sentence at the dinner table. An honest answer to the coworker who asks about your weekend. Three words, Jesus is Lord, and one truthful follow-up, He can have whatever He wants of mine. The first gift grows the same way it arrived. By being spoken.

Discussion Question

"Where have you quietly ranked the gifts in your church, treating some people as the varsity squad and others as filler (the preacher over the nursery volunteer, the singer over the man who shovels the walk)? And what would change this week if you believed the Spirit chose every one of those gifts on purpose?"

Closing Prayer

Holy Spirit, we owe You a debt we rarely notice. Every time we have said Jesus is Lord and meant it, that was You, giving us both the words and the will behind them. Thank You for the first gift. Forgive us for treating the rest like merchandise, wanting the impressive ones, discounting the quiet ones, grading each other by gifts You handed out as You decided. Teach us to trust Your judgment, both for what You gave us and for what You gave the person beside us. Same Spirit, same Lord, same God. Make that our peace when church feels uneven, and our courage when the confession gets expensive. Let every gift in our congregation, named or buried, find its work this week, so the watching world sees one Lord instead of a talent show. We are Yours, and so is everything You handed us. In Jesus' name, Amen.
