Repentance and ForgivenessLesson 10Day 7 of 7

Week in Review

A week begins at the foot of Sinai with smashed tablets and a calf still smoking, and ends at the door of a wedding banquet where the King is handing out the robe Himself, and every day in between was the same Voice in a different room.

  • ☩Sabbath: Repentance and Forgiveness. The hardest day in Israel's history was the day God walked past Moses in the cleft of a rock and named Himself "merciful and gracious," and 1 John's Memory Text held the two sides of one healing in one sentence.
  • ☩Sunday: The Rush of Life. A woman on Sabbath morning saw herself for one second mid-iron and realized she was Martha, and Isaiah handed her the robe trade that had been waiting in the closet the whole time.
  • ☩Monday: Holy Spirit Promptings. Jesus's first listed job for the Parakletos was to expose, not to accuse, and the Spirit grieved was the same Spirit who had already sealed us as His.
  • ☩Tuesday: Real Repentance. The first sermon recorded from both John and Jesus opened with the same Greek verb, and Peter named what is on the other side of the turn: cool breath after the climb.
  • ☩Wednesday: Sufficient Grace. The seven Hebrew words God spoke to Moses became the Bible's most-quoted line, and all three of last quarter's sin words appeared together as the things the verb nose lifts.
  • ☩Thursday: The Most Expensive Robe. The first death in the Bible was an animal in Eden whose skin clothed two sinners, and the wedding parable on Jesus's last week ended on the one man who refused what the King provided at the door.

Historical Context

The Sanctuary at the Center of Every Room

Every scene the week walked through was already happening inside the architecture of one building. The Hebrew word for the tabernacle Moses built at Sinai is mishkan, "dwelling place," the portable sanctuary that traveled with Israel through the wilderness and was later replaced by Solomon's stone temple in Jerusalem. The sanctuary was a teaching diagram in three dimensions. An outer court with an altar where the animal died for the worshiper. An inner Holy Place where the priest stood between the people and the throne. A Most Holy Place behind a curtain where God's glory rested over the ark, the same mercy seat from which He spoke Exodus 34's seven-word self-introduction in another generation. Once a year on the Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur), the high priest entered the Most Holy with blood, and the sins of the people for the year were "lifted" (the verb is nose again, the same verb Exodus 34:7 used) onto a scapegoat that carried them away into the wilderness.

Hebrews tells us that whole sanctuary was a copy of a heavenly one (Hebrews 8:5), and that Christ is now the High Priest there, ministering on our behalf with the blood of a sacrifice He offered once for all. Every garment the week named (Adam's tunic of skin in Eden, Joshua's festal robe in Zechariah, the wedding garment in Matthew 22, the fine linen in Revelation 19) is the same robe in different rooms of the same sanctuary. The repentance Tuesday named is the worshiper crossing the outer court. The forgiveness Wednesday named is the High Priest entering the Most Holy. The robe Thursday named is the garment the worshiper is given at the door because the High Priest's blood has already been poured at the altar. Hosea's agricultural metaphor sits in this same architecture. The hard soil of our hearts is the outer court; the rain of righteousness is what falls from the inner sanctuary onto the prepared ground.

What This Week Clarifies

Put the six days in one room and they raise a few honest tensions. A week about repentance opened on a woman who was not doing anything wrong. The God who tears and prunes turned out to move people by kindness. And the freest forgiveness in the Bible arrived wearing an if. Each of these has an answer the week already built. Here are the three, one at a time.

Nothing Was Wrong in Martha's Kitchen

Sunday made a strange opening move for a week about repentance. Martha was not sinning. Her kitchen was full of love and her hands were busy serving Jesus's own dinner. Yet this is the room where the week began its call to turn around, a good woman doing good work, and it chose that room on purpose.

The Hand That Tears Is the Hand That Bandages

The week did not soften what God does with sin. The Spirit convicts like a lawyer laying out evidence. The Gardener carries a pruning knife. Hosea's people are told plainly that God has torn. Set beside all of that, Paul's claim sounds almost out of place. What leads a person to repentance, he says, is not the pressure of any of it. It is kindness.

The If Was Never a Price Tag

The Memory Text hangs the whole week on a conditional. If we confess, He forgives. Read quickly, that if sounds like a fee at the window, as though the forgiveness the cross already paid for still costs one payment of sorrow at the door. The week kept insisting the gift is free, and then kept repeating a sentence with a condition at the front of it.

Still Speaking

The Robe That Walks Out of the Sanctuary on a Friday

The most transformative experience available to a human being is to be completely forgiven and completely covered by Someone else's righteousness. Every other repair we attempt on ourselves runs on the leaf-thin Genesis-3 fabric Sunday named. The pastor's polished sermon. The therapist's hard-won self-acceptance. The activist's purifying cause. The hobbyist's mastery. None of them are bad, but none of them is the wedding garment. The white linen is the righteousness of Christ, His own unblemished character, that through faith is wrapped around every soul who receives Him. We do not graduate from this trade. We make it again every morning, the way Adam and Eve had to walk out of Eden every day with the tunic on instead of the leaves.

Look at the week with this in mind and the geography clarifies. Sabbath's "if we confess" is not the climbing instruction to a far-off summit; it is the open hand at the sanctuary door. Sunday's Mary at the feet is the worshiper sitting where the High Priest is teaching. Monday's Parakletos is the one bringing us into the building from the parking lot. Tuesday's metanoeite is the turn that walks the threshold. Wednesday's seven Hebrew words are the inscription over the lintel naming Whose house this is. Thursday's wedding robe is the garment the attendant hands us at the door, woven on a different hill. Friday is the day we walk out wearing it. The week did not end with us closing a book on a lesson. It ended with the building still open, and a Voice that still says all seven words, and a robe that still has our name on the hook.

Christ at the Center of the Week

The face in every room of the week was the same Face. The God who walked past Moses on Sabbath saying His own name was Christ. The "good part" Mary chose on Sunday was Christ. The Parakletos Monday introduced was sent by Christ. The inaugural verb of the gospel Tuesday opened on came out of Christ's own mouth. The seven Hebrew words Wednesday spelled were already the job description Christ would walk out on a hill outside Jerusalem to fulfill. The robe Thursday handed us at the door was made of His perfect obedience and dipped in His blood. Friday's sanctuary architecture renders Him as the altar's Lamb, the Holy Place's Priest, and the Most Holy's Presence, all at once. Repentance and forgiveness are not two doctrines we juggle. They are two sides of one Person. The repentance is what we bring to Him. The forgiveness is what He brings to us. And the robe is the garment He wore in the place we should have stood, now hanging in the closet at the door of every morning we will wake up between now and the wedding supper.

Weekend Practice

Between now and Sabbath dinner, find one trusted person (spouse, friend, small-group brother or sister) and tell them one of two things. Either the sentence Tuesday's Parakletos named in your chest this week, with the concrete turn you took on the back of it. Or the question of the week that you still have not gotten an answer to (the one of God's seven Hebrew descriptors you are having the hardest time believing). Do not recap the week. Speak from your own kitchen. Then ask them to do the same in return. The robe at the door is for both of you. James says we confess our sins to one another and pray for one another, "so that you may be healed" (James 5:16); this is one of the rare practices the Bible names by its outcome. Walk into next week with at least one other person who knows what God did in yours.

Closing Prayer for the Week

Father, the week opened at the foot of a mountain that should have ended us, and closes at the door of a banquet hall we did not build. Thank You. We did not deserve the seven Hebrew words You spoke to Moses, and we did not deserve the wedding robe You handed us at the door. You gave both. We confess that we have been Aaron's people more days than we like to admit, melting our gold into something we could see, deciding by lunchtime that You had gone silent. Forgive us. Make us a household where the iron gets set down on a Sunday morning, where the Parakletos is welcomed in the kitchen, where the inaugural verb is the verb our feet actually walk, where the seven words are louder than the sentences we have been writing about ourselves, and where the robe at the door is the actual thing on our actual shoulders. We hold fast to You. We do not let go. Send us into next week with the sanctuary's whole gospel pressed against our chest, and a wedding garment we did not stitch around our shoulders, and a name on a card in our pocket that You said over Yourself in the cleft of a rock three thousand years ago and still mean today. We need You. In Jesus' name, Amen.
